# Build Taxonomy

## Purpose
Organize research findings into a structured classification system that categorizes concepts, entities, or phenomena into a hierarchical taxonomy, enabling clear communication and systematic analysis.

## When to Use
- When research reveals a complex landscape that needs organizing
- For domain mapping where categories and relationships need definition
- When stakeholders need a shared vocabulary and classification framework
- To structure findings for comparison, evaluation, or decision-making
- When the research domain lacks an established classification system

## Agents Involved
- **Lead**: Domain Specialist
- **Executing**: Synthesis Specialist
- **Supporting**: Data Researcher
- **Reviewed by**: Contrarian Analyst, Evidence Verifier

## Inputs
- Research findings with identified entities, concepts, and categories
- Existing taxonomies or classification systems in the domain (if any)
- Stakeholder needs for categorization granularity
- Evidence base supporting category distinctions

## Steps
1. Extract all entities, concepts, and phenomena from research findings
2. Identify natural groupings and clusters in the extracted items
3. Define top-level categories based on the most significant distinctions
4. Develop sub-categories within each top-level category
5. Define clear criteria for category membership (inclusion and exclusion rules)
6. Test category boundaries with edge cases and ambiguous items
7. Ensure categories are mutually exclusive where required, or document overlaps
8. Validate that categories are collectively exhaustive (nothing important is uncategorized)
9. Define relationships between categories (hierarchy, adjacency, dependency)
10. Create category descriptions with examples and counter-examples
11. Validate the taxonomy against the evidence base for accuracy
12. Document the taxonomy with rationale for major classification decisions

## Quality Gates
- Categories are clearly defined with unambiguous membership criteria
- The taxonomy covers all significant items from the research findings
- Edge cases are addressed with documented classification rationale
- The hierarchy is logically consistent (no circular or contradictory relationships)
- Category names are intuitive and communicable to the target audience
- The taxonomy is validated against the evidence base

## Outputs
- Complete taxonomy document with hierarchical structure
- Category definitions with membership criteria
- Edge case classification guide
- Relationship map between categories
- Taxonomy rationale documentation
- Visual taxonomy diagram

## Estimated Effort
- **Simple taxonomy (2-3 levels, 10-15 categories)**: 1-3 hours
- **Moderate taxonomy (3-4 levels, 15-30 categories)**: 3-5 hours
- **Complex taxonomy (4+ levels, 30+ categories)**: 5-10 hours
