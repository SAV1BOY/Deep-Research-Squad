# Present Research Findings

## Purpose
Prepare and structure a presentation of research findings optimized for the target audience, ensuring key insights are communicated clearly with appropriate evidence support and confidence framing.

## When to Use
- When research findings need to be presented to stakeholders verbally or in presentation format
- For briefing sessions with decision-makers
- When research needs to be communicated to non-technical audiences
- For knowledge sharing sessions within or across teams
- When findings need to be defended in a review or challenge session

## Agents Involved
- **Lead**: DeepResearch Chief
- **Executing**: Synthesis Specialist
- **Supporting**: Domain Specialist, Evidence Verifier
- **Reviewed by**: Contrarian Analyst

## Inputs
- Synthesis report and executive summary
- Decision brief (if applicable)
- Confidence map
- Audience profile (role, expertise level, decision context)
- Presentation format and time constraints
- Known audience questions or concerns

## Steps
1. Profile the audience (expertise level, interests, decision context, known biases)
2. Define the key message and three to five supporting points
3. Structure the presentation narrative (situation, findings, implications, recommendations)
4. Select the most compelling evidence and data points for each finding
5. Design visual aids that make complex findings accessible
6. Prepare confidence framing for each key finding (what we know vs. what we think)
7. Anticipate likely questions and prepare responses with evidence support
8. Include a "what we do not know" section to demonstrate intellectual honesty
9. Prepare appendix slides or materials for deep-dive questions
10. Rehearse the narrative flow for clarity and time compliance
11. Build a one-page takeaway summary for distribution after presentation
12. Prepare follow-up materials for post-presentation questions

## Quality Gates
- The presentation is calibrated to the audience's expertise level
- Key findings are supported by evidence, not just assertions
- Confidence levels are communicated transparently
- The narrative flows logically from context to findings to implications
- Time constraints are respected without sacrificing key content
- Anticipated questions have prepared responses

## Outputs
- Structured presentation document or slide deck
- Speaker notes with evidence references
- Anticipated Q&A preparation document
- One-page takeaway summary for distribution
- Appendix materials for deep-dive questions
- Follow-up materials package

## Estimated Effort
- **Short briefing (10-15 minutes)**: 1-2 hours
- **Standard presentation (30-45 minutes)**: 2-4 hours
- **Comprehensive presentation with defense prep**: 4-6 hours
