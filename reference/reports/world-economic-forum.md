# World Economic Forum (WEF) Reports

## Overview
The WEF is an international organization for public-private cooperation. Publishes influential reports on global risks, technology, competitiveness, and future trends. Based in Geneva, Switzerland.

## Key Annual Reports

### Global Risks Report (Annual, January)
- Published ahead of Davos annual meeting
- Surveys ~1,000 global leaders on risk perceptions
- Categories: Economic, Environmental, Geopolitical, Societal, Technological
- Tracks both likelihood and impact over 2-year and 10-year horizons
- **Use**: Understanding consensus risk landscape and emerging threats

### Global Competitiveness Report (Paused post-2019, restructured)
- Ranked 141 economies on competitiveness factors
- 12 pillars: Institutions, Infrastructure, ICT adoption, Macro stability, etc.
- **Use**: Benchmarking countries for market entry or investment analysis

### Future of Jobs Report (Biennial)
- Surveys employers on expected job creation/displacement
- Skills demand forecasts by industry and region
- Reskilling and upskilling requirements
- **Use**: Labor market and skills gap analysis

### Global Gender Gap Report (Annual)
- Country rankings on gender parity across economic, political, education, health dimensions
- **Use**: ESG and social impact research contexts

## Technology and Industry Reports
- **"Top 10 Emerging Technologies"** (Annual) - Horizon scanning for tech trends
- **"Digital Transformation Initiative"** series - Sector digital value estimates
- **"Harnessing AI for the Earth"** (2018) - AI for sustainability applications
- **"Chief Economists Outlook"** (Quarterly) - Economic consensus views

## How to Use WEF Reports
- **Strength**: Broad stakeholder input, global perspective, consistent methodology
- **Limitation**: Consensus-driven can mean bland; Davos bias toward elite perspectives
- **Access**: weforum.org/publications - free downloads
- **Citation**: World Economic Forum, "Report Title," Year

## Evaluation Notes
- Survey methodology means results reflect perceptions, not necessarily reality
- Useful for tracking how global elites frame key issues
- Cross-reference quantitative claims with primary data sources
- Strong on trend identification, weaker on actionable specifics
- Country rankings can be politically influenced by respondent selection
