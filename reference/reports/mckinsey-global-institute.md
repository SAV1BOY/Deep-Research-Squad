# McKinsey Global Institute (MGI) Reports

## Overview
MGI is the business and economics research arm of McKinsey & Company. Founded in 1990, it produces independent research on global economic and business issues. Reports are free and publicly available.

## Key Report Series

### The Future of Work
- **"Jobs Lost, Jobs Gained"** (2017) - 800M jobs displaced by automation by 2030
- **"The Future of Work After COVID-19"** (2021) - Permanent shifts in work models
- **"A New Future of Work"** (2023) - Generative AI impact on labor markets
- Tracks automation potential by occupation and activity type

### Artificial Intelligence
- **"Notes from the AI Frontier"** series - Sector-by-sector AI impact analysis
- **"The State of AI"** annual reports - Adoption trends and investment data
- **"The Economic Potential of Generative AI"** (2023) - $2.6-4.4T annual value estimate
- Detailed breakdowns by industry, function, and use case

### Productivity and Growth
- **"Solving the Productivity Puzzle"** (2018) - Declining productivity growth causes
- **"Bridging Infrastructure Gaps"** (2016) - $3.3T annual infrastructure investment needed
- **"Pixelated Transformation"** (2019) - Digital transformation varies by sector

### Global Trends
- **"Global Flows in a Digital Age"** (2014) - Trade and capital flow patterns
- **"Outperformers"** (2018) - High-growth emerging economies characteristics
- **"Risk, Resilience, and Rebalancing in Global Value Chains"** (2020)

## How to Use MGI Reports in Research
- **Strength**: Large-scale quantitative analysis, global datasets, sector detail
- **Limitation**: Potential consulting-firm bias toward transformation narratives
- **Access**: mckinsey.com/mgi - free PDF downloads
- **Citation style**: McKinsey Global Institute, "Report Title," Month Year

## Evaluation Notes
- Generally well-researched with transparent methodology sections
- Cross-reference with academic and government data where possible
- Be aware of optimistic bias toward technology adoption projections
- Historical forecast accuracy has been mixed (tend toward overestimation of speed)
- Useful for directional trends, less reliable for precise quantitative predictions
