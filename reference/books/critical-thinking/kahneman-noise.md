# Kahneman, Sibony & Sunstein - Noise: A Flaw in Human Judgment

## Core Concept: Bias vs Noise

### Bias
- Systematic deviation in one direction
- Average error across many judgments
- Predictable and often studied

### Noise
- Unwanted variability in judgments
- Different people reach different conclusions from same data
- Same person reaches different conclusions at different times
- Often invisible and unstudied

### The Target Analogy
- **Bias**: Shots clustered away from the bullseye (systematic error)
- **Noise**: Shots scattered widely (variability)
- Both reduce accuracy; noise is often the larger problem

## Types of Noise

### Level Noise
- Some judges are consistently harsher or more lenient
- Example: One analyst always rates risks higher than peers

### Pattern Noise
- Judges respond differently to specific cases
- Example: One analyst is bullish on tech, another on fintech

### Occasion Noise
- Same judge varies based on mood, time, weather, fatigue
- Example: Research quality varies by day of the week

## Noise in Research Context
- Different researchers reach different conclusions from same evidence
- Same researcher may assess differently on different days
- Framing of the question changes the assessment
- Order of evidence presentation affects conclusions
- Aggregation of multiple assessments reduces noise

## Noise Reduction Strategies

### Decision Hygiene
1. **Structure judgments**: Break complex assessments into independent components
2. **Use scales with anchors**: Define what each rating level means concretely
3. **Sequence information**: Gather impressions on each dimension separately
4. **Delay holistic judgment**: Score components before forming overall view
5. **Aggregate independent judgments**: Average multiple assessors

### Noise Audits
- Have multiple people assess the same case independently
- Measure the variability in their assessments
- Identify which dimensions have the most noise
- Target interventions at the noisiest components

### Guidelines and Algorithms
- Simple rules often outperform expert judgment
- Algorithms are noise-free (though they can be biased)
- Even imperfect algorithms reduce noise significantly
- Combine algorithmic scores with structured human review

## Practical Application for Deep Research
1. Use structured assessment frameworks, not free-form evaluation
2. Break complex judgments into sub-judgments
3. Have multiple agents assess independently before discussing
4. Aggregate assessments rather than relying on single judgment
5. Define assessment criteria explicitly before reviewing evidence
6. Be aware that order of evidence presentation matters
7. Conduct periodic noise audits on research outputs
8. Use checklists to standardize evaluation across cases
