# Kahneman - Thinking, Fast and Slow

## Two Systems of Thinking

### System 1: Fast, Automatic, Intuitive
- Operates automatically with little effort
- Generates impressions, feelings, inclinations
- Cannot be turned off
- Prone to systematic biases
- Examples: Reading text, detecting hostility, driving on empty road

### System 2: Slow, Deliberate, Analytical
- Allocates attention to effortful mental activities
- Associated with agency, choice, concentration
- Can override System 1 but is lazy
- Depletes cognitive resources
- Examples: Complex calculations, comparing products, filling out tax forms

## Key Biases Affecting Research

### Anchoring Effect
- First number encountered biases subsequent estimates
- **Research impact**: Initial findings anchor all later analysis
- **Mitigation**: Generate estimates before reviewing existing data

### Availability Heuristic
- Judging probability by ease of recall
- **Research impact**: Overweight recent, vivid, or dramatic evidence
- **Mitigation**: Systematically search for base rates

### Representativeness Heuristic
- Judging probability by similarity to stereotypes
- **Research impact**: Pattern matching without statistical basis
- **Mitigation**: Always check base rates before assessing likelihood

### WYSIATI (What You See Is All There Is)
- Building confident stories from incomplete information
- **Research impact**: Premature conclusions from limited data
- **Mitigation**: Explicitly list what you do NOT know

### Substitution
- Replacing a hard question with an easier one
- **Research impact**: Answering "what do I feel?" instead of "what does the data say?"
- **Mitigation**: Restate the exact question before answering

## Overconfidence
- People are systematically overconfident in their estimates
- Confidence intervals are too narrow
- Experts are not immune (often worse due to illusion of skill)
- **Mitigation**: Use pre-mortems, widen confidence intervals by 30-50%

## Prospect Theory
- Losses loom larger than gains (loss aversion ~2x)
- Reference point determines whether outcome is a gain or loss
- **Research impact**: Reluctance to abandon failing research directions

## Practical Application for Research
1. Slow down: Force System 2 engagement on important judgments
2. Use checklists to counter System 1 shortcuts
3. Seek disconfirming evidence actively
4. Quantify uncertainty with explicit probability ranges
5. Pre-mortem: Imagine the research conclusion is wrong, then explain why
6. Use structured analytic techniques to reduce intuitive bias
7. Separate data gathering from data interpretation
