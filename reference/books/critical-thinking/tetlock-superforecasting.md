# Tetlock & Gardner - Superforecasting: The Art and Science of Prediction

## Fox vs Hedgehog Mindset

### Hedgehog (Poor Forecasters)
- One big idea explains everything
- High confidence, dramatic predictions
- Reluctant to change views
- Sees world through single analytical lens
- "I know one big thing"

### Fox (Good Forecasters)
- Draw from many analytical frameworks
- Moderate confidence, nuanced predictions
- Update readily with new information
- Comfortable with ambiguity and uncertainty
- "I know many small things"

## What Makes Superforecasters

### Cognitive Traits
- **Actively open-minded**: Seek views that challenge their own
- **Granular thinking**: Use precise probabilities (not "likely" but "72%")
- **Probabilistic reasoning**: Think in degrees, not certainties
- **Growth mindset**: Believe forecasting skill can improve
- **Comfort with numbers**: Not afraid of quantification

### Process Habits
- Break problems into components (Fermi estimation)
- Start with base rates, then adjust
- Update frequently in small increments
- Distinguish signal from noise
- Look for analogies from multiple domains

## Calibration

### What It Means
- When you say 70% confident, the thing should happen 70% of the time
- Most people are overconfident (say 90%, right only 75%)
- Superforecasters are well-calibrated across all probability ranges

### How to Improve Calibration
1. Track your predictions with explicit probabilities
2. Review outcomes and measure your calibration
3. Practice with feedback loops
4. Use reference classes (base rates) as starting points
5. Avoid round numbers (forces more precise thinking)

## Updating Beliefs

### Bayesian Updating in Practice
1. Start with a prior probability (base rate or initial estimate)
2. Encounter new evidence
3. Assess: How likely is this evidence if my hypothesis is true vs false?
4. Update proportionally (not dramatically)
5. Repeat with each new piece of evidence

### Common Updating Errors
- Failing to update at all (belief persistence)
- Updating too dramatically (overreacting to single data points)
- Updating in wrong direction (misinterpreting evidence)
- Ignoring base rates when updating

## Team Forecasting
- Diverse teams outperform homogeneous ones
- Devil's advocate improves group accuracy
- Share reasoning, not just conclusions
- Weight more accurate forecasters more heavily over time

## Application to Deep Research
1. Assign explicit probability estimates to key claims
2. Use Fermi decomposition for unknown quantities
3. Always start with the base rate before incorporating specifics
4. Update conclusions incrementally as new evidence arrives
5. Track prediction accuracy to improve calibration over time
6. Adopt the fox mindset: multiple frameworks, moderate confidence
7. When uncertain, widen your confidence interval rather than fake precision
