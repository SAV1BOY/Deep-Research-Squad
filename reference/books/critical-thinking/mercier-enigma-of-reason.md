# Mercier & Sperber - The Enigma of Reason

## Core Thesis: Reason Evolved for Argumentation
Reason did not evolve to help individuals think better. It evolved to help humans persuade others and evaluate others' arguments in social contexts.

## The Argumentative Theory of Reasoning

### Why Reasoning Seems Flawed
- Confirmation bias is not a bug, it is the core feature
- We reason to produce arguments that support our position
- We evaluate others' arguments to avoid being deceived
- This is adaptive in social groups but problematic for solitary analysis

### Two Functions of Reason
1. **Production**: Generate arguments to convince others (biased toward own view)
2. **Evaluation**: Assess others' arguments for quality (more objective)

## Implications for Research

### Solitary Reasoning is Weak
- Individual reasoners tend to find reasons FOR their pre-existing beliefs
- Motivated reasoning is the default, not the exception
- More intelligence means better rationalization, not better reasoning
- **Key insight**: Lone researchers are especially prone to confirmation bias

### Group Reasoning is Stronger
- When people argue with those who disagree, reasoning improves
- Evaluation of others' arguments is relatively unbiased
- Diverse groups outperform individuals at reasoning tasks
- Debate forces consideration of counterarguments

### The Myside Bias
- Everyone reasons in favor of their own side by default
- This is not laziness; it is how reasoning naturally works
- Intelligence does not reduce myside bias (may increase it)
- Only structured processes can counter it effectively

## Reason and Intuition
- Intuitions (gut feelings) are often reasonable; they just lack justification
- Reason's role is to justify intuitions post-hoc, not to generate conclusions
- When intuitions and reasons conflict, people usually follow intuitions
- The best process: use intuition to generate hypotheses, then test them rigorously

## Practical Strategies Based on This Theory

### Leverage the Social Nature of Reason
1. Use adversarial collaboration: pair researchers with opposing views
2. Assign devil's advocate roles explicitly
3. Share reasoning publicly so others can evaluate it
4. Create structured debate before reaching conclusions

### Counter Solitary Reasoning Weaknesses
1. Write out your argument, then systematically attack it
2. Ask "What would someone who disagrees say?"
3. Seek out the strongest counterargument, not the weakest
4. Use red team / blue team analysis

### Design Processes That Exploit Evaluation Strength
1. Have one agent generate conclusions, another evaluate them
2. Require explicit evidence for every claim (makes evaluation easier)
3. Make assumptions visible so they can be challenged
4. Separate the roles of advocate and judge

## Application to Deep Research
- Multi-agent systems naturally leverage reason's social strengths
- Assign separate roles for argument production and evaluation
- Build in mandatory counterargument steps
- Never let a single reasoning process determine the final conclusion
- Use structured debate between agents to improve reasoning quality
- Recognize that the urge to confirm is natural and design around it
