# Pinker - Rationality: What It Is, Why It Seems Scarce, Why It Matters

## Rational Thinking Tools for Researchers

### Logic and Formal Reasoning
- **Modus ponens**: If P then Q; P is true; therefore Q is true
- **Modus tollens**: If P then Q; Q is false; therefore P is false
- **Common error**: Affirming the consequent (If P then Q; Q is true; therefore P)
- **Application**: Always check if your evidence actually supports your conclusion logically

### Probability and Bayesian Reasoning
- Prior probability x likelihood ratio = posterior probability
- Base rate neglect is one of the most common reasoning errors
- Conjunction fallacy: P(A and B) cannot exceed P(A) or P(B)
- **Application**: Never assess probability without first considering the base rate

### Signal Detection Theory
- Four outcomes: Hit, Miss, False Alarm, Correct Rejection
- Trade-off between sensitivity and specificity
- Setting thresholds depends on the costs of each error type
- **Application**: When filtering research sources, define your acceptable false positive rate

### Regression to the Mean
- Extreme observations tend to be followed by less extreme ones
- Not because of any causal mechanism, just statistics
- **Application**: Exceptional performance data often reverts; account for this in predictions

## Common Irrationalities

### Motivated Reasoning
- We reason to win arguments, not to find truth
- Confirmation bias is the default mode
- Smart people are better at rationalizing, not better at being rational
- **Counter**: Actively argue against your preferred conclusion

### Availability Cascade
- A belief gains credibility simply through repetition
- Media amplifies certain risks while ignoring larger ones
- **Counter**: Check actual frequency data, not gut feelings

### Taboo Trade-offs
- Refusing to think about certain comparisons blocks rational analysis
- Sacred values resist cost-benefit analysis
- **Counter**: Acknowledge values explicitly, then analyze trade-offs separately

### The Rationality Gap
- People can score high on IQ tests and still reason poorly
- Intelligence is not the same as rationality
- Rational thinking requires learned tools, not just raw ability

## Rational Thinking Checklist for Researchers
1. Have I considered the base rate?
2. Am I confusing correlation with causation?
3. Is my sample representative or biased?
4. Am I falling for the conjunction fallacy?
5. Have I considered regression to the mean?
6. Am I engaging in motivated reasoning?
7. Would I accept this evidence if it supported the opposite conclusion?
8. Have I updated my beliefs proportionally to the evidence?
9. Am I conflating the vividness of evidence with its reliability?
10. Have I considered what I would need to see to change my mind?

## Application to Deep Research
- Use formal logic to check argument validity
- Apply Bayesian reasoning when combining evidence from multiple sources
- Define signal detection thresholds for source quality filtering
- Guard against motivated reasoning by pre-committing to evaluation criteria
- Recognize when taboo trade-offs are preventing clear analysis
