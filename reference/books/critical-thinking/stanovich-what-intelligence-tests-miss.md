# Stanovich - What Intelligence Tests Miss: The Psychology of Rational Thought

## Core Thesis
Intelligence (IQ) and rationality are different constructs. Smart people can think irrationally. Rational thinking requires specific cognitive skills that IQ tests do not measure.

## The Tripartite Mind

### Autonomous Mind (TASS - The Autonomous Set of Systems)
- Fast, automatic, parallel processing
- Pattern matching and associative thinking
- Similar to Kahneman's System 1
- Cannot be directly controlled

### Algorithmic Mind
- What IQ tests measure: processing speed, working memory, pattern recognition
- Computational power for solving defined problems
- Necessary but not sufficient for rationality

### Reflective Mind
- Disposition to override automatic responses
- Willingness to think carefully and revise beliefs
- Values epistemic accuracy over cognitive ease
- THIS is what determines rational thinking

## Dysrationalia
- Inability to think and behave rationally despite adequate intelligence
- Smart people making poor decisions is the norm, not the exception
- Causes: mindware gaps and mindware contamination

### Mindware Gaps (Missing Knowledge)
- Not knowing about probabilistic reasoning
- Lacking knowledge of logic and argument structure
- Not understanding scientific methodology
- Unfamiliarity with base rates and Bayesian updating

### Mindware Contamination (Bad Knowledge)
- Conspiracy theories and unfalsifiable beliefs
- Superstitions and magical thinking
- Ideological reasoning that resists evidence
- Folk theories that contradict empirical data

## Thinking Dispositions That Improve Rationality
1. **Need for cognition**: Enjoying effortful thinking
2. **Actively open-minded thinking**: Seeking disconfirming evidence
3. **Epistemic humility**: Recognizing the limits of one's knowledge
4. **Reflectivity**: Pausing before accepting intuitive answers
5. **Counterfactual thinking**: Considering alternatives to what happened
6. **Probabilistic thinking**: Reasoning in degrees rather than absolutes

## The Cognitive Miser Problem
- Humans default to least-effort cognitive strategies
- System 2 is lazy and expensive to engage
- Most reasoning errors come from not thinking enough, not thinking wrong
- **Solution**: Build habits and environments that trigger deeper thinking

## Rational Thinking Assessment
Unlike IQ, rational thinking requires:
- Knowledge of probability and statistics
- Ability to separate beliefs from evidence
- Skill in hypothesis testing
- Recognition of one's own biases
- Disposition to seek and consider contrary evidence

## Application to Deep Research
1. Do not assume intelligence guarantees good analysis
2. Build systematic processes that force reflective thinking
3. Install "mindware": learn probability, logic, scientific method
4. Create checklists that counter cognitive miser tendencies
5. Measure and reward rational thinking dispositions, not just output speed
6. Actively seek to identify mindware gaps in your research process
7. Treat rationality as a skill to be trained, not a fixed trait
