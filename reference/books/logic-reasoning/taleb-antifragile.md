# Taleb - Antifragile: Things That Gain from Disorder

## The Triad: Fragile, Robust, Antifragile

### Fragile
- Harmed by volatility, randomness, stressors
- Wants tranquility and predictability
- Examples: Glass, over-optimized systems, highly leveraged companies
- Concave payoff: limited upside, unlimited downside

### Robust
- Indifferent to volatility and stressors
- Neither helped nor harmed by disorder
- Examples: A rock, redundant systems, cash reserves
- Flat payoff regardless of conditions

### Antifragile
- Benefits from volatility, randomness, stressors
- Gets stronger under pressure (up to a point)
- Examples: Muscles, evolution, entrepreneurial ecosystems, option portfolios
- Convex payoff: limited downside, unlimited upside

## Key Principles

### Via Negativa
- Improvement by removal rather than addition
- Removing harmful things is more robust than adding beneficial things
- In research: eliminating errors is more valuable than adding sophistication
- "What should I stop doing?" is more actionable than "What should I start doing?"

### Skin in the Game
- Decision makers must bear consequences of their decisions
- Without skin in the game, incentives distort judgment
- Analysts who never face consequences of wrong predictions are unreliable
- **Research impact**: Weight sources by their skin in the game

### Optionality
- Options provide asymmetric payoffs (limited downside, open upside)
- Tinkering and experimentation create options
- You do not need to predict the future if you have options
- **Research approach**: Present multiple scenarios rather than single predictions

### The Lindy Effect
- For non-perishable things, life expectancy increases with age
- A book that has been in print for 50 years will likely be in print for 50 more
- Old technologies/ideas have a longer expected remaining life
- **Research heuristic**: Prefer established frameworks over new fads

## Fragility Detection
1. Does it have more downside than upside? (Fragile)
2. Does a small input cause a disproportionately large output? (Fragile)
3. Does it need things to go exactly as planned? (Fragile)
4. Is it over-optimized for one scenario? (Fragile)
5. Does it concentrate risk in one place? (Fragile)

## Application to Research Assessment

### Evaluating Companies/Systems
- How much volatility can this company handle?
- Does it have optionality (many small bets vs one big bet)?
- Is it over-optimized for current conditions?
- Does it have skin in the game?
- Does it follow the Lindy effect or fight against it?

### Building Antifragile Research
1. Use multiple independent sources (redundancy)
2. Prefer robust methods over sophisticated fragile ones
3. Build in error correction mechanisms
4. Test conclusions against extreme scenarios
5. Prefer strategies that benefit from being partially wrong
6. Remove weak links before adding new strengths
7. Favor conclusions that are robust across many scenarios
