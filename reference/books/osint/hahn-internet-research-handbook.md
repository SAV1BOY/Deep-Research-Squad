# Hahn - Internet Research Handbook

## Internet Research Methods

### Research Planning
1. Define your information need precisely
2. Identify the best source types for your question
3. Choose appropriate search tools and databases
4. Execute searches systematically
5. Evaluate and synthesize findings
6. Document your search strategy

### Source Hierarchy for Internet Research
1. **Primary databases**: Government data, academic repositories, SEC filings
2. **Peer-reviewed sources**: Academic journals, conference proceedings
3. **Professional sources**: Industry reports, analyst coverage, trade publications
4. **Curated aggregators**: News aggregators, topic-specific portals
5. **General web**: Websites, blogs, forums (verify carefully)
6. **Social media**: Real-time but unverified (corroborate always)

## Search Strategy Development

### Building Effective Queries
- Start broad, then narrow systematically
- Use Boolean operators: AND, OR, NOT
- Use phrase searching with quotation marks
- Apply truncation/wildcards for variant forms
- Combine field-specific searches (title, author, date)

### Search Iteration Process
1. **Initial search**: Broad terms, scan results for better terminology
2. **Refined search**: Use discovered keywords, apply filters
3. **Deep search**: Specific databases, advanced operators
4. **Lateral search**: Follow citations, related articles, linked sources
5. **Verification search**: Confirm key findings through independent sources

### The Invisible Web
- Content behind login walls, paywalls, databases
- Dynamic content not indexed by search engines
- Much larger than the visible web
- Access through: specialized databases, library subscriptions, direct requests

## Source Evaluation Framework

### CRAAP Test
- **Currency**: When was it published/updated?
- **Relevance**: Does it relate to your topic?
- **Authority**: Who is the author/publisher?
- **Accuracy**: Is it supported by evidence?
- **Purpose**: Why does this content exist? (Inform, sell, persuade?)

### Cross-Referencing
- Never rely on a single source for critical claims
- Verify facts across at least 3 independent sources
- Check if sources are truly independent (not citing each other)
- Look for primary sources behind secondary claims

## Organizing Research Findings
- Use consistent citation format from the start
- Tag and categorize findings by theme
- Track source quality ratings
- Maintain a search log (queries used, databases searched, dates)
- Note gaps: what you searched for but could not find

## Legal and Ethical Considerations
- Respect terms of service for websites and databases
- Do not circumvent access controls
- Attribute sources properly
- Be aware of copyright limitations
- Consider privacy implications of personal data collection

## Application to Deep Research
- Always start with a search strategy, not ad hoc browsing
- Use the source hierarchy to prioritize higher-quality sources
- Document your search methodology for reproducibility
- Apply the CRAAP test to every source before citing
- Explore the invisible web through specialized databases
- Maintain a research log for audit trail
