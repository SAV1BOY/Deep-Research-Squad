# Sterman - Business Dynamics: Systems Thinking and Modeling for a Complex World

## System Dynamics Modeling

### Core Concept
System dynamics uses computer simulation to understand complex systems. It focuses on how structure (feedback loops, delays, nonlinearities) determines behavior over time.

### Modeling Process
1. **Problem articulation**: Define the problem dynamically (behavior over time)
2. **Formulate dynamic hypothesis**: Map causal structure producing the problem
3. **Build simulation model**: Translate structure into equations
4. **Test model**: Compare to reference behavior, sensitivity analysis
5. **Design and evaluate policies**: Test interventions in the model

## Key Modeling Elements

### Stocks (Levels)
- Accumulations that characterize the state of the system
- Examples: Inventory, workforce, brand equity, technical debt
- Change through inflows and outflows only
- Create memory and inertia in systems

### Flows (Rates)
- Activities that fill or drain stocks
- Examples: Hiring/firing, production/consumption, R&D spending
- Determined by information about stocks and other variables
- Subject to delays and constraints

### Feedback Loops
- **Positive (reinforcing)**: More leads to more (growth, collapse)
- **Negative (balancing)**: Discrepancy drives corrective action (goals, regulation)
- **Dominant loop**: The loop currently driving system behavior
- Loop dominance shifts over time, changing system behavior

### Delays
- Time between cause and effect
- Material delays: Physical flow (shipping, construction)
- Information delays: Perception, reporting, decision-making
- Delays cause oscillation, overshoot, and instability
- Often underestimated by decision makers

## Common Dynamic Behaviors

### Exponential Growth
- Single reinforcing loop, no limits
- Doubling time = 70 / growth rate (Rule of 70)

### Goal-Seeking
- Balancing loop drives toward target
- Speed depends on gap and adjustment time

### S-Shaped Growth
- Reinforcing loop drives initial growth
- Limits (balancing loop) slow growth to carrying capacity

### Oscillation
- Balancing loop with delays
- Overcorrection and undercorrection
- Longer delays = larger oscillations

### Overshoot and Collapse
- Reinforcing growth erodes carrying capacity
- System overshoots then collapses (resource depletion)

## Mental Simulation Errors
- Failure to account for feedback
- Ignoring time delays
- Assuming linear relationships (most are nonlinear)
- Thinking in terms of events, not patterns
- Ignoring stock-flow relationships
- Treating correlation as sufficient for understanding

## Application to Deep Research
- Map key stocks and flows for any industry or market analysis
- Identify dominant feedback loops driving observed trends
- Account for delays when assessing timing of market changes
- Use behavior-over-time graphs to identify dynamic patterns
- Check for overshoot risks when growth seems unsustainable
- Test "what if" scenarios by tracing effects through the system structure
- Recognize that complex behavior often arises from simple structures
