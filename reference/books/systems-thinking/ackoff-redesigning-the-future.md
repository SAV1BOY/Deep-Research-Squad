# Ackoff - Redesigning the Future / Systems Design

## Core Philosophy
Problems do not exist in isolation. They exist in systems of interacting problems (messes). To solve a problem, you must address the mess.

## Problem Types

### Simple Problems
- Clear cause, clear solution
- Can be solved analytically
- Example: Fixing a broken machine

### Complex Problems (Messes)
- Multiple interacting causes
- No single solution; requires systemic intervention
- Example: Declining market share, organizational dysfunction

### Wicked Problems
- No definitive formulation
- No stopping rule (never fully solved)
- Solutions are not true/false but better/worse
- Every solution changes the problem

## Three Approaches to Problems

### 1. Resolve (Satisfice)
- Find a "good enough" solution
- Relies on experience and common sense
- Quick but may not address root cause
- Most common approach

### 2. Solve (Optimize)
- Find the best solution within current constraints
- Uses analytical methods and models
- More thorough but may miss systemic issues
- Research-heavy approach

### 3. Dissolve (Idealize)
- Redesign the system so the problem no longer exists
- Most creative and effective
- Requires questioning fundamental assumptions
- Hardest but most impactful

## Idealized Design Process
1. **Mission**: What is the system's purpose?
2. **Specify desired properties**: What would the ideal system look like?
3. **Design**: Create a system with those properties (ignore current constraints initially)
4. **Approximate**: Identify the closest feasible approximation
5. **Implement**: Move toward the ideal incrementally

## Types of Planning

### Reactive Planning (Past-oriented)
- Try to return to a previous state
- Nostalgic; resists change
- Deals with parts separately

### Inactive Planning (Present-oriented)
- Try to maintain the status quo
- Bureaucratic; prevent change
- Satisfied with good enough

### Preactive Planning (Future-oriented)
- Try to predict and prepare for the future
- Forecast-dependent; optimize within predictions
- Technology-focused

### Interactive Planning (Ideal-oriented)
- Design a desirable future and invent ways to get there
- Participative; treats the future as creatable
- Combines all stakeholder perspectives

## Key Principles
- **Analysis**: Takes things apart to understand
- **Synthesis**: Puts things together to understand (more important)
- The performance of a system depends on how parts interact, not how they act independently
- Improving parts separately can worsen the whole
- Start with the whole, then understand parts in context

## Application to Deep Research
- Treat research problems as messes, not isolated questions
- Use dissolution thinking; synthesize across domains rather than analyzing in silos
- Question whether improving one metric may worsen the system
- Map interactions between problems before proposing solutions
