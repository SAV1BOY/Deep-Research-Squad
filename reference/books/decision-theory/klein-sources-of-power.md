# Klein - Sources of Power: How People Make Decisions

## Naturalistic Decision Making (NDM)

### Core Insight
Experts in real-world settings rarely compare options analytically. They use pattern recognition to identify a workable solution quickly, then mentally simulate it to check for problems.

### Recognition-Primed Decision (RPD) Model
1. **Recognize the situation**: Pattern match to past experience
2. **Identify a course of action**: The first option that fits the pattern
3. **Mental simulation**: Imagine executing the action; check for problems
4. **If it works**: Implement
5. **If problems found**: Modify or move to next recognized option

### Key Difference from Classical Decision Theory
- Classical: List all options, evaluate all criteria, choose the best
- NDM: Recognize the type of situation, find a workable solution, simulate it
- Experts do not compare options; they evaluate one at a time
- This is faster and works better under time pressure and uncertainty

## Sources of Expert Power

### Pattern Recognition
- Experts see patterns that novices miss
- Patterns are built from extensive experience
- "Intuition" is really compressed pattern recognition
- Takes roughly 10 years / 10,000 hours to develop deep expertise

### Mental Simulation
- Running a scenario forward in your mind
- Checking for feasibility, side effects, failure modes
- Enables rapid evaluation without full analysis
- Limited to about 6 steps into the future (cognitive constraint)

### Leverage Points
- Experts see opportunities others miss
- They know where small actions produce large effects
- Built from deep understanding of domain dynamics

### Anomaly Detection
- Experts notice when something is "off" even before they can explain why
- Subtle cues that violate expected patterns trigger attention
- This is the basis of expert intuition

## When Expert Intuition Fails
- Novel situations with no relevant patterns
- Environments with poor feedback (delayed or absent)
- Domains with low validity (stock picking, political prediction)
- When experts become overconfident in their patterns
- When the environment changes but patterns do not update

### Conditions for Trustworthy Intuition (Kahneman/Klein joint paper)
1. Sufficiently regular environment (patterns exist)
2. Adequate opportunity to learn regularities (practice with feedback)
3. If both conditions met, expert intuition is valuable
4. If not, use structured analysis instead

## Practical Decision Strategies from NDM

### Pre-mortem
- Assume the plan has failed
- Ask everyone: "What went wrong?"
- Surfaces concerns people are reluctant to voice

### Crystal Ball
- If you knew the answer, what would the key variables be?
- Works backward from outcome to identify critical factors

### Option Generation in Teams
- Do not brainstorm many options
- Generate one good option, then stress-test it
- Only seek alternatives when the current option fails simulation

## Application to Deep Research
1. Trust expert intuition in regular, high-feedback domains
2. Use structured analysis in novel, low-feedback domains
3. Mental simulation is a valid analysis technique: "If this is true, what happens next?"
4. Look for anomalies that violate expected patterns
5. Use pre-mortems to stress-test conclusions
6. Recognize that the first reasonable hypothesis may be the best starting point
7. Build expertise through deliberate practice with feedback
8. Know when to trust intuition and when to override it with data
