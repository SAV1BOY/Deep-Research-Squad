# Hubbard - How to Measure Anything: Finding the Value of Intangibles in Business

## Core Thesis
If something matters, it is observable. If it is observable, it can be measured. The claim that something is "immeasurable" usually means we have not thought carefully about what we mean.

## The Measurement Problem

### What Measurement Really Is
- Measurement = a quantitatively expressed reduction of uncertainty
- You do not need exact numbers; reducing uncertainty is measurement
- Even a rough estimate is better than "we have no idea"
- Perfect precision is unnecessary and often impossible

### Why Things Seem Immeasurable
1. Concept not defined clearly enough
2. Object of measurement confused with its units
3. Underestimation of available data
4. Overestimation of needed precision

## The Applied Information Economics (AIE) Process

### Step 1: Define the Decision
- What is the decision to be made?
- What are the variables that matter?
- What ranges of values would change the decision?

### Step 2: Determine What You Know Now
- Calibrated probability estimates (90% confidence intervals)
- Most people's 90% CI captures the true value only 50-70% of the time
- Practice calibration to improve

### Step 3: Compute the Value of Additional Information
- Expected Value of Perfect Information (EVPI)
- Expected Value of Information (EVI) for imperfect measurements
- Focus measurement effort on high-value-of-information variables
- Often, only 1-3 variables justify further measurement

### Step 4: Measure Where It Matters
- Use sampling (you rarely need to measure everything)
- Small samples often provide surprisingly useful information
- The Rule of Five: Five random samples have a 93.75% chance of containing the median
- Bayesian updating: combine prior estimates with new data

### Step 5: Make a Decision and Act
- Update estimates based on measurements
- Monte Carlo simulation: model uncertainty across all variables
- Compute expected value under uncertainty

## Calibration Training
- Practice estimating ranges for known quantities
- Get feedback on your accuracy
- Goal: when you say 90% confident, be right 90% of the time
- Most people need training to achieve good calibration
- Trivia exercises work well for calibration practice

## Measurement Methods for "Soft" Variables
- Customer satisfaction: sampled surveys, behavioral proxies
- Innovation capability: patent output, idea pipeline metrics
- Organizational culture: behavioral observation, turnover rates
- Brand value: willingness-to-pay experiments, conjoint analysis
- Risk: historical frequency, Monte Carlo simulation

## Key Principles
1. Measure what matters for the decision, not what is easy
2. Focus on the variable with the highest information value
3. Small samples are surprisingly informative
4. Decompose complex estimates into measurable components
5. Calibrate your uncertainty (you are probably overconfident)

## Application to Deep Research
- When a client says "this cannot be measured," challenge that assumption
- Use calibrated estimates when hard data is unavailable
- Calculate information value before investing in more research
- Apply Fermi decomposition to break complex estimates into parts
- Use Monte Carlo simulation for risk analysis and forecasting
- Always express uncertainty as ranges, never point estimates
- Small, targeted samples can resolve most measurement questions
