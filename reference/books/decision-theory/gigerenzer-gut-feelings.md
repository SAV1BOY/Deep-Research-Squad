# Gigerenzer - Gut Feelings: The Intelligence of the Unconscious

## Core Thesis
Simple heuristics often outperform complex analysis, especially in uncertain environments with limited data. Less can be more.

## Heuristics That Work

### Recognition Heuristic
- If you recognize one option but not the other, choose the recognized one
- Works when recognition correlates with the criterion
- Example: Recognized brands are often (not always) larger/more established
- Fails when recognition is manipulated (advertising without substance)

### Take-the-Best Heuristic
- Look at cues in order of validity
- Choose based on the first cue that differentiates
- Ignore all remaining cues
- Surprisingly accurate; avoids overfitting

### 1/N (Equality) Heuristic
- Allocate equally across N options
- Often outperforms complex optimization in portfolio allocation
- Works well when: uncertain environment, many alternatives, small sample
- Robust because it does not try to estimate parameters

### Fast-and-Frugal Trees
- Sequential decision rules with few steps
- At each node: one question, two or three exits
- One exit leads to a decision, the other continues
- Used in medical triage, emergency decisions

### Satisficing (Simon)
- Set a threshold; accept the first option that meets it
- Do not search for the optimal; stop at good enough
- Works when search costs are high and options are many
- Foundation of bounded rationality

## When Heuristics Beat Complex Models

### Bias-Variance Trade-off
- **Complex models**: Low bias but high variance (overfit to sample)
- **Simple heuristics**: Some bias but low variance (generalize better)
- With small samples or high uncertainty, simpler wins
- More parameters does not mean better predictions

### Ecological Rationality
- Heuristics are adapted to specific environments
- A heuristic that works in one environment may fail in another
- Match the heuristic to the information structure of the problem
- The key question: What information is available and reliable?

## When Heuristics Fail
- Stable, data-rich environments (use complex models instead)
- When recognition is systematically misleading
- When the environment has changed but heuristics have not
- When all cues have roughly equal validity (Take-the-Best struggles)

## Less-Is-More Effects
- More information does not always improve decisions
- More computation does not always improve predictions
- More time does not always improve judgment
- The art is knowing when simplicity wins and when complexity is needed

## Application to Deep Research
1. Use simple heuristics for initial screening and prioritization
2. Apply complex analysis only where data and stability justify it
3. When data is scarce, prefer simpler models and wider uncertainty ranges
4. Recognize that your gut feelings encode valid pattern recognition
5. But also recognize when gut feelings are biased (use checklists)
6. Match analytical complexity to data quality and availability
7. Satisfice for breadth; optimize for depth on highest-priority questions
8. Test whether simpler models outperform complex ones for your use case
