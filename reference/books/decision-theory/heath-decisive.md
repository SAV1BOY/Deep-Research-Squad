# Heath & Heath - Decisive: How to Make Better Choices in Life and Work

## The WRAP Framework

### Four Villains of Decision Making
1. **Narrow framing**: Seeing too few options
2. **Confirmation bias**: Seeking information that supports our preference
3. **Short-term emotion**: Being swayed by temporary feelings
4. **Overconfidence**: Being too sure about future outcomes

### W - Widen Your Options
- Avoid "whether or not" decisions (binary framing)
- Consider opportunity cost: What else could you do with these resources?
- Run the "Vanishing Options Test": If your current options disappeared, what would you do?
- Look at what others have done in similar situations (best practices)
- Consider "this AND that" instead of "this OR that" (multitracking)

### R - Reality-Test Your Assumptions
- Get outside your head: ask disconfirming questions
- Zoom out: What does the base rate say? (statistics about similar situations)
- Zoom in: What does a close-up look like? (concrete, specific examples)
- Run small experiments (ooch) before committing fully
- Ask "What would have to be true for this option to be the right one?"

### A - Attain Distance Before Deciding
- Overcome short-term emotion with 10/10/10 analysis:
  - How will you feel about this in 10 minutes?
  - In 10 months?
  - In 10 years?
- Consider your core priorities (what matters most?)
- Ask "What would I advise my best friend to do?"
- Set a timer: Sleep on important decisions

### P - Prepare to Be Wrong
- Bookend the future: Best case and worst case
- Set a tripwire: Pre-commit to re-evaluating if X happens
- Build in a "safety factor" for overconfidence
- Consider the "pre-mortem": It is 6 months later and this decision was a disaster. Why?
- Create a decision journal to track and learn from outcomes

## Practical Decision Tools

### Pre-mortem
1. Assume the decision has failed spectacularly
2. Each person independently writes reasons for failure
3. Share and compile reasons
4. Address the most likely failure modes
5. Decide whether to proceed, modify, or abandon

### Tripwires
- Set specific conditions that trigger re-evaluation
- Prevents the status quo bias of "we'll keep going"
- Examples: revenue drops below X, customer satisfaction falls below Y
- Automates the decision to reconsider

### Decision Journal
- Record: Date, decision, options considered, reasoning, confidence level
- Revisit: Compare prediction to outcome
- Learn: Were you calibrated? What did you miss?

## Application to Deep Research
1. Widen: Always present multiple options/scenarios, never just one recommendation
2. Reality-test: Seek disconfirming evidence for every hypothesis
3. Distance: Separate data collection from evaluation (reduce emotional bias)
4. Prepare: Include risk scenarios and failure modes in every analysis
5. Use pre-mortems before finalizing research conclusions
6. Set tripwires for monitoring ongoing situations
7. Ask "What would have to be true?" to test competing hypotheses
