# Van der Heijden - Scenarios: The Art of Strategic Conversation

## Scenario Development Framework

### Scenarios as Strategic Conversation Tools
- Purpose: Improve the quality of strategic thinking and conversation
- Not about predicting the future but about perceiving the present more clearly
- Challenge existing mental models in a safe environment
- Create shared language for discussing uncertainty

### The Business Idea
- Every organization has an implicit "business idea" — its theory of success
- Scenarios test whether the business idea holds under different futures
- Vulnerable business idea = vulnerable strategy
- Robust business idea = resilient strategy

## Inductive vs Deductive Scenario Building

### Deductive Approach (Top-Down)
1. Identify 2-3 key uncertainties
2. Create a matrix from these uncertainties
3. Fill in scenario narratives for each quadrant
4. Good for structured, time-limited workshops
5. Risk: may feel forced or artificial

### Inductive Approach (Bottom-Up)
1. Gather a wide range of events, trends, and forces
2. Cluster them into coherent groupings
3. Build narratives from clusters
4. Allow scenarios to emerge organically
5. Risk: more time-consuming, harder to facilitate

### Hybrid Approach (Recommended)
1. Start inductively: brainstorm forces and trends
2. Cluster and prioritize
3. Use deductive framework to structure final scenarios
4. Fill in with inductive detail

## Scenario Quality Criteria

### Plausibility Test
- Can you tell a credible story of how we get from here to there?
- Are the causal links believable?
- Do experts find it conceivable (even if unlikely)?

### Consistency Test
- Do the trends and events within a scenario fit together?
- No internal contradictions
- Actions and reactions make sense

### Relevance Test
- Does each scenario illuminate the focal decision?
- Do different scenarios lead to different strategic implications?
- Are decision makers engaged by the scenarios?

### Challenge Test
- Do scenarios challenge conventional wisdom?
- Do they reveal blind spots in current strategy?
- Do they surface assumptions that need testing?

## From Scenarios to Strategy

### Testing Current Strategy
- How does our current strategy perform in each scenario?
- Where is it robust? Where is it vulnerable?
- What would we need to change under each scenario?

### Developing Robust Options
- Identify strategies that perform well across scenarios
- Create contingency plans for scenario-specific risks
- Build strategic options (investments that create future flexibility)

### Wind-Tunneling
- Test any strategic option against all scenarios
- Assess performance, risks, and adaptability
- Modify options to improve robustness
- Choose the portfolio of actions that performs best overall

## Early Warning Systems
- Each scenario implies different early indicators
- Monitor indicators to detect which scenario is emerging
- Pre-define response triggers
- Update scenarios periodically (at least annually)

## Application to Deep Research
- Use scenarios as a framework for presenting uncertain futures
- Always test conclusions against multiple scenarios
- Wind-tunnel recommendations against at least 3 different futures
- Help clients develop robust strategies, not optimal-for-one-scenario strategies
- Build early warning dashboards into research deliverables
- Use scenario conversations to surface hidden assumptions
