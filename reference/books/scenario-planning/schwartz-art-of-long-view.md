# Schwartz - The Art of the Long View: Planning for the Future in an Uncertain World

## Scenario Planning Methodology

### What Scenarios Are
- Plausible stories about different futures
- NOT predictions; NOT preferences; NOT science fiction
- Tools for stretching mental models
- Multiple stories that bracket the range of possibilities
- Typically 2-4 scenarios (more is unmanageable)

### What Scenarios Are NOT
- Single-point forecasts
- Best case / worst case / most likely (too simple)
- Descriptions of what we want to happen
- Covers for predetermined conclusions

## The Scenario Building Process

### Step 1: Identify Focal Issue or Decision
- What keeps you up at night?
- What decision do you need to make?
- What is the time horizon? (typically 5-20 years)
- Be specific about the decision context

### Step 2: Identify Key Forces in the Local Environment
- Customers, competitors, suppliers, regulators
- Technology trends in the specific domain
- Market dynamics and industry structure
- What factors will most influence the outcome?

### Step 3: Identify Driving Forces in the Macro Environment
- Social dynamics (demographics, values, lifestyles)
- Economic forces (growth, inflation, trade)
- Political dynamics (regulation, stability, policy)
- Technological change (innovation pace, disruption)
- Environmental factors (climate, resources)

### Step 4: Rank by Importance and Uncertainty
- Plot forces on 2x2: Importance x Uncertainty
- **Predetermined elements**: Important but certain (include in all scenarios)
- **Critical uncertainties**: Important AND uncertain (these differentiate scenarios)
- Focus scenario logic on 2-3 critical uncertainties

### Step 5: Select Scenario Logics
- Choose the two most critical uncertainties as axes
- Create a 2x2 matrix of scenario frameworks
- Each quadrant is a different scenario
- Ensure scenarios are internally consistent

### Step 6: Flesh Out the Scenarios
- Give each scenario a memorable name
- Write a narrative: how does the world get from here to there?
- Include specific details and events
- Make it vivid and concrete

### Step 7: Implications
- What does each scenario mean for our decision?
- What strategies work across multiple scenarios? (robust strategies)
- What early warning indicators should we monitor?
- What capabilities do we need regardless of scenario?

### Step 8: Selection of Leading Indicators
- Identify observable signs that one scenario is emerging
- Create a monitoring dashboard
- Set up triggers for strategic response

## Principles of Good Scenarios
- Plausible (could actually happen)
- Internally consistent (events and trends align)
- Relevant (address the focal issue)
- Challenging (stretch thinking beyond comfort zone)
- Divergent (genuinely different from each other)
- Memorable (easy to reference in discussions)

## Common Mistakes
- Treating scenarios as forecasts or making one scenario obviously "best"
- Not challenging core assumptions; too many scenarios (more than 4)
- Not connecting scenarios to actual decisions or early warning indicators

## Application to Deep Research
- Use scenarios whenever uncertainty is high and consequences are significant
- Connect scenarios back to actionable decisions
- Look for robust strategies that work across multiple scenarios
- Monitor early warning indicators and present scenarios as narratives
