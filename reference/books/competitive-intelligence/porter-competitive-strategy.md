# Porter - Competitive Strategy

## The Five Forces Framework

### 1. Threat of New Entrants
**Barriers to entry:**
- Economies of scale
- Capital requirements
- Switching costs for customers
- Access to distribution channels
- Government regulation and licensing
- Brand loyalty and reputation
- Network effects

### 2. Bargaining Power of Suppliers
**Powerful when:**
- Few suppliers dominate
- No substitutes for their input
- Switching costs are high
- They can forward-integrate
- Industry is not an important customer

### 3. Bargaining Power of Buyers
**Powerful when:**
- Few buyers purchase large volumes
- Products are standardized
- Switching costs are low
- Buyers can backward-integrate
- Full information about alternatives

### 4. Threat of Substitutes
**High when:**
- Substitute offers better price-performance
- Switching costs are low
- Buyer propensity to substitute is high
- Cross-industry disruption is possible

### 5. Competitive Rivalry
**Intense when:**
- Many competitors of similar size
- Slow industry growth
- High fixed costs (pressure to fill capacity)
- Low differentiation
- High exit barriers

## Three Generic Strategies

### Cost Leadership
- Lowest cost producer in the industry
- Economies of scale, efficient operations, cost control
- Risk: Technology changes, competitors imitate, quality perception

### Differentiation
- Unique product/service valued by customers
- Brand, features, technology, service, network
- Risk: Price gap too large, imitation, buyer needs change

### Focus (Niche)
- Serve a specific segment better than broad competitors
- Cost focus or differentiation focus within segment
- Risk: Segment becomes unattractive, broad competitors invade

### The Danger of Being Stuck in the Middle
- No clear strategy = below-average profitability
- Cannot compete on cost OR differentiation
- Common mistake: trying to be everything to everyone

## Industry Analysis Process
1. Define the industry boundaries
2. Identify the players in each force
3. Assess the underlying drivers of each force
4. Determine overall industry structure and profitability
5. Identify most important forces (focus analysis here)
6. Analyze recent and expected changes in forces
7. Identify aspects of structure you can influence

## Application to Deep Research
- Use Five Forces to assess industry attractiveness before deep dives
- Map competitive strategy of each major player
- Assess sustainability of competitive advantages (unique value, replicability)
- Look for structural shifts that change the force balance
- Combine with PESTEL for comprehensive external analysis
