# Creswell - Research Design: Qualitative, Quantitative, and Mixed Methods

## Core Framework

Three research approaches, each suited to different questions:

### Quantitative Research
- **When to use**: Testing theories, measuring variables, establishing cause-effect
- **Design types**: Experimental, quasi-experimental, correlational, survey
- **Data**: Numbers, statistics, closed-ended instruments
- **Sample sizes**: Large (statistical power needed)
- **Key question**: "How much?" or "What is the relationship between X and Y?"

### Qualitative Research
- **When to use**: Exploring phenomena, understanding meaning, building theory
- **Design types**: Narrative, phenomenology, grounded theory, ethnography, case study
- **Data**: Words, images, open-ended interviews, observations
- **Sample sizes**: Small, purposeful sampling
- **Key question**: "What is the experience of?" or "How do people make sense of?"

### Mixed Methods
- **When to use**: When one approach alone is insufficient
- **Convergent**: Collect both simultaneously, merge results
- **Explanatory sequential**: Quantitative first, then qualitative to explain
- **Exploratory sequential**: Qualitative first, then quantitative to generalize

## Research Process Steps
1. Identify the research problem
2. Review literature to frame the problem
3. Specify a purpose and research questions
4. Collect data using appropriate methods
5. Analyze and interpret data
6. Report and evaluate the research

## Choosing Your Approach
| Factor | Quantitative | Qualitative | Mixed |
|--------|-------------|-------------|-------|
| Problem type | Known variables | Concept needs exploring | Both needed |
| Literature | Substantial exists | Little exists | Both apply |
| Audience | Expects numbers | Expects narratives | Both needed |
| Researcher skill | Statistics | Interviewing/coding | Both |

## Practical Application for Deep Research
- Start qualitative when exploring a new domain (interviews, document analysis)
- Move to quantitative when you need to validate patterns at scale
- Use mixed methods for comprehensive market/industry analyses
- Match your method to the research question, not your preference

## Key Pitfalls
- Forcing a method onto a question it does not fit
- Inadequate sample size for the chosen approach
- Mixing methods without a clear integration strategy
- Confusing research approach with data collection technique

## Quality Criteria
- **Quantitative**: Validity, reliability, generalizability
- **Qualitative**: Credibility, transferability, dependability, confirmability
- **Mixed**: Both sets of criteria plus integration quality
