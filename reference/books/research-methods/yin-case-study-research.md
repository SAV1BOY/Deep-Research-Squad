# Yin - Case Study Research: Design and Methods

## When to Use Case Studies
- Research question is "how" or "why"
- Researcher has little control over events
- Focus is on contemporary phenomenon in real-life context
- Boundaries between phenomenon and context are unclear

## Case Study Types
1. **Exploratory**: Preliminary investigation, generate hypotheses
2. **Descriptive**: Complete description of phenomenon in context
3. **Explanatory**: Explain causal links in real-life interventions

## Design Components
1. **Research questions**: Clearly defined "how" or "why" questions
2. **Propositions**: What you expect to find (guides data collection)
3. **Unit of analysis**: What the "case" is (person, org, event, decision)
4. **Logic linking data to propositions**: Pattern matching, explanation building
5. **Criteria for interpreting findings**: Rival explanations addressed

## Single vs Multiple Case Designs
### Single Case - Use when:
- Critical case testing a well-formulated theory
- Extreme or unique case
- Representative or typical case
- Revelatory case (previously inaccessible)
- Longitudinal case (same case at different times)

### Multiple Case - Use when:
- Replication logic needed (literal or theoretical)
- Cross-case patterns strengthen findings
- Greater external validity required

## Data Collection: Six Sources of Evidence
1. **Documents**: Letters, reports, articles, administrative records
2. **Archival records**: Service records, organizational charts, budgets
3. **Interviews**: Open-ended, focused, structured
4. **Direct observation**: Site visits, field observations
5. **Participant observation**: Researcher takes a role
6. **Physical artifacts**: Devices, tools, outputs

## Three Principles of Data Collection
1. Use multiple sources of evidence (triangulation)
2. Create a case study database (organized, retrievable)
3. Maintain a chain of evidence (reader can trace conclusions to data)

## Analysis Strategies
- **Pattern matching**: Compare empirical pattern with predicted
- **Explanation building**: Iterative narrative of causal links
- **Time-series analysis**: Trace events over time
- **Logic models**: Cause-effect chains
- **Cross-case synthesis**: Aggregate findings across cases

## Common Pitfalls
- Lack of rigor (sloppy evidence, biased views)
- Confusion with teaching case studies (no systematic procedures)
- Too long, unreadable reports
- No clear criteria for case selection
- Generalizing from a single case without theoretical basis

## Application to Deep Research
- Use case studies to analyze specific companies, markets, or decisions
- Always triangulate with multiple evidence sources
- Document your chain of evidence for credibility
- Multiple mini-cases strengthen competitive analysis
