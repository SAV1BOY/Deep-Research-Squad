# Comparative Analysis

## Overview
Comparative analysis systematically examines similarities and differences between two or more cases, entities, or phenomena to identify patterns, test theories, and generate insights. Fundamental method across social sciences, business strategy, and policy research.

## Types of Comparative Analysis

### Most Similar Systems Design (MSSD)
- Compare cases that are similar on most variables but differ on the outcome
- Identifies the variable(s) responsible for the different outcome
- **Example**: Comparing two similar companies where one succeeded and one failed
- Controls for confounding variables through case selection

### Most Different Systems Design (MDSD)
- Compare cases that differ on most variables but share the same outcome
- Identifies common factors despite different contexts
- **Example**: AI adoption succeeding across different industries/cultures
- Strengthens generalizability of identified factors

### Qualitative Comparative Analysis (QCA)
- Combines qualitative case knowledge with formal logic (Boolean algebra)
- Identifies necessary and sufficient conditions for outcomes
- Handles multiple causation and equifinality (multiple paths to same outcome)
- Variants: crisp-set (csQCA), fuzzy-set (fsQCA), multi-value (mvQCA)

## Frameworks for Comparison

### Structured Comparison Matrix
| Dimension | Case A | Case B | Case C |
|-----------|--------|--------|--------|
| Variable 1 | ... | ... | ... |
| Variable 2 | ... | ... | ... |
| Outcome | ... | ... | ... |

### Key Dimensions to Compare
- Context (market, geography, timing, regulatory environment)
- Inputs (resources, capabilities, strategy)
- Process (implementation, execution, adaptation)
- Outputs (results, metrics, performance)
- Mechanisms (causal pathways, mediating factors)

## Process Steps
1. **Define the research question**: What are you comparing and why?
2. **Select cases**: Use theoretical criteria, not convenience
3. **Define comparison dimensions**: What variables matter?
4. **Collect comparable data**: Same types of data across all cases
5. **Analyze within cases**: Understand each case individually first
6. **Analyze across cases**: Identify patterns, similarities, differences
7. **Interpret**: What explains the patterns? What are the mechanisms?

## Common Pitfalls
- **Selection bias**: Choosing cases that confirm pre-existing beliefs
- **Conceptual stretching**: Applying concepts beyond their valid scope
- **Galton's problem**: Cases are not independent (diffusion effects)
- **Overdetermination**: Too many variables, too few cases
- **Superficial comparison**: Listing differences without explaining why they matter

## Application to Deep Research
- Use comparison matrices to organize findings across multiple sources/cases
- Apply MSSD logic when comparing similar entities with different outcomes
- Structure competitive analysis using consistent comparison dimensions
- Compare across time periods to identify trends and shifts
- Always explain WHY differences exist, not just WHAT the differences are
- Consider multiple causal paths (equifinality) when outcomes are similar
