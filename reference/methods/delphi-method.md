# Delphi Method

## Overview
The Delphi method is a structured communication technique for forecasting and consensus-building among expert panels. Developed by the RAND Corporation in the 1950s for military forecasting, now widely used in business strategy, technology forecasting, and policy research.

## Core Process

### Round Structure
1. **Round 1**: Open-ended questions to experts; collect initial opinions/forecasts
2. **Round 2**: Summarize Round 1 responses; experts review and revise positions
3. **Round 3+**: Share statistical summary (median, IQR); outliers justify their positions
4. **Final Round**: Convergence point; document consensus and remaining disagreements

### Key Principles
- **Anonymity**: Experts do not know each other's identities during the process
- **Iteration**: Multiple rounds allow reflection and revision
- **Controlled feedback**: Statistical summaries shared between rounds
- **Statistical aggregation**: Final output is group judgment, not individual

## When to Use
- Forecasting future trends where data is scarce
- Policy development requiring diverse stakeholder input
- Technology roadmapping and scenario planning
- Establishing clinical guidelines or best practices
- When face-to-face meetings are impractical or would create groupthink

## Strengths
- Reduces bandwagon effects and groupthink through anonymity
- Structured iteration improves judgment quality over single surveys
- Allows geographically distributed expert participation
- Forces explicit reasoning, especially from outlier positions
- Produces both consensus metrics and documented dissent

## Limitations
- Time-consuming (weeks to months for multiple rounds)
- Expert selection bias determines output quality
- Attrition risk across rounds
- Consensus does not equal accuracy (experts can converge on wrong answer)
- Sensitive to question framing

## Application to Deep Research
- Use Delphi-like reasoning when synthesizing conflicting expert views
- Weight expert consensus but document dissenting positions explicitly
- Apply iterative refinement: initial broad scan, then focused investigation
- When multiple sources disagree, treat it as a "virtual Delphi" - seek convergence but report divergence
- Consider using expert panel surveys when primary research is feasible
