# Systematic Review

## Overview
A systematic review is a rigorous, transparent, and reproducible method for identifying, evaluating, and synthesizing all relevant research on a specific question. Considered the gold standard for evidence synthesis. Distinguished from narrative reviews by its explicit, pre-specified methodology.

## PRISMA Framework (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews)

### 27-Item Checklist (Key Elements)
- Title identifies the report as a systematic review
- Structured abstract with background, objectives, methods, results, conclusions
- Explicit eligibility criteria
- Information sources and search strategy (reproducible)
- Study selection process with flow diagram
- Data extraction process
- Risk of bias assessment
- Synthesis methods described
- Results of individual studies and synthesis

### PRISMA Flow Diagram
```
Records identified (N) -> Duplicates removed (N)
-> Records screened (N) -> Excluded (N)
-> Full-text assessed (N) -> Excluded with reasons (N)
-> Studies included in synthesis (N)
```

## Process Steps

### 1. Formulate the Question
- Use PICO framework: Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcome
- Or SPIDER: Sample, Phenomenon of Interest, Design, Evaluation, Research type
- Register protocol (PROSPERO for health; OSF for others)

### 2. Search Strategy
- Search minimum 3 databases relevant to the topic
- Document exact search strings, dates, and results per database
- Include grey literature search (reports, theses, conference proceedings)
- Hand-search reference lists of included studies
- Contact experts for unpublished work

### 3. Study Selection
- Two independent reviewers screen titles/abstracts
- Full-text review of potentially eligible studies
- Resolve disagreements through discussion or third reviewer
- Document reasons for exclusion at full-text stage

### 4. Data Extraction
- Standardized extraction form
- Two independent extractors with reconciliation
- Extract: study design, sample, methods, outcomes, quality indicators

### 5. Quality Assessment
- Cochrane Risk of Bias tool (for RCTs)
- Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (for observational studies)
- GRADE framework for certainty of evidence

### 6. Synthesis
- Narrative synthesis: describe patterns, similarities, differences
- Meta-analysis: if studies are sufficiently homogeneous
- Thematic synthesis: for qualitative evidence

## Types of Systematic Reviews
- **Quantitative**: Includes meta-analysis of numerical outcomes
- **Qualitative**: Synthesizes findings from qualitative studies
- **Mixed-methods**: Combines quantitative and qualitative evidence
- **Scoping review**: Maps extent of evidence (less rigorous, broader scope)
- **Rapid review**: Streamlined for time-sensitive decisions

## Application to Deep Research
- Apply systematic review principles to evidence gathering even informally
- Document your search strategy for transparency
- Use multiple sources to reduce selection bias
- Assess quality of each source, not just its conclusions
- Create evidence tables summarizing key findings across sources
- Report both supporting and contradicting evidence
