# Case Study Method

## Overview
The case study method is an in-depth investigation of a contemporary phenomenon within its real-world context. Particularly useful when boundaries between phenomenon and context are not clearly evident. Robert Yin's framework is the most widely cited methodology.

## When to Use Case Studies
- Research questions focus on "how" or "why"
- Researcher has little control over events
- Focus is on contemporary phenomena in real-world context
- Need to understand complex social phenomena holistically
- Theory building in new or understudied domains

## Types of Case Studies

### By Purpose
- **Exploratory**: Preliminary investigation to define questions and hypotheses
- **Descriptive**: Complete description of a phenomenon within its context
- **Explanatory**: Explaining causal links in complex real-world interventions

### By Design
- **Single case**: Critical, extreme, representative, revelatory, or longitudinal case
- **Multiple case**: Replication logic (literal or theoretical replication across cases)
- **Embedded**: Multiple units of analysis within a single case
- **Holistic**: Single unit of analysis per case

## Yin's Five Components of Research Design
1. **Study question**: What "how" or "why" question is being addressed?
2. **Propositions**: What theoretical propositions guide data collection?
3. **Unit of analysis**: What is the "case"? (person, organization, event, etc.)
4. **Logic linking data to propositions**: Pattern matching, explanation building, etc.
5. **Criteria for interpreting findings**: Rival explanations, significance thresholds

## Data Collection
- **Documentation**: Reports, articles, administrative records
- **Archival records**: Service records, organizational data, survey data
- **Interviews**: Open-ended, focused, or structured
- **Direct observation**: Field visits, meeting attendance
- **Participant observation**: Researcher takes active role
- **Physical artifacts**: Tools, instruments, devices

### Principles of Data Collection
- Use multiple sources of evidence (triangulation)
- Create a case study database (organized repository)
- Maintain a chain of evidence (traceability from question to conclusion)

## Quality Criteria
- **Construct validity**: Multiple sources, chain of evidence, member checking
- **Internal validity**: Pattern matching, explanation building, rival explanations
- **External validity**: Replication logic (not statistical generalization)
- **Reliability**: Case study protocol, case study database

## Analysis Strategies
- **Pattern matching**: Compare empirical patterns with predicted patterns
- **Explanation building**: Iterative narrative explaining the case
- **Time-series analysis**: Tracing events over time
- **Logic models**: Matching observed events to theoretically predicted sequences
- **Cross-case synthesis**: Comparing patterns across multiple cases

## Application to Deep Research
- Use case study logic when investigating specific companies, products, or events
- Triangulate across multiple data sources for every key finding
- Maintain clear chain of evidence from data to conclusions
- Consider rival explanations for observed patterns
- Use multiple cases for stronger external validity
- Document methodology transparently for credibility
