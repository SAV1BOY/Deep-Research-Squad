# Evidence-Based Practice Papers

## Foundations of EBP

### "Evidence-Based Medicine: What It Is and What It Isn't" - Sackett et al. (1996)
- **Citation**: Sackett, D.L. et al. (1996). *BMJ*, 312(7023), 71-72
- Defined EBP as integrating best evidence with clinical expertise and patient values
- NOT cookbook medicine; requires judgment
- **Key takeaway**: Evidence informs but does not replace expert judgment

### "Levels of Evidence" - Oxford Centre for Evidence-Based Medicine
- Level 1: Systematic reviews of RCTs
- Level 2: Individual RCTs or observational studies with dramatic effect
- Level 3: Non-randomized controlled studies
- Level 4: Case series, case-control studies
- Level 5: Expert opinion without critical appraisal
- **Key takeaway**: Not all evidence is equal; use the hierarchy to weight sources

### "Evidence-Based Management" - Rousseau (2006)
- **Citation**: Rousseau, D.M. (2006). *Academy of Management Review*, 31(2), 256-269
- Translating EBP principles to management and business decisions
- Evaluating consulting claims and business research rigorously
- **Key takeaway**: Business decisions should follow same evidence standards

## Critical Appraisal

### "Users' Guides to the Medical Literature" - Guyatt et al. (JAMA series)
- Systematic framework for evaluating research quality
- Questions: Is the study valid? What are the results? Are results applicable?
- **Key takeaway**: Every study should be appraised before being used

### "GRADE System" - Guyatt et al. (2008)
- **Citation**: Guyatt, G.H. et al. (2008). *BMJ*, 336(7650), 924-926
- Rates certainty of evidence: High, Moderate, Low, Very Low
- Considers: Risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, publication bias
- **Key takeaway**: Structured approach to rating evidence quality

### "Realistic Evaluation" - Pawson & Tilley (1997)
- Context-Mechanism-Outcome (CMO) configurations
- Understanding why interventions work in specific contexts
- **Key takeaway**: Context is critical; what works somewhere may not work everywhere

## Evidence Synthesis Methods

### "Systematic Reviews in Social Sciences" - Petticrew & Roberts (2006)
- Adapted systematic review methods beyond medicine
- Structured evidence synthesis for business/policy questions
- **Key takeaway**: Systematic review principles apply beyond healthcare

### "Scoping Reviews" - Arksey & O'Malley (2005)
- Map the extent and nature of evidence on a topic
- Less rigorous than systematic reviews but broader in scope
- Good for: identifying gaps, clarifying concepts, examining methodologies
- **Key takeaway**: Use scoping reviews to map evidence before deep diving

### "Rapid Reviews" - Khangura et al. (2012)
- Streamlined systematic review for time-sensitive decisions
- Trade-offs: speed vs comprehensiveness
- **Key takeaway**: Adapt review rigor to the decision timeline

### "Realist Reviews" - Pawson et al. (2005)
- Theory-driven synthesis: What works, for whom, in what context?
- Focus on causal mechanisms, not just outcomes
- **Key takeaway**: Context matters; what works somewhere may not work everywhere

## Practical EBP Checklist for Research
- [ ] Is the evidence from peer-reviewed or audited sources?
- [ ] What is the sample size and study design?
- [ ] Where does this sit on the evidence hierarchy?
- [ ] Are there conflicting findings? If so, why?
- [ ] Is the context transferable to the research question?
- [ ] What is the recency of the evidence?
- [ ] Are there known biases in the source or methodology?
- [ ] Has the finding been replicated independently?

## Application to Deep Research
- Use the evidence hierarchy to weight different source types
- Apply GRADE-like criteria to assess certainty of research findings
- Conduct scoping reviews before deep dives to map the evidence landscape
- Use rapid review methods when timelines are tight
- Always appraise evidence quality before incorporating into analysis
- Consider context when translating evidence from one setting to another
- Recognize that expert opinion is the lowest level of evidence
