# Cognitive Biases for Researchers

## Overview
Cognitive biases are systematic deviations from rational judgment that affect how we process information, form beliefs, and make decisions. Researchers are not immune - awareness of biases is essential for producing rigorous, reliable research outputs.

## Bias Categories Relevant to Research

### Information Gathering Biases
- **Confirmation bias**: Seeking/favoring information that confirms existing beliefs
- **Availability heuristic**: Overweighting easily recalled information
- **Anchoring**: Over-relying on the first piece of information encountered
- **Selection bias**: Systematically excluding certain types of evidence
- **Recency bias**: Overweighting recent information over historical patterns

### Information Processing Biases
- **Narrative fallacy**: Creating coherent stories from random or unconnected data
- **Base rate neglect**: Ignoring statistical base rates when evaluating evidence
- **Framing effect**: Conclusions change based on how information is presented
- **Conjunction fallacy**: Judging specific scenarios as more probable than general ones
- **Representativeness**: Judging probability by similarity rather than statistics

### Social and Authority Biases
- **Authority bias**: Overweighting claims from prestigious sources
- **Groupthink**: Conforming to group consensus at expense of critical evaluation
- **Bandwagon effect**: Adopting beliefs because many others hold them
- **Halo effect**: Positive impression in one area influences judgment in others

### Output and Reporting Biases
- **Survivorship bias**: Focusing on successes while ignoring failures
- **Hindsight bias**: Believing past events were predictable after knowing outcomes
- **Dunning-Kruger effect**: Overestimating competence in unfamiliar domains
- **Overconfidence**: Excessive certainty in conclusions and predictions

## Debiasing Strategies for Research Agents

### Pre-Search Phase
- Formulate research questions before searching (avoid confirmation bias)
- Document initial hypotheses explicitly so they can be tested
- Define what evidence would DISPROVE your hypothesis (falsification)

### During Research
- Actively search for contradicting evidence (steel-man opposing views)
- Use structured search strategies to avoid availability bias
- Note the base rates before evaluating specific cases
- Consider multiple framings of the same information

### Synthesis Phase
- Use pre-defined frameworks to avoid narrative fallacy
- Include a "What could go wrong" section in every analysis
- Seek out failure cases, not just successes (counter survivorship bias)
- Assign confidence levels to claims (counter overconfidence)

### Review Phase
- Red-team your own conclusions
- Check: Would I draw the same conclusion if the source were less prestigious?
- Ask: What am I NOT seeing? What data is missing?
- Review with fresh eyes after a break
