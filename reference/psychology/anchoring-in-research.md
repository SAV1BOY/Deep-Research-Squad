# Anchoring Effects in Research

## Definition
Anchoring is the cognitive bias where initial information disproportionately influences subsequent judgments and decisions. The first number, estimate, or piece of information encountered serves as an "anchor" that subsequent adjustments fail to adequately correct.

## How Anchoring Works

### Insufficient Adjustment
- Start from an anchor value and adjust up or down
- Adjustments are almost always insufficient
- Even random, irrelevant anchors affect estimates
- Higher anchors produce higher estimates; lower anchors produce lower estimates

### Selective Accessibility
- Anchor activates related information in memory
- This makes anchor-consistent information more accessible
- Subsequent judgment is biased toward the anchor

## Anchoring in Research Contexts

### Market Sizing
- First market size estimate encountered becomes the anchor
- All subsequent estimates are evaluated relative to it
- A high initial estimate makes the market seem "smaller than expected" even if objectively large
- **Counter**: Generate your own estimate before reviewing existing ones

### Valuation Analysis
- Anchor on previous round valuation, comparable transactions, or asking price
- Independent valuation becomes contaminated by known reference points
- **Counter**: Use multiple independent valuation methodologies

### Risk Assessment
- Anchor on the most salient risk or the first risk identified
- Subsequent risks are judged relative to the anchor risk
- **Counter**: Use structured risk frameworks; assess each risk independently

### Forecasting
- Anchor on current values or recent trends when projecting future
- Status quo bias: future estimates cluster around present values
- **Counter**: Use base rates and reference class forecasting

## Research on Anchoring Strength
- Anchoring persists even when the anchor is obviously irrelevant
- Expertise reduces but does not eliminate anchoring
- Anchoring effects are remarkably robust across contexts
- Financial incentives for accuracy do not eliminate anchoring
- Being warned about anchoring does not eliminate it

## Debiasing Strategies

### Before Analysis
1. Generate your own estimate before reviewing any existing data
2. Use multiple independent starting points
3. Consider the opposite: "What if the true value is very different?"

### During Analysis
4. Use structured frameworks that force consideration of multiple factors
5. Decompose estimates into sub-components (Fermi estimation)
6. Seek disconfirming evidence for anchor-consistent conclusions

### Quality Checks
7. Test sensitivity: How much does the conclusion change with different starting assumptions?
8. Have multiple analysts independently estimate before sharing
9. Use formal calibration exercises to improve estimation accuracy

## Application to Deep Research
- Be aware that the order of evidence presentation matters
- Generate independent estimates before reviewing source material
- Use Fermi decomposition to build estimates from components
- When presenting findings, acknowledge what anchors may be influencing conclusions
- In competitive analysis, avoid anchoring on a single competitor's metrics
- Rotate the order of evidence review to reduce first-evidence anchoring
