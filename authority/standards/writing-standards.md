# Writing Standards

## Purpose

These standards govern how research findings are communicated. Clear writing
is not optional — it is an ethical obligation. If the reader misunderstands
our research because of poor writing, the research has failed regardless
of its analytical quality.

## Core Principles

### 1. Answer First (BLUF)
- Lead with the conclusion, not the background
- First sentence of any deliverable should answer the question
- Supporting detail follows the answer, not precedes it
- The reader should know your conclusion within 30 seconds

### 2. One Idea Per Unit
- One idea per sentence
- One theme per paragraph
- One finding per section
- One story per chart or table

### 3. Active Voice
- "Revenue grew 23%" not "A 23% growth in revenue was observed"
- "Three sources confirm this" not "This was confirmed by three sources"
- "We recommend" not "It is recommended"
- Passive voice is acceptable only for emphasis on the object

### 4. Specific Over Vague
- "Revenue grew 23% to $142M" not "Revenue grew significantly"
- "3 of 5 analysts predict growth" not "Most analysts predict growth"
- "Decision needed by March 30" not "Decision needed soon"
- Quantify everything that can be quantified

### 5. Short Over Long
- Sentences: Under 25 words preferred, 30 words maximum
- Paragraphs: 3-4 sentences maximum
- Sections: Scannable with headers and bullets
- Total length: Shortest length that covers all required content

## Formatting Standards

### Headers
- Use descriptive headers that convey content (not "Section 3")
- Headers should be scannable — reader gets the story from headers alone

### Bullets and Lists
- Use bullets for lists of 3+ items
- Each bullet is a complete thought
- Parallel structure across all bullets in a list
- No more than 7 items per list (cognitive limit)

### Tables
- Title states the takeaway, not the topic
- Column headers are clear and unambiguous
- Units specified in headers, not repeated in cells
- Sorted by most relevant criterion

### Numbers
- Round appropriately (not $142,347,891 when $142M suffices)
- Use consistent units throughout a document
- Provide context for numbers ("23% growth, vs 12% industry average")

## Prohibited Patterns

- **Throat-clearing**: Opening with background before getting to the point
- **Weasel words**: "Some experts say," "It is widely believed"
- **Jargon without definition**: Assume smart non-specialist audience
- **Passive hedging**: "It could potentially be argued that perhaps..."
- **False precision**: "Market will reach $14,237M" from rough estimates
- **Zombie nouns**: "Implementation" instead of "implement"

## Quality Test

Before delivery, apply this test:
1. Can a smart non-specialist understand every sentence?
2. Does the reader know the answer within 30 seconds?
3. Could any sentence be deleted without losing meaning?
4. Are all claims specific and quantified where possible?
5. Read it aloud — does any sentence require re-reading?
