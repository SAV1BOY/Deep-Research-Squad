# Source Hierarchy

## Purpose

This standard defines how sources are ranked, weighted, and used in
research. Not all sources are equal. The hierarchy ensures that evidence
from stronger sources carries more weight in analysis and conclusions.

## Source Tiers

### Tier 1: Primary / Highest Reliability
Sources with legal liability, peer review, or direct observation.

- **Peer-reviewed research**: Published in reputable journals, replicated
- **Legal filings**: SEC filings, court documents, patent applications
- **Official statistics**: Government statistical agencies (BLS, Census, Eurostat)
- **Audited financial statements**: Annual reports with auditor opinion
- **Direct observation/measurement**: First-hand verified data

**Weight in analysis**: Full weight. Can anchor findings independently.

### Tier 2: Institutional / High Reliability
Sources from credible institutions with editorial standards.

- **Major research institutions**: RAND, Brookings, major universities
- **Quality journalism**: FT, Reuters, Bloomberg, NYT investigations
- **Industry analysts**: Gartner, Forrester, IDC (with methodology caveats)
- **Central bank publications**: Fed, ECB, Bank of England reports
- **International organizations**: IMF, World Bank, OECD data

**Weight in analysis**: High weight. Strong supporting evidence.

### Tier 3: Professional / Moderate Reliability
Sources from professionals with domain expertise but potential biases.

- **Consulting firm reports**: McKinsey, BCG, Bain (note: may promote services)
- **Company earnings calls**: Management commentary under securities law
- **Think tank publications**: Policy-oriented, may have ideological lean
- **Conference presentations**: Expert views, limited peer review
- **Pre-print papers**: Not yet peer-reviewed but from credible researchers

**Weight in analysis**: Moderate weight. Good for triangulation.

### Tier 4: Informed Opinion / Lower Reliability
Sources from knowledgeable individuals without institutional backing.

- **Expert blogs and substacks**: Individual analysis, no editorial review
- **Podcast interviews**: Real-time expert commentary, may be imprecise
- **Social media from experts**: Informal, unedited, but potentially insightful
- **Industry surveys**: Self-reported data, methodology may be unclear

**Weight in analysis**: Supporting weight only. Cannot anchor findings.

### Tier 5: Unverified / Lowest Reliability
Sources with significant reliability concerns.

- **Anonymous reports**: No accountability for accuracy
- **Company marketing materials**: Promotional intent
- **Unattributed claims**: "Industry sources say..."
- **Wikipedia** (without checking citations): Editable by anyone
- **AI-generated content**: May contain hallucinations

**Weight in analysis**: Flag only. Never use as sole evidence.

## Application Rules

1. Major findings require at least one Tier 1 or Tier 2 source
2. Claims supported only by Tier 4-5 sources get confidence penalty
3. Source tier must be noted in evidence tables
4. When sources across tiers disagree, investigate why before defaulting to higher tier
5. A Tier 3 source with transparent methodology may outweigh a Tier 2 source without it

## Special Cases

- **Leaked documents**: May be highly reliable content but unverifiable provenance — treat as Tier 3 with caveats
- **Government data from authoritarian regimes**: Official but potentially manipulated — treat as Tier 3
- **Industry-funded research**: Tier depends on independence of methodology, not funder prestige
