# Decision Translation Standards

## Purpose

Research that doesn't connect to decisions is academic exercise. These
standards ensure every research deliverable is translated into terms
that enable concrete decisions and actions.

## Core Principle

Every piece of research exists to help someone decide or act.
If you can't identify the decision, question whether the research is needed.

## Translation Framework

### Step 1: Identify the Decision
Before writing, answer:
- What specific decision does this research inform?
- Who is the decision-maker?
- When must the decision be made?
- What are the options being considered?
- What would change the decision-maker's mind?

### Step 2: Frame Findings as Decision Inputs
Transform research findings into decision language:

**Research language** → **Decision language**
- "Market is growing at 18% CAGR" → "Window to enter is 12-18 months before saturation"
- "Competitor raised Series C" → "Competitive intensity increases; speed matters more"
- "Regulation expected Q3" → "Compliance cost: $X; delay cost of non-compliance: $Y"
- "Evidence is mixed" → "Decision under uncertainty; here are hedging options"

### Step 3: Provide Decision Architecture
Structure the output for decision-making:

1. **The question**: What must be decided
2. **The options**: What are the choices (always 2+)
3. **The evidence**: What we know about each option
4. **The recommendation**: What we suggest and why
5. **The risks**: What could go wrong with each option
6. **The triggers**: What would change our recommendation

### Step 4: Specify Reversibility
For each option or recommendation:
- **Easily reversible**: Low-cost to undo. Bias toward action.
- **Partially reversible**: Some sunk costs. Moderate caution.
- **Irreversible**: Cannot undo. Requires high confidence evidence.

### Step 5: Define Next Actions
Every deliverable ends with concrete next steps:
- Who does what by when
- What additional information is needed
- When to revisit the decision
- What triggers require immediate reassessment

## Decision Translation Checklist

- [ ] Decision identified and stated explicitly
- [ ] Decision-maker identified
- [ ] Timeline for decision stated
- [ ] Options presented (minimum 2)
- [ ] Evidence mapped to options (not just topics)
- [ ] Recommendation provided with confidence level
- [ ] Risks stated for recommended option
- [ ] Reversibility assessed
- [ ] Next actions specified with owners and dates
- [ ] Triggers for reassessment defined

## Anti-Patterns

- **Research for research's sake**: No decision identified
- **Options without recommendation**: Passing the buck
- **Recommendation without evidence link**: Ungrounded opinion
- **Ignoring reversibility**: Treating all decisions as equal weight
- **No next steps**: Insight without action path
- **Open-ended timeline**: "Consider this when appropriate"

## Integration with Deliverable Types

| Deliverable Type | Decision Translation Focus |
|-----------------|--------------------------|
| Executive brief | Decision + recommendation in BLUF |
| Deep dive | Decision architecture in final section |
| Market map | Strategic options derived from landscape |
| Competitor analysis | Competitive response options |
| Due diligence | Go/no-go recommendation |
| Scenario memo | Robust and contingent strategies |
