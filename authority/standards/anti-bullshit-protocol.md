# Anti-Bullshit Protocol

## Purpose

This protocol exists to catch and eliminate bullshit from research outputs.
Bullshit, as defined by philosopher Harry Frankfurt, is speech that is
indifferent to truth — not lying (knowing the truth and hiding it) but
not caring whether statements are true or false.

## What Counts as Bullshit in Research

### 1. Weasel Claims
Statements that sound informative but say nothing verifiable.

**Bullshit**: "The market is expected to experience significant growth"
**Not bullshit**: "The market grew 18% in 2024 and three analyst firms
project 15-22% growth in 2025 (confidence: 72)"

### 2. Ungrounded Authority
Invoking authority without evidence.

**Bullshit**: "Industry leaders agree that AI will transform healthcare"
**Not bullshit**: "12 of 15 healthcare CIOs surveyed by HIMSS (n=340)
plan AI deployments by 2026"

### 3. False Precision
Precise numbers from imprecise sources.

**Bullshit**: "The addressable market is $47.3B"
**Not bullshit**: "Market estimates range from $35B-$55B depending on
scope definition. Our triangulated estimate: $45B (±20%)"

### 4. Confidence Theater
Appearing certain when evidence is weak.

**Bullshit**: "Our analysis clearly shows that..."
**Not bullshit**: "Based on 4 sources with moderate agreement,
we assess that... (confidence: 62)"

### 5. Complexity Hiding
Using jargon or complex structures to obscure weak reasoning.

**Bullshit**: "The synergistic value creation potential of this strategic
paradigm shift leverages core competencies..."
**Not bullshit**: "This acquisition would add $12M revenue and reduce
customer acquisition cost by 15%"

## The BS Detection Checklist

Run on every deliverable before delivery:

- [ ] **The "Specifically" Test**: Can you replace every vague claim with a specific one?
- [ ] **The "Says Who" Test**: Does every claim have an identified source?
- [ ] **The "So What" Test**: Does every paragraph answer "why does this matter?"
- [ ] **The "How Do You Know" Test**: Is the evidence chain transparent?
- [ ] **The "What Would Change Your Mind" Test**: Is the claim falsifiable?
- [ ] **The "Grandparent Test**: Could a smart non-expert understand this?
- [ ] **The "Delete Test**: If you delete this sentence, is anything lost?

## BS Severity Levels

### Level 1: Lazy Language (Fixable)
Vague words that should be specific. Fix: Replace with data.

### Level 2: Missing Evidence (Concerning)
Claims without sources. Fix: Add evidence or remove claim.

### Level 3: Misleading Framing (Serious)
Evidence presented in a way that distorts meaning. Fix: Reframe honestly.

### Level 4: Fabricated Confidence (Critical)
Presenting speculation as established fact. Fix: Recalibrate confidence
and rewrite with appropriate hedging.

## Zero Tolerance Items

These are never acceptable in any deliverable:
- Citing sources that don't exist
- Presenting AI-generated content as human-verified findings
- Suppressing contradicting evidence
- Claiming consensus where none exists
- Providing recommendations not supported by the evidence presented

## Cultural Standard

It is better to say "We don't know" than to fill the gap with bullshit.
Saying "I don't know" with confidence is a sign of strength, not weakness.
The squad's credibility depends on being reliably honest, including about
the limits of our knowledge.
