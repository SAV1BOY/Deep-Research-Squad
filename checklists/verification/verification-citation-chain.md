# Verification Citation Chain Check

## Purpose

Trace the citation chain from a claim back through its references to the
original source. Catches misquotation, misattribution, and degraded accuracy
through successive retellings.

## Gate Question

**Has the citation chain been traced from claim to original source?**

## Prerequisites

- Claims with their citations identified.
- Access to cited sources and their references.
- Priority list of claims to trace (at minimum, all critical claims).
- Citation chain tracking template ready.

## Pass Criteria

1. At least all critical claims have had their citation chains traced
   to the primary source.
2. Each link in the chain accurately represents the previous link's
   content.
3. No broken links in the chain (missing or inaccessible intermediate
   sources).
4. The original source actually states what the citing sources claim
   it states.
5. Context of the original statement is preserved (not quote-mined).
6. Any transformation of meaning through the chain is documented.
7. Citation chains of 4+ links are flagged as high-risk for distortion.

## Fail Actions

- If a chain cannot be traced to the primary: note the gap and assess
  the risk of distortion.
- If misquotation is found: correct the claim to match the original.
- If context was lost: restore the original context in the claim.
- If a chain link is broken: attempt alternative access (archives,
  libraries, author contact).
- If meaning transformed through the chain: use the primary source's
  original statement.

## Escalation Rules

- Escalate if tracing reveals that a widely cited claim is actually
  misattributed or fabricated.
- Escalate if primary sources are behind access barriers that cannot
  be overcome.
- Escalate if citation chain analysis reveals systemic issues with a
  source that other research also relies on.
