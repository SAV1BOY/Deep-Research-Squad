# Citation Traceability Gate

## Purpose

This gate ensures that every claim, statistic, and factual assertion in the
research output can be traced back to its original source. Without full
traceability, readers cannot verify claims, errors cannot be identified, and
the research loses its evidentiary foundation. This gate enforces a chain
of custody from source to final output for every substantive claim.

## Gate Question

**Can every claim be traced to its source?**

Each factual claim, data point, statistic, and attributed opinion in the
research output must link to a specific, retrievable source. The link must
be direct and verifiable, not vague or implied.

## Pass Criteria

1. Every factual claim in the output has an explicit citation to a specific source.
2. Citations include sufficient detail to locate the exact passage or data point referenced.
3. Statistics and numerical data cite the original data source, not secondary reports.
4. Paraphrased content is accurately represented and not distorted from the original meaning.
5. Direct quotes are verbatim and clearly marked with quotation formatting.
6. Sources are retrievable and accessible for verification by the reader.
7. Citation format is consistent throughout the document.
8. Claims attributed to experts cite the specific statement, publication, or interview.
9. Synthesized conclusions clearly distinguish between sourced evidence and researcher inference.
10. No orphan claims exist that lack any supporting citation.
11. Secondary citations (citing a source that cites another source) are flagged and primary sources sought.
12. The citation index or bibliography is complete with no missing entries.
13. Date of access is recorded for web-based and dynamic sources.

## Fail Actions

- If orphan claims are found, either add citations or remove the claims from the output.
- If citations lack specificity, add page numbers, section references, or timestamps.
- If secondary citations cannot be traced to primary sources, flag the limitation explicitly.
- If paraphrased content is inaccurate, correct it to match the source or re-cite.
- If sources are no longer accessible, note the access date and seek archived versions.
- If citation format is inconsistent, standardize before final output.
- If synthesized conclusions are not distinguished from sourced claims, add clear labeling.
- Run a final citation audit matching every claim to its source before release.
- Do not release research output until full traceability is confirmed.
