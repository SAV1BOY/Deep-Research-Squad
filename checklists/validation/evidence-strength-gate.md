# Evidence Strength Gate

## Purpose

This gate ensures that the evidence supporting each conclusion is strong
enough to justify the confidence level assigned to it. Weak evidence
presented as strong leads to overconfident recommendations and poor
decision-making. This gate enforces a rigorous mapping between evidence
quality and conclusion confidence throughout the research output.

## Gate Question

**Is evidence strong enough for conclusions?**

Every conclusion, claim, and recommendation must be supported by evidence
whose strength matches the confidence level expressed. Where evidence is
weak, conclusions must be appropriately hedged or flagged as preliminary.

## Pass Criteria

1. Each conclusion is linked to specific, identifiable pieces of evidence.
2. Evidence strength is rated on a defined scale (e.g., strong, moderate, weak, anecdotal).
3. The confidence level of each conclusion matches the strength of its supporting evidence.
4. Multiple independent sources corroborate key findings where possible.
5. The distinction between correlation and causation is maintained in all causal claims.
6. Sample sizes and statistical significance are evaluated for quantitative evidence.
7. Expert consensus versus minority opinion is identified and labeled.
8. Anecdotal evidence is clearly marked and not used as a sole basis for conclusions.
9. Evidence gaps are documented with their potential impact on conclusions.
10. The evidence base for each conclusion is resilient to the removal of any single source.
11. Temporal validity of evidence is confirmed relative to the research question.
12. Contradictory evidence has been weighed and accounted for in the conclusion.
13. No conclusion exceeds the evidential warrant of its supporting data.

## Fail Actions

- If a conclusion lacks linked evidence, either find supporting evidence or remove the conclusion.
- If confidence exceeds evidence strength, downgrade confidence and add appropriate hedging.
- If only a single source supports a key conclusion, seek corroboration or flag the risk.
- If causal claims lack causal evidence, reframe as correlational or hypothetical.
- If evidence gaps threaten core conclusions, document the risk and propose further research.
- If anecdotal evidence is treated as definitive, reclassify and adjust conclusions.
- If contradictory evidence is unaddressed, conduct explicit resolution analysis.
- Update the evidence-to-conclusion mapping table before finalizing any output.
- Do not present conclusions as final until evidence strength has been validated.
