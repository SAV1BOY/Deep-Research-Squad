# Thesis Testing Quality Checklist

## Purpose
Ensure that thesis testing research rigorously evaluates hypotheses with appropriate evidence, avoids confirmation bias, and produces clear verdicts on whether a thesis is supported, refuted, or inconclusive. This checklist upholds the intellectual honesty required for evidence-based decision-making.

## When to Use
- Before delivering thesis testing results or hypothesis evaluation reports
- When reviewing research designed to validate or invalidate a strategic thesis
- During assessment of investment theses, product hypotheses, or strategic assumptions
- When a go/no-go decision depends on thesis validation outcomes

## Prerequisites
- Thesis statement is clearly articulated in testable terms
- Success and failure criteria are defined before testing begins
- Evidence requirements and sources have been identified
- Bias mitigation measures are in place (devil's advocate, red team review)
- Stakeholders understand that the goal is truth-seeking, not thesis confirmation

## Checklist Items

### Section 1: Thesis Formulation
1. [ ] Thesis is stated as a clear, falsifiable proposition — PASS: specific and testable; FAIL: vague or unfalsifiable
2. [ ] Key terms in the thesis are operationally defined — PASS: definitions provided; FAIL: ambiguous terminology
3. [ ] Success criteria are quantified where possible — PASS: measurable thresholds; FAIL: subjective success criteria
4. [ ] Failure criteria are explicitly stated alongside success criteria — PASS: both defined; FAIL: only success criteria specified
5. [ ] The thesis is broken into sub-hypotheses where appropriate — PASS: decomposition done for complex theses; FAIL: monolithic thesis when decomposition would improve testability

### Section 2: Evidence Collection
6. [ ] Evidence is gathered from multiple independent sources — PASS: 5+ independent sources; FAIL: fewer than 5 or non-independent sources
7. [ ] Both supporting and contradicting evidence is actively sought — PASS: balanced evidence search; FAIL: only confirming evidence gathered
8. [ ] Evidence types include quantitative and qualitative data — PASS: mixed methods; FAIL: single evidence type
9. [ ] Source quality has been assessed for all evidence — PASS: quality ratings applied; FAIL: evidence accepted without quality review
10. [ ] Evidence collection methodology is documented and reproducible — PASS: methodology transparent; FAIL: ad hoc collection

### Section 3: Bias Mitigation
11. [ ] Confirmation bias countermeasures are documented and applied — PASS: specific bias controls in place; FAIL: no bias mitigation
12. [ ] A devil's advocate or red team review has been conducted — PASS: adversarial review completed; FAIL: no adversarial challenge
13. [ ] Disconfirming evidence is given equal weight to confirming evidence — PASS: balanced treatment; FAIL: disconfirming evidence minimized
14. [ ] The researcher's prior beliefs or expectations are disclosed — PASS: priors stated; FAIL: undisclosed bias potential
15. [ ] Selection bias in evidence is addressed — PASS: selection methodology prevents cherry-picking; FAIL: evidence appears selectively chosen

### Section 4: Analysis and Verdict
16. [ ] Evidence is systematically evaluated against success and failure criteria — PASS: structured evaluation; FAIL: impressionistic assessment
17. [ ] Strength of evidence is rated for each piece (strong, moderate, weak) — PASS: evidence strength assessed; FAIL: all evidence treated equally
18. [ ] Verdict is one of: supported, refuted, partially supported, or inconclusive — PASS: clear categorical verdict; FAIL: ambiguous conclusion
19. [ ] Confidence level for the verdict is stated with justification — PASS: confidence rated and explained; FAIL: verdict without confidence assessment
20. [ ] Alternative explanations for the evidence are considered — PASS: alternatives explored; FAIL: single interpretation assumed

### Section 5: Reporting and Implications
21. [ ] Evidence table maps each piece of evidence to the thesis — PASS: structured evidence mapping; FAIL: narrative without systematic mapping
22. [ ] The strongest arguments against the verdict are presented — PASS: steelman of opposing view; FAIL: strawman or no opposing view
23. [ ] Implications of the verdict for the decision at hand are stated — PASS: practical implications drawn; FAIL: academic conclusion without application
24. [ ] Conditions that would change the verdict are identified — PASS: conditions for reassessment noted; FAIL: verdict presented as permanent
25. [ ] Recommendations for follow-up research or monitoring are provided — PASS: next steps outlined; FAIL: no forward-looking guidance

## Scoring
- Count the total number of items marked PASS
- Quality Score = (PASS count / 25) x 100
- Grade thresholds:
  - 92-100%: Excellent — rigorous and intellectually honest thesis testing
  - 80-91%: Good — sound methodology with minor gaps
  - 68-79%: Acceptable — bias or rigor concerns that should be addressed
  - Below 68%: Insufficient — thesis testing lacks credibility

## Escalation
- **Accept**: Score of 80% or above with no FAIL on items 1, 4, 11, or 18
- **Revise**: Score of 68-79%, or any FAIL on items 1, 4, 11, or 18
- **Escalate to lead**: Score below 68%, or evidence of systemic confirmation bias, or methodology fundamentally flawed
- **Escalate to stakeholder**: Thesis testing reveals that the underlying strategic question is malformed, or verdict contradicts strong stakeholder expectations and requires careful communication
