# Evidence Completeness Gate

## Purpose

Determine whether sufficient evidence has been gathered to draw defensible
conclusions. Premature conclusions on incomplete evidence are a common
research failure mode.

## Gate Question

**Is the evidence sufficient to support a conclusion?**

## Prerequisites

- Evidence collection phase substantially complete.
- Sub-questions mapped to their evidence sets.
- Minimum evidence thresholds defined per claim importance.
- Evidence strength ratings assigned.

## Pass Criteria

1. Every sub-question has at least the minimum required evidence items
   (critical: 5+, standard: 3+, minor: 1+).
2. No critical claim rests on a single piece of evidence.
3. Both supporting and challenging evidence has been sought for each
   major claim.
4. Evidence covers all relevant dimensions of the claim (who, what,
   when, where, why, how -- as applicable).
5. Saturation point indicators are present (new searches return
   diminishing new information).
6. Known evidence gaps are listed and their impact assessed.
7. The evidence base is judged sufficient to meet the target confidence
   level.

## Fail Actions

- If a critical claim has fewer than 5 evidence items: continue searching.
- If only supporting evidence exists: conduct disconfirming searches.
- If evidence gaps are unassessed: evaluate impact of each gap on the
  conclusion.
- If saturation is not reached and time allows: continue collection.
- If evidence is insufficient for the target confidence: lower the
  confidence level and disclose.

## Escalation Rules

- Escalate if evidence is insufficient for any confident conclusion and
  the requester needs a definitive answer.
- Escalate if critical evidence is known to exist but is inaccessible.
- Escalate if the evidence gap suggests the original scope was too broad
  for the available resources.
