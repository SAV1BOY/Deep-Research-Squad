# Evidence Falsifiability Gate

## Purpose

Verify that the research hypotheses and claims are falsifiable -- that is,
there exists evidence that could, in principle, disprove them. Non-falsifiable
claims are not researchable and should be flagged.

## Gate Question

**Is the hypothesis or claim falsifiable with obtainable evidence?**

## Prerequisites

- Key hypotheses and claims identified.
- Contrarian Agent has proposed counter-arguments.
- Evidence collection plan includes disconfirming as well as confirming
  searches.
- Domain norms for falsifiability understood.

## Pass Criteria

1. Each major claim can be stated in a form that specifies what evidence
   would disprove it.
2. The research plan includes explicit searches for disconfirming evidence.
3. At least one disconfirming search was executed per critical claim.
4. Claims that survived disconfirmation attempts are noted as more robust.
5. Claims that are inherently unfalsifiable (e.g., value judgments) are
   labeled as such and separated from empirical findings.
6. The absence of disconfirming evidence is not treated as proof of the
   claim (noted explicitly).
7. Falsifiability assessment is documented per claim.

## Fail Actions

- If a claim has no falsification criteria: reformulate it into a
  testable form or label it as opinion/value judgment.
- If no disconfirming searches were run: execute at least one before
  finalizing.
- If unfalsifiable claims are mixed with empirical ones: separate them
  clearly in the output.
- If absence of disconfirmation is presented as proof: correct the
  language to reflect the limitation.

## Escalation Rules

- Escalate if a central claim is unfalsifiable and the research question
  depends on it.
- Escalate if the domain makes falsifiability difficult (e.g., predictions
  about the far future) and alternative quality criteria are needed.
- Escalate if disconfirming evidence was found that overturns a previously
  high-confidence claim.
