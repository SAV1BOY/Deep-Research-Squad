# Evidence Traceability Gate

## Purpose

Ensure every claim in the research output can be traced back to a specific
piece of evidence with a retrievable source. Untraceable claims undermine
the credibility and auditability of the entire research product.

## Gate Question

**Does each claim point to specific, retrievable proof?**

## Prerequisites

- All claims extracted and numbered.
- Evidence inventory compiled with source references.
- Citation format agreed upon.
- Source access verified (links live, documents available).

## Pass Criteria

1. Every factual claim has at least one citation pointing to a specific
   source, page, section, or data point.
2. Citations use a consistent format throughout the output.
3. Cited sources are retrievable (URLs work, documents are accessible).
4. Claims derived from synthesis of multiple sources cite all contributing
   sources.
5. Paraphrased claims accurately reflect the original source's meaning.
6. No "orphan claims" exist (assertions without any source linkage).
7. The citation chain from claim to evidence to source is documented
   and auditable.

## Fail Actions

- If orphan claims found: locate supporting evidence or remove the claim.
- If citations are broken (dead links): find alternative access points
  or archive copies (Wayback Machine, cached versions).
- If paraphrasing distorts the source: correct the claim to match the
  source or quote directly.
- If citation format is inconsistent: standardize before delivery.
- If a claim cites a source that does not actually support it: correct
  the citation or remove the claim.

## Escalation Rules

- Escalate if a significant number of citations (> 10%) are broken and
  cannot be restored.
- Escalate if a claim is central to the conclusion but no traceable
  evidence can be found.
- Escalate if traceability review reveals potential fabrication or
  hallucination in the evidence chain.
