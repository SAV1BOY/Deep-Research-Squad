# Source Diversity Gate

## Purpose

Ensure that the discovery phase identifies sources from a diverse range of
types, perspectives, geographies, and stakeholder groups. Homogeneous
sourcing creates blind spots and amplifies the biases of a single viewpoint
or information ecosystem.

## When Triggered

- After the initial source identification phase of discovery.
- Before finalizing the source list for deep research.
- When preliminary findings appear one-sided or lack nuance.

## Prerequisites

- Research question and scope defined.
- Initial source list compiled.
- Source classification taxonomy available (type, geography, perspective).

## Checklist

- [ ] Sources span multiple types: academic, industry, government, media, primary data.
- [ ] Geographic diversity is present where the topic has regional dimensions.
- [ ] Sources represent multiple stakeholder perspectives (proponents, critics, neutral).
- [ ] Both quantitative and qualitative sources are included where appropriate.
- [ ] Mainstream and non-mainstream sources are represented.
- [ ] Sources from different time periods are included to capture evolution of thought.
- [ ] No single organization or author dominates the source list.
- [ ] Sources in relevant non-English languages are included or their absence justified.
- [ ] Both for-profit and non-profit / independent sources are represented.
- [ ] Practitioner and theoretical perspectives are both captured.
- [ ] Source list is checked against known blind spots for the domain.

## Pass / Fail Criteria

**Pass**: Sources cover at least three distinct types, represent multiple
perspectives including dissenting views, and no single source category
exceeds 40% of the total.

**Fail**: Sources are concentrated in one type or perspective, dissenting
views are absent, or a single category exceeds 40% of the total.

## Escalation if Failed

- Expand the search strategy to target underrepresented source categories.
- Consult domain experts to identify overlooked source types or communities.
- Escalate to the Architect if source homogeneity may compromise the
  research design.
- Document any justified gaps in diversity (e.g., topic only studied in
  one region) in the research plan.
