# Scope Boundary Gate

## Purpose

This gate ensures that the boundaries of the research effort are clear,
appropriate, and agreed upon before work begins. Without well-defined
boundaries, research expands indefinitely, loses focus, and delivers
diluted results. This gate protects against scope creep and ensures
resources are directed toward the highest-value investigation areas.

## Gate Question

**Are boundaries clear and appropriate?**

The research scope must have explicit geographic, temporal, domain, and
methodological boundaries that match the problem's requirements without
being so narrow that critical context is excluded or so broad that the
effort becomes unfocused.

## Pass Criteria

1. Temporal boundaries are defined with explicit start and end dates or periods of relevance.
2. Geographic or market boundaries are specified where applicable.
3. Domain boundaries clarify which disciplines or sectors are in scope.
4. Entity boundaries identify which organizations, products, or actors are included.
5. Exclusions are explicitly listed with rationale for each exclusion.
6. The scope is proportional to the decision the research will inform.
7. Adjacent topics that might be confused as in-scope are explicitly called out as excluded.
8. Depth expectations are set for each sub-question or research area.
9. Data source boundaries identify which source types will and will not be used.
10. The scope has been validated against the original problem framing for consistency.
11. A scope-change protocol is defined for handling discoveries that suggest boundary adjustment.
12. Resource allocation is feasible within the defined boundaries.

## Fail Actions

- If temporal boundaries are missing, define them based on the decision timeline and confirm.
- If the scope is too broad, prioritize sub-questions and propose a phased approach.
- If the scope is too narrow, identify what critical context may be missed and flag the risk.
- If exclusions lack rationale, document why each item is excluded and get confirmation.
- If boundaries conflict with the problem framing, reconcile before proceeding.
- If no scope-change protocol exists, establish one including who can approve changes.
- If resource allocation is infeasible, propose scope reduction with trade-off analysis.
- Log all boundary decisions and their rationale in the research record.
- Do not proceed to search planning until boundaries are locked.
