# Contradiction Map Quality Checklist

## Purpose
Validate that contradiction maps accurately identify, document, and analyze conflicting information across sources. Contradiction maps are essential for intellectual honesty in research, ensuring that disagreements in data, interpretation, or expert opinion are surfaced rather than suppressed.

## When to Use
- Before incorporating a contradiction map into any research deliverable
- When reviewing the completeness of conflict analysis in research
- During thesis testing to ensure disconfirming evidence is properly handled
- When multiple sources present conflicting data or conclusions on the same topic

## Prerequisites
- Evidence table for the research topic has been completed
- Multiple sources covering the same claims or topics have been reviewed
- Categorization schema for contradiction types has been established
- Domain expertise is available to assess the significance of contradictions
- Research question is clear enough to determine what constitutes a relevant contradiction

## Checklist Items

### Section 1: Identification Completeness
1. [ ] All pairs of contradicting claims in the evidence base are identified — PASS: systematic cross-referencing done; FAIL: ad hoc identification only
2. [ ] Contradictions are found across at least 3 different dimensions (data, methodology, interpretation, scope, temporal) — PASS: multi-dimensional search; FAIL: surface-level conflict identification
3. [ ] Minor discrepancies are distinguished from material contradictions — PASS: severity classification applied; FAIL: all conflicts treated equally
4. [ ] Implicit contradictions (conflicting implications even if not directly opposed) are captured — PASS: implicit conflicts noted; FAIL: only explicit contradictions mapped
5. [ ] Self-contradictions within individual sources are checked — PASS: intra-source consistency verified; FAIL: only inter-source contradictions considered

### Section 2: Documentation Quality
6. [ ] Each contradiction entry includes the specific conflicting claims with direct quotes — PASS: precise documentation; FAIL: paraphrased or vague references
7. [ ] Source details are provided for each side of the contradiction — PASS: full source attribution; FAIL: incomplete sourcing
8. [ ] Date and context of each conflicting claim are recorded — PASS: temporal and contextual framing; FAIL: decontextualized claims
9. [ ] Category or type of contradiction is assigned (factual, interpretive, methodological, etc.) — PASS: categorized; FAIL: uncategorized
10. [ ] Severity rating is assigned (critical, significant, minor) — PASS: severity assessed; FAIL: unrated

### Section 3: Analysis and Resolution
11. [ ] Root cause of each contradiction is investigated — PASS: cause analysis attempted; FAIL: contradictions listed without investigation
12. [ ] Possible explanations for the contradiction are documented — PASS: explanations proposed; FAIL: no attempt at explanation
13. [ ] Resolution status is tracked (resolved, unresolved, partially resolved) — PASS: status tracked; FAIL: no resolution tracking
14. [ ] For resolved contradictions, the resolution rationale is documented — PASS: rationale provided; FAIL: resolution without explanation
15. [ ] For unresolved contradictions, impact on conclusions is assessed — PASS: impact analysis done; FAIL: unresolved contradictions ignored in conclusions

### Section 4: Source Quality Context
16. [ ] Source quality ratings are compared for contradicting sources — PASS: quality differential noted; FAIL: source quality not factored in
17. [ ] More recent sources are flagged when contradicting older sources — PASS: recency noted; FAIL: temporal dimension ignored
18. [ ] Methodological differences that may explain contradictions are identified — PASS: methodology comparison done; FAIL: methodology not examined
19. [ ] Sample size or evidence base differences are noted — PASS: evidentiary weight compared; FAIL: all evidence treated as equal weight
20. [ ] Potential bias in contradicting sources is assessed — PASS: bias analysis performed; FAIL: bias not considered

### Section 5: Integration and Utility
21. [ ] Contradiction map is cross-referenced to the evidence table — PASS: linked to evidence base; FAIL: standalone without connections
22. [ ] Key contradictions are highlighted in the executive summary of the parent deliverable — PASS: contradictions surfaced; FAIL: contradictions buried
23. [ ] Implications of unresolved contradictions for the research conclusions are stated — PASS: impact on conclusions explicit; FAIL: conclusions presented without caveat
24. [ ] Recommendations for resolving outstanding contradictions are provided — PASS: next steps for resolution; FAIL: no path forward
25. [ ] Visual representation of the contradiction landscape is provided — PASS: visual map or matrix; FAIL: text-only listing

## Scoring
- Count the total number of items marked PASS
- Quality Score = (PASS count / 25) x 100
- Grade thresholds:
  - 92-100%: Excellent — thorough and transparent conflict analysis
  - 80-91%: Good — solid contradiction mapping with minor gaps
  - 68-79%: Acceptable — usable but should be strengthened
  - Below 68%: Insufficient — contradiction analysis lacks rigor

## Escalation
- **Accept**: Score of 80% or above with no FAIL on items 1, 10, 15, or 23
- **Revise**: Score of 68-79%, or any FAIL on items 1, 10, 15, or 23
- **Escalate to lead**: Score below 68%, or contradictions reveal potential data integrity issues, or the contradiction landscape fundamentally undermines the research thesis
- **Escalate to stakeholder**: Critical unresolved contradictions prevent a confident conclusion on the primary research question
