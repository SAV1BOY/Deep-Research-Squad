# Synthesis Actionability Check

## Purpose

Ensure the research output is actionable -- that the requester can use it
to make decisions, take action, or advance their understanding. Research
that is academically interesting but practically useless fails its purpose.

## Gate Question

**Is the research output actionable for the requester?**

## Prerequisites

- Requester's intended use case known.
- Conclusions and recommendations drafted.
- Confidence scores assigned.
- Decision context understood (what choices does the requester face).

## Pass Criteria

1. Findings are framed in terms relevant to the requester's context.
2. Recommendations are specific, not generic ("do X by Y date" rather
   than "consider doing X").
3. Each recommendation links to the supporting evidence and its
   confidence level.
4. Trade-offs between options are made explicit.
5. Next steps or action items are provided when appropriate.
6. Timeframes for action are suggested where relevant.
7. The output answers the "so what?" question -- why these findings
   matter for the requester's specific situation.

## Fail Actions

- If findings are not contextualized: reframe in the requester's terms.
- If recommendations are vague: sharpen them with specific actions and
  timelines.
- If trade-offs are hidden: surface them explicitly.
- If evidence-recommendation links are missing: add traceability.
- If "so what?" is unanswered: add an implications section.

## Escalation Rules

- Escalate if the evidence does not support any specific recommendations
  and the requester expects them.
- Escalate if actionability requires domain expertise beyond the agents'
  capability.
- Escalate if the recommended actions involve significant risk and the
  requester should be warned directly.
