# Executive Clarity Gate

## Purpose

This gate ensures that the executive summary and top-level findings are
clear, accurate, and accessible to decision-makers who may not read the
full report. The executive summary is often the only section that senior
stakeholders read, so it must stand alone as an accurate representation
of the research. This gate prevents misalignment between the executive
summary and the detailed findings.

## Gate Question

**Is the executive summary clear and accurate?**

The executive summary must convey the essential findings, recommendations,
and uncertainties in language that a non-specialist can understand, without
sacrificing accuracy or introducing distortions through oversimplification.

## Pass Criteria

1. The executive summary can be understood without reading the full report.
2. Every claim in the executive summary is supported by detailed findings in the body.
3. No findings in the executive summary contradict or overstate the detailed analysis.
4. Key uncertainties and limitations are present in the executive summary, not just findings.
5. The summary uses plain language accessible to the intended audience.
6. Technical jargon is either avoided or defined upon first use.
7. The most important findings are presented first in order of priority.
8. Recommendations in the summary match those in the detailed section exactly.
9. The summary length is proportional to the report length and appropriate for the audience.
10. Quantitative claims in the summary include context for interpretation.
11. The summary clearly states what decision or action it is intended to inform.
12. A non-specialist reviewer has confirmed the summary is understandable and accurate.
13. Visual aids or structured formatting are used to enhance clarity where appropriate.

## Fail Actions

- If the summary contains unsupported claims, either add support in the body or remove them.
- If the summary contradicts detailed findings, reconcile the discrepancy immediately.
- If uncertainties are absent from the summary, add a concise limitations statement.
- If jargon makes the summary inaccessible, rewrite in plain language.
- If findings are not prioritized, reorder by importance to the decision-maker.
- If the summary is disproportionately long or short, resize appropriately.
- If recommendations differ between summary and body, align them precisely.
- If no non-specialist review has occurred, arrange one before release.
- If quantitative claims lack context, add baselines, comparisons, or ranges.
- Do not release the research output until the executive summary passes this gate.
