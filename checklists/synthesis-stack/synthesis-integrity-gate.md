# Synthesis Integrity Gate

## Purpose

This gate ensures that the synthesis of research findings accurately reflects
the underlying evidence without distortion, omission, or overstatement. The
synthesis stage is where bias most commonly enters research output, as
findings are combined, summarized, and interpreted. This gate protects
against the introduction of narrative bias during the synthesis process.

## Gate Question

**Does synthesis accurately reflect evidence?**

The synthesized output must faithfully represent the evidence base in its
totality. No evidence should be selectively emphasized or suppressed to
support a preferred narrative. The synthesis must be an honest aggregation
of what the evidence shows, including its limitations.

## Pass Criteria

1. Every key finding in the synthesis maps to documented evidence from the validation stage.
2. The relative weight given to findings in the synthesis matches their evidence strength.
3. Disconfirming evidence is represented proportionally, not minimized or buried.
4. The synthesis does not introduce claims or interpretations absent from the evidence base.
5. Aggregation of multiple data points is mathematically and logically sound.
6. Causal language in the synthesis is justified by causal evidence, not mere correlation.
7. Nuance and conditionality in the evidence are preserved in the synthesized narrative.
8. The synthesis distinguishes between what the evidence shows and what the researcher infers.
9. No cherry-picking has occurred where only supportive evidence is highlighted.
10. The logical flow from evidence to conclusions is explicit and reviewable.
11. Alternative interpretations of the evidence are acknowledged where reasonable.
12. A peer reviewer has verified that the synthesis does not misrepresent any source.

## Fail Actions

- If synthesis claims lack evidence mapping, create an explicit evidence-to-synthesis trace.
- If disconfirming evidence is underrepresented, revise to give it proportional treatment.
- If unsupported claims are found, either find evidence or remove the claims.
- If causal language exceeds evidential warrant, revise to correlational or conditional phrasing.
- If nuance has been lost, restore conditionality and context from the original evidence.
- If cherry-picking is detected, conduct a full review and rebalance the synthesis.
- If no peer review has been conducted, assign a reviewer before finalizing.
- If alternative interpretations are missing, add a section addressing them.
- Do not proceed to recommendations until synthesis integrity is confirmed.
