# Source Authority Gate

## Purpose

Confirm that sources have established authority and credibility in their
domain. Using authoritative sources increases the reliability of research
findings and the trust placed in conclusions.

## Gate Question

**Do the sources have proven authority in the relevant domain?**

## Prerequisites

- Sources collected and catalogued.
- Author and publisher information extracted.
- Domain expertise markers identified (peer review, institutional
  affiliation, citation count, regulatory status).
- Source credibility rubric available.

## Pass Criteria

1. Each source's author or institution has identifiable domain credentials.
2. Academic sources are from peer-reviewed journals or recognized
   institutions.
3. Government sources are from official agencies with jurisdiction over
   the topic.
4. Industry sources are from established firms or recognized analysts.
5. News sources are from outlets with editorial standards and correction
   policies.
6. At least 60% of sources score "high authority" on the credibility
   rubric.
7. Low-authority sources are used only for leads, not for final claims.

## Fail Actions

- If author credentials cannot be verified: flag the source as unverified
  and reduce its weight in conclusions.
- If fewer than 60% are high-authority: search for more authoritative
  replacements.
- If low-authority sources support critical claims: find corroborating
  high-authority sources.
- If a source's institutional affiliation raises conflict-of-interest
  concerns: note the conflict and seek independent confirmation.

## Escalation Rules

- Escalate if the most relevant sources are from unknown or controversial
  entities and no authoritative alternatives exist.
- Escalate if authority assessment requires domain expertise the agents
  lack.
- Escalate if a traditionally authoritative source has been recently
  discredited or retracted.
