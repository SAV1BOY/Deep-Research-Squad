# Literature State of the Art Check

## Purpose

Ensure the literature review captures the current state of the art in the
relevant domain. Missing the frontier of knowledge leads to outdated or
incomplete research conclusions.

## Gate Question

**Does the literature review reflect the current state of the art?**

## Prerequisites

- Literature search completed across academic databases.
- Key journals and conferences for the domain identified.
- Recent survey papers or meta-analyses located (if they exist).
- Citation counts and publication dates recorded.

## Pass Criteria

1. The most recent survey or review paper in the field is included
   (published within the last 3 years).
2. Publications from the last 2 years are represented in the review.
3. Leading research groups and authors in the field are identified.
4. Current methodological trends are documented.
5. Active debates and open problems in the field are noted.
6. Preprint servers have been checked for very recent work not yet
   peer-reviewed.
7. The state-of-the-art summary can answer: "What do we currently know,
   and what remains unknown?"

## Fail Actions

- If no recent survey exists: build a mini-survey from the most cited
  recent papers.
- If publications from the last 2 years are missing: expand the search
  with date filters.
- If leading authors are unknown: use citation analysis to identify them.
- If preprints were not checked: search arXiv, SSRN, or domain-specific
  preprint servers.
- If open problems are not noted: extract them from the discussion
  sections of key papers.

## Escalation Rules

- Escalate if the field is so new that no established state of the art
  exists.
- Escalate if access to key journals requires subscriptions not available.
- Escalate if the state of the art is contested and experts disagree on
  the current frontier.
