# Literature Gap Identification Check

## Purpose

Identify gaps in the existing literature that are relevant to the research
question. Understanding what is NOT known is as important as summarizing
what IS known.

## Gate Question

**Have gaps in the existing literature been systematically identified?**

## Prerequisites

- State-of-the-art review completed.
- Research question sub-questions mapped to literature findings.
- Comparison matrix of what literature covers vs. what the question asks.
- Domain experts' commentary on unresolved questions reviewed.

## Pass Criteria

1. Each sub-question is mapped to available literature, with gaps marked.
2. Gaps are classified: no research exists, research exists but is
   inconclusive, research exists but is outdated.
3. Methodological gaps are noted (topics studied only with limited methods).
4. Geographic or population gaps are identified (research only in certain
   contexts).
5. Temporal gaps are flagged (no recent studies on a changing topic).
6. The gap analysis informs which claims will have lower confidence.
7. Gaps are documented as explicit "unknown" items in the output.

## Fail Actions

- If gaps are not identified: review the literature-to-question mapping
  and mark uncovered areas.
- If gaps are not classified: apply the classification scheme.
- If methodological gaps are ignored: assess whether the available
  methodology limits the conclusions.
- If gaps are not reflected in confidence levels: adjust confidence
  downward for gap-affected claims.
- If gap documentation is missing: add a dedicated gaps section.

## Escalation Rules

- Escalate if the gaps are so large that the research question cannot
  be meaningfully answered from the literature alone.
- Escalate if gaps suggest that primary research (surveys, experiments)
  would be needed to answer the question.
- Escalate if a critical gap was not discovered until late in the
  research process, requiring scope adjustment.
