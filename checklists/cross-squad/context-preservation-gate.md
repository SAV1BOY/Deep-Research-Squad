# Context Preservation Gate

## Purpose

Ensure that essential research context is preserved when work transitions
between squads. Loss of context at handoff boundaries leads to
misinterpretation, duplicated effort, and conclusions disconnected from the
original research intent.

## When Triggered

- At every squad-to-squad handoff point.
- When research threads are split across multiple squads.
- When a squad resumes work after a pause or personnel change.

## Prerequisites

- Sending squad has documented its work products.
- Research question and scope are established.
- Handoff protocol is agreed upon between squads.

## Checklist

- [ ] Original research question is restated in the handoff materials.
- [ ] Scope boundaries and exclusions are explicitly carried forward.
- [ ] Key definitions and terminology used by the sending squad are documented.
- [ ] Methodological choices and their rationale are explained, not just results.
- [ ] Assumptions made during the sending squad's work are listed with justifications.
- [ ] Confidence levels and uncertainty qualifiers on findings are preserved.
- [ ] Known limitations, caveats, and open questions are carried forward.
- [ ] Source trust ratings and evidence strength assessments are included.
- [ ] Relationships between findings (dependencies, contradictions) are mapped.
- [ ] Decision rationale for what was included and excluded is documented.
- [ ] The receiving squad can trace any finding back to its evidence without the sending squad's help.
- [ ] A context briefing has been provided to the receiving squad lead.

## Pass / Fail Criteria

**Pass**: The receiving squad can independently understand the research
context, reproduce the reasoning chain, and continue work without consulting
the sending squad for clarification.

**Fail**: The receiving squad requires clarification calls, cannot trace
findings to evidence, or misinterprets scope or definitions.

## Escalation if Failed

- Schedule a context transfer session between sending and receiving squad
  leads.
- Document the missing context and add it to the handoff materials before
  proceeding.
- Escalate to the Chief if context loss has already caused downstream
  errors or misinterpretation.
- Update the handoff protocol to prevent recurrence of the specific gap.
