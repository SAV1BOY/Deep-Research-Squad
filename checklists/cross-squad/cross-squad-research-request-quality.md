# Cross-Squad Research Request Quality Gate

## Purpose

This gate ensures that incoming research requests from other squads contain
sufficient information for the receiving squad to begin work effectively.
Vague or incomplete requests lead to wasted effort, misaligned research,
and frustrating back-and-forth cycles. This gate enforces minimum request
quality before a squad accepts and begins work on a cross-squad research
task.

## Gate Question

**Does this incoming request contain sufficient information to begin work?**

A research request must clearly articulate the problem, context, constraints,
expected output, and timeline so the receiving squad can plan, scope, and
execute without ambiguity about what is being asked.

## Pass Criteria

1. The research question or objective is stated clearly and specifically.
2. The business context explaining why this research is needed is provided.
3. The decision this research will inform is identified with its timeline.
4. Scope boundaries are defined including what is in and out of scope.
5. The expected output format and level of detail are specified.
6. Known constraints (time, budget, data access) are documented.
7. Prior research or existing knowledge on the topic is referenced or linked.
8. Key stakeholders and their specific information needs are identified.
9. Success criteria define what constitutes a satisfactory research outcome.
10. Priority level is assigned relative to other work in the receiving squad's queue.
11. A primary contact for clarification questions is designated.
12. Any domain-specific requirements or standards are noted.
13. Dependencies on other work streams or data sources are flagged.

## Fail Actions

- If the research question is vague, return to the requesting squad for clarification.
- If business context is missing, request an explanation of the decision being supported.
- If scope boundaries are undefined, propose boundaries and confirm before starting.
- If the expected output format is unspecified, agree on deliverable format upfront.
- If constraints are undocumented, ask about time, budget, and access limitations.
- If prior research is not referenced, ask whether previous work exists.
- If success criteria are absent, draft criteria and confirm with the requesting squad.
- If no primary contact is designated, require one before accepting the request.
- If priority level is unassigned, negotiate placement in the work queue.
- Do not begin work on the request until minimum quality criteria are met.
