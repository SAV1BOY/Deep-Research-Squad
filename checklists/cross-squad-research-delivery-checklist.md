# Cross-Squad Research Delivery Quality Checklist

## Purpose
Ensure that research deliverables shared across squads or teams meet the additional standards required for cross-functional consumption. Cross-squad deliveries must be self-contained, contextually accessible to audiences without domain expertise, and properly integrated into the receiving squad's workflows and standards.

## When to Use
- Before delivering research products to a different squad or functional team
- When research is commissioned by one squad for use by another
- During multi-squad collaborations where research inputs cross team boundaries
- When standardizing handoff procedures between research squads

## Prerequisites
- Receiving squad's requirements, formats, and standards have been documented
- Points of contact on both the delivering and receiving squads are identified
- Scope of the cross-squad engagement has been formally agreed upon
- Delivery timeline and review process have been coordinated
- Confidentiality and access permissions have been established for cross-squad sharing
- Glossary of squad-specific terminology differences has been reviewed

## Checklist Items

### Section 1: Audience Adaptation
1. [ ] Deliverable is adapted for the receiving squad's domain knowledge — PASS: appropriate context provided; FAIL: insider language without explanation
2. [ ] Technical depth is calibrated to the receiving audience — PASS: right level of detail; FAIL: too technical or too superficial
3. [ ] Squad-specific jargon from the originating team is defined or translated — PASS: glossary or definitions included; FAIL: unexplained jargon
4. [ ] Use case for the receiving squad is explicitly articulated — PASS: application context provided; FAIL: relevance assumed but not stated
5. [ ] Format matches the receiving squad's preferred standards — PASS: format aligned; FAIL: format mismatch

### Section 2: Self-Containment
6. [ ] Deliverable can be understood without access to the originating squad's internal documents — PASS: self-contained; FAIL: requires external references not shared
7. [ ] All prerequisite context is included or clearly referenced with accessible links — PASS: context complete; FAIL: context gaps
8. [ ] Data, charts, and visualizations include full labels, legends, and source notes — PASS: self-explanatory visuals; FAIL: visuals requiring verbal explanation
9. [ ] Methodology is described in sufficient detail for the receiving squad to assess reliability — PASS: method transparent; FAIL: black-box results
10. [ ] Assumptions specific to the originating squad's perspective are made explicit — PASS: assumptions surfaced; FAIL: hidden assumptions from originating context

### Section 3: Quality Verification
11. [ ] Type-specific quality checklist has been completed and passed — PASS: quality gate passed; FAIL: type checklist not completed
12. [ ] Cross-functional peer review has been performed (reviewer from outside originating squad) — PASS: external review done; FAIL: internal-only review
13. [ ] Factual accuracy has been verified — PASS: facts checked; FAIL: unverified claims
14. [ ] Data currency and relevance to the receiving squad's timeframe are confirmed — PASS: current and relevant; FAIL: stale or misaligned data
15. [ ] Conflicting information or limitations are transparently disclosed — PASS: conflicts and limits noted; FAIL: limitations suppressed

### Section 4: Handoff Process
16. [ ] Delivery includes a cover memo explaining scope, purpose, and key findings — PASS: cover memo present; FAIL: raw deliverable without context
17. [ ] Point of contact for questions is identified — PASS: contact provided; FAIL: no follow-up path
18. [ ] Handoff meeting or walkthrough is scheduled if complexity warrants — PASS: meeting arranged or complexity assessed as not warranting; FAIL: complex deliverable sent without walkthrough
19. [ ] Feedback mechanism is established for the receiving squad — PASS: feedback channel defined; FAIL: one-way delivery
20. [ ] Version control and update responsibility are agreed upon — PASS: ownership clear; FAIL: ambiguous ongoing responsibility

### Section 5: Integration and Follow-Up
21. [ ] Receiving squad's intended use of the research is documented — PASS: use case recorded; FAIL: delivery without tracking downstream use
22. [ ] Dependencies between this deliverable and other work streams are mapped — PASS: dependencies identified; FAIL: integration points unknown
23. [ ] Timeline for the receiving squad's consumption and feedback is established — PASS: timeline set; FAIL: open-ended review period
24. [ ] Lessons learned from previous cross-squad deliveries have been incorporated — PASS: past lessons applied; FAIL: known issues repeated
25. [ ] Success criteria for the cross-squad engagement are defined — PASS: criteria established; FAIL: no way to measure engagement success

## Scoring
- Count the total number of items marked PASS
- Quality Score = (PASS count / 25) x 100
- Grade thresholds:
  - 92-100%: Excellent — seamless cross-squad delivery
  - 80-91%: Good — solid delivery with minor coordination gaps
  - 68-79%: Acceptable — usable but integration friction likely
  - Below 68%: Insufficient — delivery process needs significant improvement

## Escalation
- **Accept**: Score of 80% or above with no FAIL on items 1, 6, 11, or 16
- **Revise**: Score of 68-79%, or any FAIL on items 1, 6, 11, or 16
- **Escalate to lead**: Score below 68%, or receiving squad has rejected previous deliveries, or fundamental misalignment between squads on scope or standards
- **Escalate to stakeholder**: Cross-squad delivery failure risks project timeline, or governance issues between squads cannot be resolved at team level
