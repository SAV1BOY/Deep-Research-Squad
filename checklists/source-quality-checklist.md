# Source Quality Quality Checklist

## Purpose
Provide a systematic framework for evaluating the quality, reliability, and credibility of individual sources used in research. This checklist ensures that every source contributing to research findings meets minimum quality standards and that source limitations are transparently documented.

## When to Use
- When evaluating any new source before incorporating it into research
- During periodic review of source quality across a research project
- When source credibility is questioned during peer review
- As part of the evidence table construction process
- When training new squad members on source evaluation standards

## Prerequisites
- Source has been fully accessed and reviewed (not just abstract or summary)
- Context of how the source will be used in the research is understood
- Source type classification system is familiar to the evaluator
- Domain expertise or consultation is available for specialized sources
- Comparison sources in the same domain are accessible for benchmarking

## Checklist Items

### Section 1: Authority and Credibility
1. [ ] Author or publishing organization is identified — PASS: clear attribution; FAIL: anonymous or unattributable
2. [ ] Author credentials or organizational reputation are relevant to the topic — PASS: qualified author; FAIL: author lacks relevant expertise
3. [ ] Publishing outlet is reputable or appropriate for the content type — PASS: credible outlet; FAIL: unknown or unreliable publisher
4. [ ] Peer review or editorial process is evident — PASS: review process identifiable; FAIL: no quality control apparent
5. [ ] Potential conflicts of interest are assessed — PASS: conflicts checked and noted; FAIL: conflicts not considered

### Section 2: Accuracy and Verifiability
6. [ ] Factual claims within the source are internally consistent — PASS: no internal contradictions; FAIL: contradictions found
7. [ ] Key claims can be verified against independent sources — PASS: cross-verification possible; FAIL: unverifiable claims
8. [ ] Data sources and methodology are disclosed — PASS: methods transparent; FAIL: black-box conclusions
9. [ ] Statistical claims include sample sizes, confidence intervals, or similar rigor — PASS: statistical rigor present; FAIL: unsupported quantitative claims
10. [ ] Errors, corrections, or retractions are checked for — PASS: correction history reviewed; FAIL: not checked

### Section 3: Currency and Relevance
11. [ ] Publication date is identified — PASS: dated source; FAIL: undated or date unknown
12. [ ] Source is sufficiently current for the research purpose — PASS: recency appropriate; FAIL: outdated without justification for inclusion
13. [ ] Content is directly relevant to the research question — PASS: clear relevance; FAIL: tangential content
14. [ ] Geographic and contextual scope matches research needs — PASS: scope alignment; FAIL: scope mismatch
15. [ ] Source has not been superseded by more current work — PASS: still current or seminal; FAIL: superseded by newer research

### Section 4: Objectivity and Bias
16. [ ] Tone is balanced and evidence-based — PASS: measured tone; FAIL: polemical or advocacy-driven
17. [ ] Multiple perspectives are represented or acknowledged — PASS: balanced viewpoint; FAIL: one-sided presentation
18. [ ] Funding sources or sponsors are disclosed if applicable — PASS: funding transparency; FAIL: undisclosed funding with potential bias
19. [ ] Language is precise and avoids loaded terms — PASS: neutral language; FAIL: emotionally charged or misleading wording
20. [ ] Source purpose (inform, persuade, sell, entertain) is identified — PASS: purpose assessed; FAIL: purpose not considered

### Section 5: Source Classification and Rating
21. [ ] Source is classified by type (primary, secondary, tertiary) — PASS: type assigned; FAIL: unclassified
22. [ ] Source is classified by category (academic, industry, government, media, etc.) — PASS: category assigned; FAIL: uncategorized
23. [ ] Overall quality rating is assigned (A/B/C/D or equivalent) — PASS: rating assigned; FAIL: unrated
24. [ ] Limitations of the source are documented — PASS: limitations noted; FAIL: limitations not considered
25. [ ] Recommended use context is stated (e.g., background only, citable for claims, primary evidence) — PASS: usage guidance provided; FAIL: no guidance on how to use the source

## Scoring
- Count the total number of items marked PASS
- Quality Score = (PASS count / 25) x 100
- Grade thresholds:
  - 92-100%: A-grade source — high confidence in reliability
  - 80-91%: B-grade source — reliable with noted limitations
  - 68-79%: C-grade source — use with caution and corroboration
  - Below 68%: D-grade source — consider excluding or use for background only

## Escalation
- **Accept**: Score of 80% or above with no FAIL on items 1, 6, or 16
- **Revise assessment**: Score of 68-79%, or any FAIL on items 1, 6, or 16 — seek corroboration before relying on this source
- **Escalate to lead**: Score below 68% and the source is critical to key findings, or potential fabrication or manipulation detected
- **Escalate to stakeholder**: Source quality concerns materially affect the confidence level of the overall research conclusions
