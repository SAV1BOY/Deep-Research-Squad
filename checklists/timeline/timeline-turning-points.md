# Timeline Turning Points Check

## Purpose

Identify and validate the key turning points in the timeline -- moments
where the trajectory changed significantly. Turning points are essential
for understanding why the current state exists and what could change it.

## Gate Question

**Have the key turning points been identified and validated?**

## Prerequisites

- Complete timeline available.
- Trend analysis performed (what was the trajectory before and after
  each candidate turning point).
- Stakeholder perspectives on pivotal moments collected.
- Quantitative indicators (if available) charted over time.

## Pass Criteria

1. At least the top 3-5 turning points are identified and ranked.
2. Each turning point has a before-and-after comparison showing the
   trajectory change.
3. The cause or trigger of each turning point is documented.
4. Turning points are validated by multiple sources (not just one
   account's perspective).
5. False turning points (events that seemed important but did not
   change the trajectory) are identified and excluded.
6. The relative importance of turning points is assessed.
7. Turning points inform the forward-looking analysis (scenarios,
   predictions).

## Fail Actions

- If fewer than 3 turning points identified: look for subtler shifts
  in the timeline data.
- If before-and-after comparison is missing: create it for each
  turning point.
- If a turning point lacks multi-source validation: seek corroboration.
- If false turning points are not filtered: review each candidate
  against quantitative evidence.
- If turning points do not inform forward analysis: connect them to
  scenario planning.

## Escalation Rules

- Escalate if the identification of turning points is contested and
  different stakeholders disagree on which events were pivotal.
- Escalate if a critical turning point is too recent to assess its
  full impact.
- Escalate if turning point analysis reveals that the topic is at a
  current inflection point requiring real-time monitoring.
