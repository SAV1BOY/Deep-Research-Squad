# Health Research Gate

## Purpose

This gate ensures that health research meets domain-specific quality
standards before delivery. Health research informs decisions that can
directly affect human wellbeing, making accuracy and responsible
communication paramount. The health domain requires particular rigor
in evidence evaluation, regulatory awareness, and ethical consideration.
This gate enforces the highest standards of evidence-based reasoning
for health-related research outputs.

## Gate Question

**Does this health research meet quality standards?**

Health research must demonstrate adherence to evidence-based methodology,
proper use of clinical and epidemiological evidence hierarchies, regulatory
awareness, and responsible communication that neither overstates nor
understates health-related findings.

## Pass Criteria

1. Evidence is evaluated using established evidence hierarchies (systematic reviews, RCTs, etc.).
2. Clinical study quality is assessed using recognized frameworks (GRADE, Cochrane risk of bias).
3. Sample sizes, study designs, and statistical methods are evaluated for appropriateness.
4. The distinction between clinical significance and statistical significance is maintained.
5. Regulatory status and approval history are documented for products and interventions.
6. Safety data including adverse events and contraindications are included where relevant.
7. Conflict of interest disclosures for cited researchers and institutions are checked.
8. Population generalizability is assessed for all clinical evidence cited.
9. Emerging evidence is distinguished from established medical consensus.
10. Health claims comply with relevant regulatory frameworks for the jurisdiction.
11. Ethical considerations including patient privacy and informed consent are addressed.
12. The research avoids language that could be interpreted as medical advice.
13. Peer-reviewed sources are prioritized over preprints and press releases.

## Fail Actions

- If evidence hierarchy is not applied, re-evaluate sources using established frameworks.
- If study quality assessment is missing, apply appropriate quality evaluation tools.
- If clinical and statistical significance are conflated, distinguish them explicitly.
- If regulatory status is undocumented, verify approval and compliance status.
- If safety data is absent, add known risks and adverse event profiles.
- If conflict of interest checks are incomplete, review author disclosures.
- If generalizability is unassessed, note population limitations on clinical evidence.
- If language could be interpreted as medical advice, revise with appropriate disclaimers.
- Do not deliver health research until domain-specific standards are met.
