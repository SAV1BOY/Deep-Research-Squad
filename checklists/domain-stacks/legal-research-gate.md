# Legal Research Gate

## Purpose

This gate ensures that legal research meets domain-specific quality
standards before delivery. Legal research informs compliance decisions,
litigation strategy, contract negotiations, and regulatory risk management.
Inaccurate legal research can result in regulatory penalties, contractual
liability, or litigation exposure. This gate enforces jurisdictional
precision, source authority, and appropriate disclaimers in all legal
research outputs.

## Gate Question

**Does this legal research meet quality standards?**

Legal research must demonstrate jurisdictional accuracy, reliance on
authoritative legal sources, current awareness of legal developments,
and clear communication that distinguishes between legal analysis and
legal advice.

## Pass Criteria

1. Jurisdictional scope is explicitly defined and consistently maintained throughout.
2. Primary legal sources (statutes, regulations, case law) are cited over secondary commentary.
3. Legal authorities are current with all recent amendments and judicial interpretations.
4. The hierarchy of legal authority (constitutional, statutory, regulatory, case law) is respected.
5. Relevant case law includes binding and persuasive authority with the distinction noted.
6. Regulatory guidance and enforcement patterns are included alongside formal legal text.
7. Pending legislation or regulatory proposals that may change the landscape are flagged.
8. Cross-jurisdictional conflicts or variations are identified where applicable.
9. Legal terminology is used precisely and defined for non-legal audiences where needed.
10. The research distinguishes between established legal principles and emerging interpretations.
11. Compliance obligations are stated in actionable terms with clear applicability criteria.
12. The research includes appropriate disclaimers that it does not constitute legal advice.
13. Confidentiality and privilege considerations are respected in source handling.

## Fail Actions

- If jurisdictional scope is ambiguous, define and confirm the applicable jurisdictions.
- If secondary sources are used without primary verification, check against primary authorities.
- If legal authorities are outdated, update to reflect current law and recent developments.
- If binding and persuasive authority are not distinguished, add appropriate classification.
- If pending legislative changes are omitted, scan for relevant proposals and flag them.
- If cross-jurisdictional conflicts are unaddressed, identify and document the variations.
- If compliance obligations are vague, translate into specific actionable requirements.
- If legal advice disclaimers are missing, add them prominently.
- Do not deliver legal research until domain-specific standards are met.
