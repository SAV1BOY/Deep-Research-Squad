# Technology Timeline — Swipe Examples

## Purpose

Technology timelines map the evolution, adoption, and convergence of
technologies over time. They help identify patterns, predict inflection
points, and contextualize where we are in a technology's lifecycle.

## Gold Standard Structure

### 1. Timeline Header
- Technology or technology cluster being mapped
- Time range covered
- Key thesis about the trajectory

### 2. Phase-Based Timeline

**Phase 1: Research (Years X-Y)**
- Key papers and breakthroughs
- Funding sources and amounts
- Lab demonstrations and benchmarks

**Phase 2: Early Adoption (Years X-Y)**
- First commercial applications
- Early adopter profiles
- Initial market sizing

**Phase 3: Growth (Years X-Y)**
- Mass market signals
- Competitive landscape formation
- Standards and regulation emergence

**Phase 4: Maturity (Years X-Y)**
- Commoditization indicators
- Consolidation events
- Next-generation research beginning

### 3. Parallel Tracks
Map enabling and dependent technologies alongside the primary one.

### 4. Inflection Points
Specific events that accelerated or decelerated the timeline.

## Example: Large Language Models Timeline

> **2017**: Transformer architecture published (Google, "Attention Is All You Need")
> **2018**: BERT released, GPT-1 published. NLP benchmarks begin falling.
> **2019**: GPT-2 released with controversy. First "too dangerous to release" debate.
> **2020**: GPT-3 demonstrates few-shot learning. API access model begins.
> **2022**: ChatGPT launches. Consumer AI adoption inflection point.
> **2023**: GPT-4, Claude, Gemini. Enterprise adoption begins at scale.
> **2024**: Multimodal models standard. Agent frameworks emerge.
> **2025**: Reasoning models, tool use, autonomous research capabilities.
>
> **Enabling technologies**: GPU compute scaling, RLHF, web-scale datasets
> **Inflection point**: ChatGPT launch (Nov 2022) — fastest consumer adoption ever

## Timeline Quality Standards

1. **Date-specific**: Use exact dates, not vague periods
2. **Source-backed**: Every timeline entry has a citation
3. **Parallel tracks**: Show enabling and dependent technologies
4. **Inflection markers**: Highlight phase transitions explicitly
5. **Forward projection**: Include near-term projections with confidence bands

## Common Pitfalls

- Retrospective bias: making the past look more linear than it was
- Missing dead ends and failed alternatives
- Ignoring parallel developments in other geographies
- Treating adoption as inevitable rather than contingent
- Not noting what was surprising versus expected at each stage
