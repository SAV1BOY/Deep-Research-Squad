# Scenario Planning — Swipe Examples

## Purpose

Scenario planning constructs multiple plausible futures to stress-test
strategies and prepare for uncertainty. It is not prediction — it is
structured preparation for different possible worlds.

## Gold Standard Structure

### 1. Scenario Framework
- **Key uncertainty axis 1**: The most impactful uncertain variable
- **Key uncertainty axis 2**: The second most impactful uncertain variable
- **2×2 matrix**: Four scenarios from the intersection

### 2. Per-Scenario Template

**Scenario Name**: [Evocative name that captures the essence]

- **World description**: 3-5 sentences painting the picture
- **How we got here**: The sequence of events that led to this world
- **Key indicators**: What signals would tell us this scenario is materializing
- **Winners and losers**: Who thrives and who struggles in this world
- **Strategic implications**: What our strategy should be in this scenario
- **Probability estimate**: Current assessed likelihood (with reasoning)

### 3. Robust Strategies
Actions that perform well across multiple scenarios.

### 4. Monitoring Dashboard
Early indicators to watch for each scenario.

## Example: AI Regulation Scenarios (2025-2030)

> **Axis 1**: Regulation strictness (Light vs Heavy)
> **Axis 2**: Global coordination (Fragmented vs Unified)
>
> **Scenario A — "Digital Wild West"** (Light + Fragmented): 25% probability
> Minimal regulation, each country goes its own way. Innovation maximized
> but trust crises emerge. Big tech self-regulates inconsistently.
>
> **Scenario B — "Brussels Rules the World"** (Heavy + Unified): 15%
> EU-style regulation becomes global standard. Compliance costs high,
> but market certainty increases. Favors large incumbents.
>
> **Scenario C — "Patchwork Quilt"** (Heavy + Fragmented): 40%
> Every major economy creates different rules. Compliance nightmare.
> Regional champions emerge. Global platforms fragment.
>
> **Scenario D — "Enlightened Governance"** (Light + Unified): 20%
> Global agreement on principles-based regulation. Innovation continues
> with guardrails. Most optimistic but least likely.

## Quality Standards for Scenario Planning

1. Scenarios must be plausible, not just possible
2. Include at least one scenario that challenges current strategy
3. Probability estimates must sum to ~100%
4. Each scenario needs specific, observable indicators
5. Time horizon must be explicit

## Common Failures

- Making the preferred outcome the most likely scenario
- Scenarios that are too similar to each other
- Missing the scenario that actually happens because it seemed extreme
- Not updating probabilities as new information arrives
- Building scenarios around predictions instead of uncertainties
