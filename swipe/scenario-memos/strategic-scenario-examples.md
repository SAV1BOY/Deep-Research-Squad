# Strategic Scenario Memo — Swipe Examples

## Purpose

Strategic scenario memos translate scenario planning into specific strategic
recommendations. They answer: "Given these possible futures, what should
we do today?"

## Gold Standard Structure

### 1. Strategic Context
- Current strategy summary (2-3 sentences)
- Key assumption being tested
- Why scenario analysis is needed now

### 2. Scenario Summary
Brief recap of 3-4 scenarios with current probability weights.

### 3. Strategy Stress Test

| Strategic Element | Scenario A | Scenario B | Scenario C | Scenario D |
|------------------|-----------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| Market entry plan | Works     | Fails     | Partial   | Works     |
| Pricing strategy  | Works     | Works     | Fails     | Works     |
| Partnership model | Fails     | Works     | Works     | Partial   |
| Tech investment   | Optimal   | Wasteful  | Partial   | Optimal   |

### 4. Robust Moves
Actions that perform well across most scenarios.

### 5. Option-Creating Moves
Investments that create flexibility without committing to one path.

### 6. Scenario-Specific Hedges
Actions to take only if a specific scenario materializes.

### 7. Decision Triggers
Specific, observable events that should trigger strategy review.

## Example Strategic Scenario Memo

> **Context**: Our expansion into Southeast Asia assumes stable regulatory
> environment and growing digital adoption. Recent political shifts in
> three target countries challenge both assumptions.
>
> **Robust moves** (do regardless):
> 1. Build modular compliance infrastructure (serves all scenarios)
> 2. Maintain partnerships in 2+ countries (reduces concentration risk)
> 3. Keep burn rate below threshold X (preserves optionality)
>
> **Option-creating moves**:
> 1. Pilot in Singapore (low-risk, high-signal market test)
> 2. Build local team before committing infrastructure (reversible)
>
> **Trigger**: If regulatory framework Y passes in Indonesia by Q2,
> accelerate to full market entry (Scenario B becomes dominant).

## What Makes Scenario Memos Actionable

1. **Today-focused**: What to do now, not what to do if...
2. **Distinguishes types of action**: Robust, option-creating, contingent
3. **Observable triggers**: Not "when the market shifts" but "when metric X hits Y"
4. **Resource-aware**: Accounts for budget and capacity constraints
5. **Time-bounded**: Clear review dates and decision points

## Integration with Research Squad

- Scenario memos draw on evidence tables for probability estimates
- Timeline maps feed inflection point analysis into scenario construction
- Competitive intelligence informs competitor behavior assumptions
- Contrarian analysis ensures uncomfortable scenarios are included
- Decision briefs emerge from scenario memos when triggers are hit
