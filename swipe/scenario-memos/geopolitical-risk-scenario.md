# Geopolitical Risk Scenario Memo — Swipe Example

## Why This Is Gold Standard

Geopolitical scenario memos must be analytically rigorous while acknowledging deep
uncertainty. They avoid prediction in favor of preparedness. The best examples define
clear scenario boundaries, identify leading indicators for each path, and connect
geopolitical dynamics to concrete business impact. They never pretend to know the
unknowable.

## Structure Template

### 1. Memo Header
- **Subject**: Taiwan Strait escalation — supply chain and market access scenarios
- **Prepared for**: Executive Committee
- **Classification**: Confidential — Strategic Planning
- **Date**: March 2026
- **Horizon**: 6-24 months
- **Overall uncertainty**: Very High (geopolitical scenarios inherently low-confidence)

### 2. Situation Assessment
Cross-strait tensions have intensified following expanded military exercises and
diplomatic realignments in the Indo-Pacific. While direct military conflict remains
a tail risk, the spectrum of scenarios between status quo and conflict carries
significant business implications. This memo maps four scenarios and their operational
impact.

**Key variables driving scenarios:**
1. Military posture intensity (exercises, deployments, incidents)
2. Economic coercion measures (trade restrictions, sanctions)
3. Third-party alignment (US, Japan, ASEAN positioning)
4. Domestic political dynamics in all parties

### 3. Scenario Matrix

|  | Low Economic Coercion | High Economic Coercion |
|--|----------------------|----------------------|
| **Low Military Tension** | **Scenario A**: Managed Competition | **Scenario B**: Economic Pressure Campaign |
| **High Military Tension** | **Scenario C**: Military Posturing | **Scenario D**: Full Spectrum Escalation |

### 4. Scenario Details

**Scenario A — Managed Competition (probability: 45%)**
Continued strategic competition within established guardrails. Periodic tensions
but effective de-escalation mechanisms. Trade continues with incremental restrictions.
- **Business impact**: Moderate; compliance costs rise 10-15%, supply chain diversification
  continues at current pace
- **Leading indicators**: Regular diplomatic communication, military hotline active,
  trade volumes stable

**Scenario B — Economic Pressure Campaign (probability: 25%)**
Significant escalation in economic tools: expanded export controls, investment
screening, selective sanctions on technology sectors. Military tension remains managed.
- **Business impact**: Severe for tech sector; potential loss of market access, forced
  entity list compliance, 20-40% revenue impact for exposed business lines
- **Leading indicators**: Expanded entity lists, investment ban announcements,
  retaliatory trade measures, diplomatic recall

**Scenario C — Military Posturing (probability: 20%)**
Increased military activity (exercises, shows of force, gray zone operations) without
direct confrontation. Economic relationship strained but not severed.
- **Business impact**: High uncertainty premium; insurance costs spike, shipping routes
  disrupted, customer decision-making freezes in affected region
- **Leading indicators**: Military exercise frequency doubling, airspace/maritime
  incidents, carrier group deployments, defense spending surges

**Scenario D — Full Spectrum Escalation (probability: 10%)**
Combined military and economic escalation. Potential for blockade, severe sanctions,
or conflict. This is the tail risk scenario.
- **Business impact**: Catastrophic for exposed operations; semiconductor supply
  collapse, market access loss, asset seizure risk, global recession trigger
- **Leading indicators**: Evacuation advisories, military mobilization beyond
  exercises, UN Security Council emergency sessions, financial market circuit breakers

### 5. Exposure Assessment

| Business Function | Scenario A | Scenario B | Scenario C | Scenario D |
|-------------------|-----------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| Supply chain | Low | High | Medium | Critical |
| Revenue (APAC) | Low | High | Medium | Critical |
| R&D operations | Low | Medium | Low | High |
| Talent mobility | Low | Medium | Medium | Critical |
| IP protection | Medium | High | Medium | Critical |
| Financial (FX, capital) | Low | Medium | High | Critical |

### 6. Preparedness Recommendations

**Immediate (all scenarios):**
1. Map full supply chain exposure to Taiwan and cross-strait dependencies
2. Review force majeure clauses in all APAC contracts
3. Establish scenario monitoring dashboard with leading indicators

**Scenario B preparedness:**
4. Identify alternative suppliers for critical components (12-18 month qualification)
5. Legal review of sanctions compliance obligations across jurisdictions
6. Stress-test revenue model for 30% APAC reduction

**Scenario C/D preparedness:**
7. Business continuity plan for 90-day supply chain disruption
8. Data and IP localization review — ensure critical assets not at risk
9. Employee safety and evacuation protocols for regional staff

### 7. Monitoring Framework

| Indicator | Current State | Threshold for Reassessment |
|-----------|--------------|---------------------------|
| Military exercises per quarter | 4 (baseline) | >8 triggers Scenario C review |
| Entity list additions per quarter | 12 (baseline) | >25 triggers Scenario B review |
| Diplomatic meeting frequency | Monthly | Cessation triggers all-scenario review |
| Semiconductor spot pricing | Stable | >30% spike triggers supply chain review |
| Shipping insurance premiums | +15% YoY | >50% YoY triggers logistics review |

### 8. What This Memo Does Not Do
- Predict which scenario will occur (geopolitical prediction is unreliable)
- Assess military capabilities or intentions (outside our competence)
- Replace government travel or security advisories
- Constitute legal advice on sanctions compliance

## Quality Checklist
- [ ] Scenario matrix built on two independent variables
- [ ] Probabilities assigned to each scenario (summing to ~100%)
- [ ] Leading indicators defined for each scenario
- [ ] Business impact mapped by function and scenario
- [ ] Recommendations tiered by urgency and scenario
- [ ] Monitoring framework with specific thresholds
- [ ] Limitations explicitly stated
- [ ] No false precision — uncertainty acknowledged throughout
