# Landscape Analysis — Swipe Examples

## Purpose

Landscape analyses go beyond market maps to analyze dynamics, power shifts,
and strategic implications. While a market map shows who's where, a landscape
analysis explains why things are moving and what happens next.

## Gold Standard Structure

### 1. Landscape Overview
- Market definition and boundaries
- Current state in one paragraph
- Key tension or dynamic driving change

### 2. Power Analysis
- **Who has leverage and why**: Identify 3-5 power holders
- **Shifting dynamics**: What's changing the power balance
- **Dependency mapping**: Who depends on whom

### 3. Segment Deep-Dives
For each major segment:
- Current leaders and challengers
- Growth trajectory with data
- Key battlegrounds
- Likely consolidation patterns

### 4. Cross-Cutting Trends
- Regulatory forces affecting multiple segments
- Technology shifts creating new categories
- Capital flows indicating future direction
- Talent migration patterns

### 5. Scenario Implications
- Bull case: what the landscape looks like in 3 years if trends accelerate
- Base case: steady-state evolution
- Bear case: disruption or contraction scenario

## Example: Enterprise AI Landscape (2025)

> **Key Dynamic**: The enterprise AI market is bifurcating. Large enterprises
> are consolidating on 2-3 platform providers. Mid-market companies are
> assembling best-of-breed stacks. This creates two distinct competitive
> landscapes with different success factors.
>
> **Power shift**: Cloud providers (AWS, Azure, GCP) are gaining leverage as
> AI workloads drive compute dependency. Model providers are losing leverage
> as open-source alternatives reach 85% of proprietary model performance.
>
> **Implication**: The value is migrating from models to data infrastructure
> and domain-specific applications. Invest accordingly.

## Quality Indicators

1. **Identifies non-obvious dynamics**: Goes beyond what everyone knows
2. **Quantifies power shifts**: Uses revenue, market share, dependency data
3. **Time-layered**: Shows trajectory, not just snapshot
4. **Actionable segments**: Each section has strategic implications
5. **Contrarian signals noted**: Where the consensus might be wrong

## Common Pitfalls

- Listing companies without analyzing dynamics
- Ignoring adjacent market threats
- Treating the landscape as static
- Over-relying on analyst reports without primary data
- Missing regulatory or geopolitical forces
