# Evidence Table — Gold Standard Examples

## Purpose

Evidence tables are the backbone of rigorous research. They organize all
evidence supporting or contradicting a claim in a structured, auditable
format that makes the strength of any conclusion transparent.

## Gold Standard Structure

### Claim: [Specific, falsifiable claim being evaluated]

| # | Source | Type | Finding | Supports | Confidence | Date | Notes |
|---|--------|------|---------|----------|------------|------|-------|
| 1 | Source name | Category | What it found | Y/N/Partial | 0-100 | Date | Caveats |

### Synthesis Row
- **Sources supporting**: Count and quality assessment
- **Sources contradicting**: Count and quality assessment
- **Overall confidence**: Weighted score with justification
- **Key gaps**: What evidence is missing

## Example Evidence Table

### Claim: "Remote work reduces employee productivity"

| # | Source | Type | Finding | Supports | Conf | Date | Notes |
|---|--------|------|---------|----------|------|------|-------|
| 1 | Stanford/Bloom 2024 | Peer-reviewed | Hybrid 3-4% more productive | No | 85 | 2024-Q2 | n=1,612 |
| 2 | Microsoft Work Index | Corporate research | Collaboration down 15% | Partial | 70 | 2024-Q1 | Self-reported |
| 3 | NBER Working Paper | Pre-print | Fully remote -8% output | Yes | 65 | 2024-Q3 | Manufacturing only |
| 4 | Gallup Workplace | Survey | Engagement higher remote | No | 75 | 2024-Q4 | n=15,000 |
| 5 | Meta internal study | Corporate leak | Remote engineers ship less | Yes | 50 | 2024-Q1 | Leaked, unverified |

### Synthesis
- **Supporting**: 2 sources (one limited to manufacturing, one unverified)
- **Contradicting**: 2 sources (stronger methodology, larger samples)
- **Partial**: 1 source (measures collaboration, not productivity)
- **Overall confidence in claim**: 35/100 — Evidence leans against the claim
- **Key gap**: No longitudinal studies >3 years

## Evidence Grading Criteria

| Grade | Definition | Example |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| A | Peer-reviewed, large sample, replicated | Meta-analysis in top journal |
| B | Peer-reviewed OR large sample | Single well-designed study |
| C | Credible source, methodology unclear | Think tank report |
| D | Self-reported or potential bias | Company's own research |
| F | Unverified, anonymous, or conflicted | Blog post, leaked memo |

## Rules for Evidence Tables

1. Never exclude contradicting evidence
2. Weight by methodology quality, not by agreement with thesis
3. Date all evidence — recency matters
4. Flag conflicts of interest
5. Note sample sizes and populations
6. Distinguish correlation from causation claims
