# Claim Verification — Swipe Examples

## Purpose

Claim verification is the systematic process of testing whether a specific
assertion is supported by evidence. It goes beyond fact-checking to assess
nuance, context, and confidence levels.

## Gold Standard Verification Process

### Step 1: Decompose the Claim
Break the claim into individually testable components.

**Original claim**: "AI will replace 40% of jobs within 10 years"
- Component A: AI is capable of automating job tasks
- Component B: The percentage is approximately 40%
- Component C: The timeframe is approximately 10 years
- Component D: "Replace" means full elimination, not augmentation

### Step 2: Source Each Component

| Component | Source 1 | Source 2 | Source 3 | Agreement |
|-----------|----------|----------|----------|-----------|
| A | McKinsey (Yes) | OECD (Yes) | MIT (Partial) | High |
| B | McKinsey (30%) | Goldman (25%) | WEF (14%) | Low |
| C | Varies: 10-30yr | Varies: 5-20yr | No consensus | Low |
| D | Most say augment | Few say replace | Mixed | Low |

### Step 3: Verdict

> **Verdict**: MOSTLY FALSE (Confidence: 72)
> AI will automate significant portions of many jobs (supported), but
> the 40% figure is higher than most credible estimates (14-30%),
> the timeframe is uncertain, and most research indicates augmentation
> rather than replacement.

## Verification Verdict Scale

- **CONFIRMED** (90-100): Multiple independent, high-quality sources agree
- **LIKELY TRUE** (70-89): Preponderance of evidence supports
- **MIXED** (40-69): Evidence is contradictory or insufficient
- **LIKELY FALSE** (20-39): Preponderance of evidence contradicts
- **FALSE** (0-19): Multiple independent sources contradict
- **UNVERIFIABLE**: Cannot be tested with available evidence

## Common Verification Traps

1. **Cherry-picking**: Finding one source that confirms and stopping
2. **Authority bias**: Believing a claim because the source is prestigious
3. **Recency bias**: Preferring newer data even when older data is better
4. **Precision bias**: Treating specific numbers as more credible
5. **Survivorship bias**: Only seeing claims that survived to publication

## Rapid Verification Checklist

- [ ] Is the claim specific and falsifiable?
- [ ] Can I find the original source (not just citations of it)?
- [ ] Do 3+ independent sources address this claim?
- [ ] Have I sought disconfirming evidence?
- [ ] Have I checked for conflicts of interest?
- [ ] Is the claim's context preserved or distorted?
- [ ] Have I decomposed compound claims into parts?
