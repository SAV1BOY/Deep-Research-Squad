# One-Page Brief — Swipe Examples

## Purpose

One-page briefs compress complex research into a single page that a busy
executive can read in 2-3 minutes and make a decision. Every word earns its place.

## Gold Standard Structure

### Header Block
- **Topic**: Clear, specific title (not "Market Update" but "AI Chip Supply Gap Widening Through 2026")
- **Date**: When this analysis was completed
- **Confidence**: Overall confidence score (0-100)
- **Decision needed by**: Deadline if applicable

### Bottom Line Up Front (BLUF)
2-3 sentences maximum. The answer, the confidence level, the key implication.

### Key Findings (3-5 bullets)
Each bullet: Claim + Evidence + Confidence
- "Enterprise AI spending will grow 34% in 2025 (Gartner, IDC, internal data; confidence: 85)"
- "Talent shortage is the #1 constraint, cited by 72% of CTO respondents (n=340; confidence: 78)"

### Evidence Quality
One-line assessment: source count, diversity score, any contradictions noted.

### Recommendation
Numbered actions, each one sentence, each tied to a finding above.

### What We Don't Know
2-3 bullets on key unknowns and how they could change the recommendation.

## Example Brief

> **BLUF**: The autonomous vehicle market has reached an inflection point.
> Waymo's cost-per-mile dropped below human rideshare drivers in 3 markets.
> Companies without an AV strategy will face disruption within 36 months.
>
> **Key Findings**:
> 1. Waymo cost/mile: $0.31 vs Uber driver $0.44 (Q4 2025 data, confidence: 90)
> 2. Regulatory approvals accelerating: 12 states approved in 2025 vs 4 in 2024 (confidence: 95)
> 3. Insurance data shows AV accident rates 62% below human drivers (confidence: 75)
>
> **Recommendation**: Begin AV integration planning within 60 days.

## Anti-Patterns

- Dense paragraphs that require re-reading
- Findings without confidence scores
- Recommendations not linked to findings
- Missing "what we don't know" section
- Burying the answer in background context

## Compression Techniques

1. Cut every adjective that doesn't change meaning
2. Replace clauses with data points
3. Use active voice exclusively
4. One idea per sentence, one claim per bullet
5. Delete any sentence the reader could guess from context
