# Crisis Response Brief — Swipe Example

## Why This Is Gold Standard

Crisis briefs must be produced under extreme time pressure while maintaining analytical
rigor. They prioritize speed-to-decision over comprehensiveness. Every section answers
"what do we do right now?" The best crisis briefs separate confirmed facts from
unconfirmed reports and provide clear decision trees.

## Structure Template

### Header Block
- **Incident**: Concise description (e.g., "Critical supply chain disruption — Southeast Asia semiconductor fabs")
- **Severity**: Critical / High / Medium / Low
- **Time sensitivity**: Decision needed within [hours/days]
- **Prepared**: Date and time of preparation
- **Version**: Brief version number (crisis briefs are updated frequently)
- **Confidence**: Overall confidence in assessment (0-100)

### Situation (What happened)
At 0340 UTC on March 2, 2026, flooding in central Taiwan disrupted operations at three
major semiconductor fabrication facilities. Initial reports indicate 4-8 weeks of
reduced capacity affecting advanced node production (sub-7nm). This impacts an estimated
18% of global advanced chip supply.

### Confirmed Facts vs. Unconfirmed Reports

| # | Item | Status | Source | Confidence |
|---|------|--------|--------|------------|
| 1 | Three fabs shut down | Confirmed | TSMC official statement | 0.95 |
| 2 | 4-8 week recovery estimate | Unconfirmed | Industry contacts, Reuters | 0.60 |
| 3 | Competing fabs at capacity | Confirmed | Samsung, Intel public statements | 0.88 |
| 4 | Spot pricing up 35% | Confirmed | Market data, broker reports | 0.92 |
| 5 | Secondary supply from Japan available | Unconfirmed | Single trade source | 0.40 |

### Impact Assessment
- **Immediate (0-72 hours)**: Supply commitments for Q2 at risk; 3 product launches may delay
- **Short-term (1-4 weeks)**: Inventory buffer covers ~60% of demand; rationing required
- **Medium-term (1-3 months)**: Revenue impact estimated at $45-80M depending on recovery speed
- **Reputational**: Customer SLAs at risk for 12 enterprise accounts

### Decision Tree

**Decision 1: Activate emergency procurement?**
- Yes → Triggers pre-negotiated backup contracts (cost premium 22-30%)
- No → Accept delivery delays, notify affected customers within 48 hours

**Decision 2: Delay Q2 product launches?**
- Delay all → Reduces risk but cedes 3-5 weeks to competitors
- Delay selectively → Launch flagship only, defer secondary SKUs
- Launch on schedule → Requires emergency procurement at premium

**Decision 3: Customer communication strategy?**
- Proactive disclosure → Builds trust, may trigger panic ordering
- Reactive only → Respond to inquiries, buy time for resolution

### Recommended Actions (Priority Order)
1. **Immediately**: Activate backup procurement for top-tier customers (Decision 1: Yes)
2. **Within 24 hours**: Convene supply chain war room; update demand forecast
3. **Within 48 hours**: Selective customer notification for accounts with SLA risk
4. **Within 1 week**: Reassess launch calendar based on updated recovery timeline
5. **Ongoing**: Monitor daily — next brief update scheduled in 24 hours

### Information Gaps
- Exact recovery timeline (TSMC has not confirmed beyond "weeks")
- Whether insurance covers force majeure at negotiated terms
- Competitor response — whether rivals will exploit the disruption

### Escalation Triggers
- If recovery estimate exceeds 8 weeks → escalate to board level
- If spot pricing exceeds 50% premium → trigger long-term contract renegotiation
- If a fourth fab reports impact → activate full business continuity plan

## Quality Checklist
- [ ] Severity and time sensitivity stated in header
- [ ] Facts and unconfirmed reports clearly separated
- [ ] Impact assessed across multiple time horizons
- [ ] Decision tree with clear options and tradeoffs
- [ ] Recommended actions numbered and prioritized by urgency
- [ ] Next update scheduled
- [ ] Information gaps listed honestly
- [ ] Escalation triggers defined with thresholds
