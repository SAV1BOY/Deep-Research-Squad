# Competitor Teardown — Swipe Examples

## Purpose

A competitor teardown is a structured, evidence-based analysis of a single
competitor. It goes deeper than a profile — it reverse-engineers their
strategy, identifies vulnerabilities, and extracts actionable intelligence.

## Gold Standard Structure

### 1. Company Identity
- Name, founded, HQ, employee count, funding/revenue
- One-sentence description of what they actually do (not their marketing)

### 2. Strategy Reverse-Engineering
- **Target customer**: Who they actually serve (not who they say)
- **Value proposition**: What problem they solve, stated plainly
- **Business model**: How they make money, unit economics if available
- **Distribution strategy**: How they acquire customers
- **Moat**: What makes them defensible (network effects, data, switching costs)

### 3. Product Analysis
- Core product capabilities (verified, not marketing claims)
- Product gaps and limitations (from reviews, forums, user interviews)
- Development velocity (release cadence, feature shipping speed)
- Technical architecture if discoverable

### 4. Financial Profile
- Revenue trajectory (public data or estimates)
- Funding history and investor quality
- Burn rate indicators (hiring pace, office expansion)
- Path to profitability assessment

### 5. Vulnerability Assessment
- **Strategic weaknesses**: Misalignment between strategy and market
- **Operational weaknesses**: Execution gaps visible from outside
- **Competitive exposure**: Where they're most vulnerable to attack
- **Trigger events**: What would create an opening against them

## Example Teardown Opening

> **Competitor**: Acme Analytics (Series C, $120M raised, ~400 employees)
> **What they actually do**: Real-time product analytics for mobile apps.
> They've expanded into web analytics but mobile remains 78% of revenue.
>
> **Strategy read**: Acme is pursuing a land-and-expand strategy targeting
> product managers at Series B-D startups. They convert free-tier users
> to paid through usage limits. Their moat is integration depth — average
> customer has 14 Acme event calls per user session.

## Evidence Standards for Teardowns

- Minimum 5 independent sources per competitor
- Distinguish fact from inference explicitly
- Date all data points (competitor data decays fast)
- Flag any data from the competitor's own marketing as "self-reported"
- Cross-reference employee reviews (Glassdoor) with strategic claims

## What Makes a Teardown Actionable

1. Identifies specific vulnerabilities, not vague weaknesses
2. Connects vulnerabilities to your potential actions
3. Predicts competitor reactions to your moves
4. Flags early warning signals to monitor
