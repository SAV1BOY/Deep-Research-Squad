# McKinsey Deep Dive Report — Swipe Example

## Why This Is Gold Standard

McKinsey deep dives follow a pyramid structure: answer first, then supporting arguments,
then data. Every claim is backed by proprietary or triangulated data. The writing is
precise, jargon-free where possible, and ruthlessly focused on "so what?"

## Structure Template

### 1. Executive Summary (1 page max)
- **Situation**: The context that makes this question urgent
- **Complication**: What changed, or what tension exists
- **Resolution**: The answer, stated plainly with confidence level

### 2. Core Findings (3-5 findings, each 1-2 pages)
Each finding follows:
- **Finding statement**: One sentence, declarative
- **Evidence base**: 3+ data points from independent sources
- **Confidence level**: High / Medium / Low with justification
- **Implications**: What this means for the decision at hand

### 3. Data Exhibits
- Each exhibit tells one story
- Title is the takeaway, not a description
- Source and methodology noted at bottom

### 4. Recommendations
- Numbered, prioritized by impact and feasibility
- Each tied explicitly to findings above
- Risk and mitigation noted for each

## What Makes It Work

1. **Answer-first**: Reader knows the conclusion in 30 seconds
2. **MECE structure**: Mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive
3. **Triangulated evidence**: Never a single source for key claims
4. **Quantified where possible**: "Revenue grew 23%" not "Revenue grew significantly"
5. **Action-oriented**: Every section answers "so what do we do?"

## Example Opening Paragraph

> The European SaaS market will reach $142B by 2027, growing at 18% CAGR —
> 3 percentage points faster than North America. This acceleration is driven by
> three factors: delayed digital transformation creating catch-up demand,
> favorable regulatory tailwinds from the EU Data Act, and a maturing local
> venture ecosystem that has tripled Series B funding since 2021.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- Opening with background instead of the answer
- Using hedge words when evidence is strong
- Presenting data without interpretation
- Making recommendations disconnected from findings
- Failing to quantify confidence levels

## Adaptation Notes

When adapting this format for Deep Research Squad outputs:
- Maintain answer-first structure in all deliverables
- Use confidence scoring (0-100) instead of High/Medium/Low
- Add source diversity metrics to evidence sections
- Include contradiction flags where sources disagree
