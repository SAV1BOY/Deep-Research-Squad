# Industry Disruption Deep Dive — Swipe Example

## Why This Is Gold Standard

Industry disruption analysis requires identifying inflection points, mapping incumbent
vulnerabilities, and quantifying the speed and depth of displacement. The best disruption
deep dives avoid hype by anchoring every claim in adoption data, unit economics, and
structural barriers. They distinguish between disruption (market redefinition) and
incremental competition.

## Structure Template

### 1. Executive Summary
- **Disruption thesis**: One-sentence statement of what is being disrupted and by what
- **Stage assessment**: Where this disruption sits on the S-curve (emergence / acceleration / maturity)
- **Timeline**: Expected timeline for mainstream displacement
- **Confidence**: Overall confidence level with justification

### 2. Disruption Mechanics
- **Incumbent value chain**: Map the existing value chain and where value accrues
- **Point of attack**: Where the disruptor enters and why incumbents are slow to respond
- **Enabling technologies**: What made this disruption possible now vs. five years ago
- **Business model shift**: How the economic model changes (e.g., CapEx to OpEx, ownership to access)

### 3. Evidence of Disruption

| Signal | Data Point | Source | Confidence |
|--------|-----------|--------|------------|
| Adoption velocity | 340% YoY growth in enterprise contracts | Gartner, internal data | 0.85 |
| Incumbent response | 3 of top 5 incumbents launched competing products in 18 months | Press releases, SEC filings | 0.92 |
| Talent migration | 28% of senior engineers moved to disruptors in 2024-2025 | LinkedIn Talent Insights | 0.72 |
| Pricing pressure | Average contract value declined 22% across incumbent base | Industry survey (n=480) | 0.78 |
| Regulatory shift | EU framework explicitly accommodates new model | Official regulatory text | 0.95 |

### 4. Incumbent Vulnerability Assessment

**Structural barriers to response:**
1. **Revenue cannibalization** — New model yields 40-60% lower per-unit revenue
2. **Channel conflict** — Existing distribution partners resist transition
3. **Technical debt** — Legacy architecture prevents rapid adaptation
4. **Cultural inertia** — Organization optimized for previous model

**Incumbent response patterns observed:**
- Denial phase (6-12 months): "Our customers want the full-service model"
- Bolt-on phase (12-24 months): Acquire or build a parallel offering
- Restructure phase (24-36 months): Reorganize around new model (if they survive)

### 5. Disruption Depth Scenarios

**Scenario A — Full displacement (probability: 25%)**
Incumbents fail to adapt. Disruptors capture 70%+ of market value within 5 years.
Trigger: regulatory tailwind + capital availability sustained through downturn.

**Scenario B — Bifurcated market (probability: 50%)**
Market splits into legacy (declining) and next-gen (growing) segments. Incumbents
retain high-end, disruptors dominate mid-market and new entrants.
Trigger: incumbents successfully defend top 20% of accounts.

**Scenario C — Absorption (probability: 25%)**
Incumbents acquire key disruptors, integrate technology, maintain market position.
Trigger: disruptor capital constraints + incumbent M&A execution.

### 6. Investment & Strategic Implications
- **For disruptors**: Focus on land-and-expand; avoid competing on incumbents' terms
- **For incumbents**: Separate innovation unit with distinct P&L; accept cannibalization
- **For investors**: Stage of disruption determines risk/return profile
- **For customers**: Dual-vendor strategy during transition; negotiate flexibility clauses

### 7. What We Don't Know
- Whether regulatory environment will tighten or loosen in key markets
- Long-term unit economics at scale for the disruptor model
- Customer switching costs once embedded (lock-in risk transfers)

## Quality Checklist
- [ ] Disruption thesis stated in one sentence
- [ ] S-curve stage identified with supporting evidence
- [ ] Incumbent vulnerability mapped structurally, not anecdotally
- [ ] At least three scenarios with assigned probabilities summing to ~100%
- [ ] Evidence table with 5+ signals from independent sources
- [ ] "What We Don't Know" section present and honest
- [ ] Recommendations tied to specific stakeholder types
