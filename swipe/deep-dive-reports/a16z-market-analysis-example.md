# a16z Market Analysis — Swipe Example

## Why This Is Gold Standard

Andreessen Horowitz market analyses combine rigorous data with bold theses.
They identify inflection points before consensus, back claims with proprietary
data from their portfolio, and write for builders — not just investors.

## Structure Template

### 1. The Big Idea (1 paragraph)
State the thesis in one bold, falsifiable sentence. Then add 2-3 sentences
of context for why now.

### 2. Market Sizing
- **Top-down**: TAM from industry reports, cross-referenced
- **Bottom-up**: Unit economics × addressable customers
- **Wedge analysis**: Which segment enters first and expands how

### 3. Landscape Map
- Incumbents and their moats
- Emerging challengers and their wedges
- Adjacent markets that could converge

### 4. Inflection Points
- Technology shifts enabling new approaches
- Regulatory changes opening or closing doors
- Behavioral shifts in buyer/user patterns

### 5. What Would Have to Be True
- For the bull case to play out
- For the bear case to materialize
- Key assumptions that could break either way

## Example Market Sizing Approach

> **Top-down**: Global enterprise software spend is $685B (Gartner 2024).
> Developer tools represent ~4.2% historically = $28.8B.
> AI-assisted development is capturing 12% of that = $3.5B today.
>
> **Bottom-up**: 27M professional developers × $1,200 avg annual tool spend
> × 15% adoption of AI tools = $4.9B.
>
> **Delta analysis**: Bottom-up exceeds top-down by 40%, suggesting either
> faster adoption than industry reports capture or price premiums not yet
> reflected in aggregate data.

## What Makes It Work

1. **Contrarian but right**: Takes a position, doesn't hedge everything
2. **Builder perspective**: Written for people making things, not just funding
3. **Data + narrative**: Numbers serve the story, story contextualizes numbers
4. **Time-aware**: Explicit about "why now" not just "why"
5. **Honest about uncertainty**: "What would have to be true" framework

## Adaptation for Deep Research Squad

- Use the "What Would Have to Be True" framework for all market analyses
- Apply dual top-down/bottom-up sizing as standard practice
- Flag delta between approaches as a research quality signal
- Integrate inflection point analysis into timeline deliverables
- Maintain the bold-thesis-first approach while noting confidence levels
