# Bellingcat OSINT Investigations

## Why This Is Iconic

Bellingcat, founded by Eliot Higgins in 2014, demonstrated that open-source
intelligence (OSINT) conducted by civilians could match or exceed traditional
investigative journalism and even government intelligence. Their work on
MH17, the Skripal poisoning, and Syrian chemical attacks set new standards.

## Core OSINT Methodology

### 1. Digital Verification
Every piece of digital evidence must be verified:
- **Geolocation**: Match images/video to specific locations using landmarks,
  satellite imagery, sun position, and environmental features
- **Chronolocation**: Determine when something happened using shadows,
  weather data, celestial positions, metadata
- **Reverse image search**: Verify images aren't recycled from other events
- **Metadata analysis**: Extract EXIF data, check for manipulation

### 2. Source Triangulation
No single source is trusted alone:
- Cross-reference social media posts from multiple independent witnesses
- Compare citizen reports with satellite imagery
- Match ground-level evidence with aerial/satellite views
- Verify through open databases (flight tracking, ship tracking, etc.)

### 3. Chain of Evidence
Maintain evidentiary chain:
- Archive all sources at time of discovery (Wayback Machine, screenshots)
- Document the discovery process step by step
- Preserve metadata and context
- Enable independent verification by publishing methodology

### 4. Database Cross-Referencing
Combine multiple open databases:
- Vehicle registration databases
- Flight tracking (FlightRadar24, ADS-B Exchange)
- Ship tracking (MarineTraffic)
- Corporate registries
- Social media accounts and connections
- Phone number databases and call records (where legally available)

## Landmark Investigations

### MH17 Investigation (2014-2018)
- Traced the Buk missile launcher from Russia to eastern Ukraine and back
- Used social media posts, satellite imagery, and vehicle identification
- Results independently confirmed by Dutch Safety Board and JIT investigation

### Skripal Poisoning (2018)
- Identified GRU agents using passport databases and travel records
- Cross-referenced with military unit databases
- Revealed real identities of agents using pseudonyms

### Syrian Chemical Attacks
- Verified chemical weapon use through open-source evidence
- Geolocated munitions and impact sites
- Matched evidence to specific military units

## Lessons for Deep Research Squad

### What We Adopt
- Verification-first mindset: nothing is true until verified
- Source archiving: preserve evidence at time of discovery
- Methodology transparency: publish how conclusions were reached
- Multi-source triangulation as standard practice

### What We Adapt
- Geolocation techniques → source verification protocols
- Chain of evidence → evidence table audit trails
- Database cross-referencing → source map methodology
- Open collaboration → multi-agent research with shared evidence

### OSINT Ethics
- All information must be legally obtainable
- Privacy considerations for individuals not in public life
- Responsible disclosure of sensitive findings
- No misrepresentation to obtain information
