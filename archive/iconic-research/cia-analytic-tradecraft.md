# CIA Analytic Tradecraft

## Why This Is Iconic

The CIA's analytic tradecraft, especially as codified in "Psychology of
Intelligence Analysis" by Richards Heuer and the "Tradecraft Primer,"
represents decades of hard-won lessons about analyzing ambiguous information
under uncertainty — with life-or-death consequences for errors.

## Core Tradecraft Principles

### 1. Analysis of Competing Hypotheses (ACH)
The signature CIA analytical method:
1. Identify all plausible hypotheses (not just the leading one)
2. List all significant evidence
3. For each piece of evidence, assess consistency with each hypothesis
4. Focus on disconfirming evidence (not confirming)
5. The best hypothesis is the one with least disconfirming evidence
6. Analyze sensitivity: how much does removing one piece of evidence change the ranking?

### 2. Structured Analytic Techniques (SATs)
- **Devil's Advocacy**: Deliberately argue against the prevailing view
- **Team A/Team B**: Two teams independently analyze the same question
- **Red Teaming**: Assume the adversary's perspective
- **Key Assumptions Check**: Explicitly list and challenge all assumptions
- **What-If Analysis**: Explore low-probability, high-impact scenarios

### 3. Cognitive Bias Awareness
Heuer identified key biases that plague intelligence analysis:
- **Anchoring**: Over-relying on first information received
- **Confirmation bias**: Seeking evidence that confirms existing beliefs
- **Mirror imaging**: Assuming others think like you do
- **Availability bias**: Overweighting easily recalled information
- **Groupthink**: Conforming to team consensus

### 4. Confidence and Uncertainty
CIA uses specific language for confidence levels:
- "We assess with high confidence" = based on high-quality, corroborating sources
- "We assess with moderate confidence" = plausibly supported but with gaps
- "We assess with low confidence" = fragmentary, poorly corroborated

## Key Publications

1. **"Psychology of Intelligence Analysis"** — Richards Heuer (1999)
2. **"Tradecraft Primer"** — CIA Center for the Study of Intelligence
3. **"Structured Analytic Techniques"** — Heuer & Pherson (2010)

## Lessons for Deep Research Squad

### What We Adopt
- Analysis of Competing Hypotheses for contested claims
- Key Assumptions Check as standard practice
- Cognitive bias awareness training for all agents
- Explicit confidence language with defined meanings
- Focus on disconfirming evidence

### What We Adapt
- ACH → contradiction map and evidence table methodology
- Devil's Advocacy → contrarian analyst agent role
- Red Teaming → scenario planning with adversarial scenarios
- Confidence levels → 0-100 confidence scoring system

### What We Learn From Their Failures
- Iraq WMD intelligence failure: confirmation bias at scale
- Groupthink can override good methodology
- Politicization of analysis corrupts outputs
- Complexity of the real world exceeds any framework
