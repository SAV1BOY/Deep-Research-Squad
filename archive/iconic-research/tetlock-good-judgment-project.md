# Tetlock's Good Judgment Project

## Why This Is Iconic

Philip Tetlock's research, spanning "Expert Political Judgment" (2005)
through the "Good Judgment Project" (2011-present), fundamentally changed
how we think about forecasting accuracy. He proved that some people
("superforecasters") consistently outperform experts and even intelligence
analysts with classified data.

## Key Findings

### 1. Foxes Beat Hedgehogs
From "Expert Political Judgment":
- **Hedgehogs**: Know one big thing, apply it to everything. Confident, dramatic.
- **Foxes**: Know many small things, synthesize across domains. Cautious, nuanced.
- Foxes significantly outperform hedgehogs in forecasting accuracy.
- Media rewards hedgehog confidence, not fox accuracy.

### 2. Superforecasters
The Good Judgment Project identified traits of top forecasters:
- **Actively open-minded**: Seek out disconfirming evidence
- **Granular thinkers**: Distinguish between 60% and 65% probability
- **Perpetual updaters**: Constantly revise beliefs with new evidence
- **Team players**: Synthesize diverse perspectives effectively
- **Self-critical**: Track and learn from their mistakes
- **Numerate**: Comfortable with probabilistic thinking

### 3. Calibration
Superforecasters are well-calibrated:
- When they say 70% likely, it happens ~70% of the time
- Most experts are overconfident (70% predictions right only 50%)
- Calibration is a learnable skill through feedback

### 4. The Key Practices
What superforecasters actually do:
1. Start with the base rate (outside view)
2. Adjust for specific factors (inside view)
3. Make many small updates rather than few large ones
4. Seek disconfirming evidence actively
5. Decompose questions into sub-components
6. Use precise probability estimates
7. Track their track record

## Good Judgment Project Results

- IARPA tournament: Superforecasters beat intelligence analysts by 30%
- Beat prediction markets by 15-20%
- Teams of superforecasters beat individual superforecasters
- Effect persisted over 4 years of the tournament

## Lessons for Deep Research Squad

### What We Adopt
- Fox mindset: synthesize across domains, embrace nuance
- Calibrated confidence: 0-100 scores must be well-calibrated
- Base rate thinking: always start with the outside view
- Continuous updating: revise as new evidence appears
- Track record keeping: log predictions and review accuracy

### What We Build Into Process
- Confidence scoring script embeds calibration principles
- Contrarian analyst role prevents groupthink
- Evidence tables force engagement with disconfirming evidence
- Quality tracker enables long-term accuracy assessment
- Decomposition of complex questions into sub-components

### Key Warning
- Even superforecasters struggle beyond 1-2 year horizons
- Accuracy degrades rapidly with time horizon
- Acknowledge forecast horizon limitations in all deliverables
