# Evolution of Evidence-Based Practice

## Medicine → Policy → Business → Everything

This document traces how evidence-based practice spread from medicine
to virtually every professional domain, transforming how decisions are made.

## Origin: Evidence-Based Medicine (1990s)

### The Revolution
- **1992**: Gordon Guyatt coins "evidence-based medicine" (EBM)
- **Core idea**: Clinical decisions should be based on the best available
  evidence, not just tradition, authority, or clinical experience
- **Cochrane Collaboration** (1993): Systematic reviews as gold standard

### Evidence Hierarchy (Medical)
1. Systematic reviews and meta-analyses
2. Randomized controlled trials (RCTs)
3. Cohort studies
4. Case-control studies
5. Case series and case reports
6. Expert opinion

### Impact
- Transformed medical training and practice
- Created infrastructure for evidence synthesis
- Reduced reliance on authority and tradition
- Established systematic review as the highest level of evidence

## Expansion: Evidence-Based Policy (2000s)

### Key Developments
- **What Works Centres** (UK): Government-funded evidence synthesis
- **J-PAL** (MIT): Randomized evaluations for development policy
- **Campbell Collaboration**: Cochrane equivalent for social science
- **Nudge Units**: Behavioral science applied to policy design

### Adaptation
- RCTs applied to social programs and policy interventions
- Cost-effectiveness analysis for resource allocation
- Systematic reviews of policy evidence
- Evidence gap maps for policy domains

### Challenges
- Not everything can be randomized (ethics, feasibility)
- Context matters more in policy than medicine
- Political incentives often override evidence
- Publication bias skews available evidence

## Further Expansion: Evidence-Based Management (2010s)

### Key Developments
- **Center for Evidence-Based Management**: Applying EBM to business
- **People analytics**: HR decisions based on data, not intuition
- **A/B testing culture**: Technology companies testing everything
- **Decision science**: Behavioral economics in organizations

### Business Evidence Hierarchy
1. Systematic reviews of business research
2. Well-designed organizational studies
3. Industry benchmarking data
4. Expert analysis and case studies
5. Best practices (experience-based)
6. Gut instinct and authority

### Challenges
- Business contexts are more variable than clinical settings
- Fewer high-quality business studies than medical studies
- Proprietary data limits public evidence base
- Speed requirements conflict with rigorous evidence gathering

## Current State: Evidence-Based Everything (2020s)

### Domains Now Applying Evidence-Based Practice
- Education (What Works Clearinghouse)
- Criminal justice (Campbell Collaboration)
- Environmental policy (IPCC methodology)
- Technology (A/B testing, ML evaluation)
- Philanthropy (GiveWell, effective altruism)
- Personal decision-making (quantified self)

## Implications for Deep Research Squad

1. Apply evidence hierarchy appropriate to each domain
2. Systematic review principles adapted for speed (rapid reviews)
3. Always state the evidence level supporting each claim
4. Acknowledge that business evidence is inherently weaker than medical
5. Use confidence scoring to make evidence quality transparent
6. Combine multiple evidence types when gold-standard evidence is unavailable
