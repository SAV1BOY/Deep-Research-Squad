# Evolution of Research Methods

## Library Era → Internet Era → AI-Assisted Era

This document traces how research methodology has evolved through three
major paradigm shifts, each expanding access while creating new challenges.

## Era 1: Library Research (Pre-1990s)

### Methods
- Physical card catalogs and index systems
- Manual literature searches through print journals
- Interlibrary loan for rare materials
- Expert consultation through personal networks
- Government document repositories

### Strengths
- Deep engagement with sources (reading cover to cover)
- Strong institutional curation (librarians as gatekeepers)
- High signal-to-noise ratio in indexed materials
- Citation chains were reliable (peer-reviewed publications)

### Limitations
- Access limited by geography and institutional affiliation
- Search was slow (days to weeks for comprehensive review)
- Recency gap (publishing lag of months to years)
- Bias toward English-language, Western sources
- Duplication of effort across researchers

## Era 2: Internet Research (1990s-2010s)

### Key Milestones
- 1991: World Wide Web launches
- 1998: Google transforms search
- 2004: Google Scholar indexes academic literature
- 2008: Semantic Scholar and citation graph tools
- 2010s: Open access movement gains momentum

### Methods
- Keyword search across billions of documents
- Database access (JSTOR, PubMed, SSRN)
- Real-time news monitoring
- Social media as primary source material
- Collaborative tools (Google Docs, Zotero, Mendeley)

### Strengths
- Massive expansion of accessible sources
- Speed of search (seconds, not days)
- Global reach and multilingual access
- Real-time information availability
- Collaborative research capabilities

### Limitations
- Information overload (too much, not too little)
- Quality filtering becomes researcher's problem
- SEO gaming distorts search results
- Echo chambers and filter bubbles
- Misinformation scales as fast as information

## Era 3: AI-Assisted Research (2020s-Present)

### Key Milestones
- 2020: GPT-3 demonstrates language understanding
- 2022: ChatGPT brings AI to mainstream research
- 2023: Specialized research AI tools emerge
- 2024: Multi-agent research systems appear
- 2025: Autonomous research capabilities developing

### Methods
- AI-powered literature review and synthesis
- Automated source discovery and verification
- Multi-agent research workflows
- Real-time evidence table construction
- Confidence scoring and contradiction detection

### Strengths
- Synthesis across thousands of sources simultaneously
- Pattern detection humans would miss
- 24/7 operation with consistent methodology
- Scalable without proportional cost increase
- Structured output with transparency

### Limitations
- Hallucination risk (AI can fabricate plausible sources)
- Training data cutoffs create blind spots
- Over-reliance on AI without human judgment
- Bias amplification from training data
- Verification remains human-essential

## Implications for Deep Research Squad

We operate at the frontier of Era 3. Our methodology must:
1. Leverage AI speed and synthesis while maintaining human verification standards
2. Apply Era 1 depth of engagement to Era 3 breadth of access
3. Use Era 2 database infrastructure with AI-powered analysis
4. Never sacrifice accuracy for speed
5. Document methodology transparently (an Era 1 virtue often lost in Era 2-3)
