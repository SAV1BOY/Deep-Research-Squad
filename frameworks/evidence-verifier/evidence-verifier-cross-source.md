# Cross-Source Verification Framework

## Purpose
Systematically check whether claims are consistent across independent sources. When sources agree, confidence increases. When they disagree, the framework guides investigation into the reasons for disagreement and provides methods for resolution.

## When to Use
- When a critical claim needs corroboration beyond a single source.
- When two or more sources present conflicting information.
- When assessing the corroboration column in the Claim Grid.
- When the research output will be used for high-stakes decisions requiring robust evidence.

## Inputs
- A specific claim to verify.
- Two or more sources that address the same claim.
- Source metadata (type, methodology, date, author, independence status).

## Process

### Step 1: Confirm Source Independence
- Before treating agreement as corroboration, verify that the sources are truly independent.
- Check: Do they share the same original data source?
- Check: Did one source cite or derive from the other?
- Check: Were they produced by the same organization, research group, or funding body?
- Use the Publication Chain framework to trace origins.
- Only independent sources provide genuine corroboration.

### Step 2: Standardize the Claim Across Sources
- Each source may phrase the claim differently.
- Restate what each source claims in a standardized format.
- Identify the core factual assertion, separated from interpretation or framing.
- Document the exact wording from each source for comparison.

### Step 3: Classify Agreement or Disagreement
- **Full agreement**: Sources make the same core claim with consistent details.
- **Partial agreement**: Sources agree on the core claim but differ on details, magnitude, or caveats.
- **Direct contradiction**: Sources make mutually incompatible claims.
- **Apparent contradiction**: Sources seem to disagree but may be addressing different aspects, time periods, or definitions.

### Step 4: When Sources Agree — Strengthen Confidence
- Corroboration from two independent sources: upgrade confidence one level.
- Corroboration from three or more independent sources: upgrade confidence two levels.
- Check that agreement is not due to shared origin (circular corroboration).
- Document which specific sources corroborate and their independence status.

### Step 5: When Sources Disagree — Investigate
Apply the following diagnostic sequence:

**5a: Check Definitions**
- Are the sources using the same definitions and measurements?
- Disagreement often dissolves when definitional differences are identified.

**5b: Check Methodology**
- Compare methodologies (sample size, time period, data collection method).
- Methodological differences often explain quantitative disagreements.

**5c: Check Time Period**
- Are the sources from different time periods when conditions may have changed?
- What was true in 2020 may not be true in 2025.

**5d: Check Scope**
- Are the sources covering the same geographic, demographic, or market scope?
- A global finding may contradict a regional finding without either being wrong.

**5e: Check for Bias**
- Does either source have a known bias, conflict of interest, or incentive to reach a particular conclusion?
- Bias does not automatically invalidate a source but does reduce its weight.

### Step 6: Resolve Disagreements
- If the disagreement is explained by definitions, methodology, time, or scope: document the explanation and present both findings with context.
- If the disagreement is genuine and unexplained: present both positions, weight them by evidence strength, and flag the uncertainty.
- Never silently ignore contradictory evidence. Always surface it.

### Step 7: Update the Claim Grid
- Record the corroboration status and supporting detail.
- Adjust confidence levels based on agreement or disagreement findings.
- Note any unresolved contradictions for the final output.

## Outputs
- A corroboration assessment for each critical claim.
- Independence verification for each source pair.
- Disagreement analysis with diagnostic findings when sources conflict.
- Updated confidence levels in the Claim Grid.

## Common Pitfalls
- Treating citation-linked sources as independent corroboration.
- Ignoring disagreements because resolving them is time-consuming.
- Assuming the majority view is correct without checking methodology.
- Not standardizing claims before comparing, leading to false disagreements.
- Weighting sources by prestige rather than by evidence quality when they disagree.
- Presenting only agreeing sources and suppressing contradictory evidence.

## Related Frameworks
- **Publication Chain**: Essential for verifying source independence.
- **Claim Grid**: Cross-source findings populate the corroboration column.
- **Citation Integrity**: Ensures compared claims are accurately attributed.
- **Signal-Noise Scoring**: Source quality scores help weigh conflicting evidence.
- **Falsification**: Disagreements may represent natural falsification attempts.
