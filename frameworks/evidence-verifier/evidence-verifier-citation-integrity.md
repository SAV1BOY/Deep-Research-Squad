# Citation Integrity Framework

## Purpose
Verify that citation chains are intact and faithful. Ensures that when a source is cited as evidence for a claim, the cited source actually says what is claimed, the context is preserved, and the citation has not been distorted through paraphrasing, selective quoting, or misattribution.

## When to Use
- When verifying high-stakes or surprising claims before including them in research output.
- When a claim's wording feels different from what the cited source would likely say.
- When multiple secondary sources attribute different claims to the same primary source.
- When building the evidence base for contested or controversial topics.

## Inputs
- A claim and its cited source(s).
- Access to the cited source (full text preferred, abstract as fallback).
- The context in which the citation is used.

## Process

### Step 1: Locate the Exact Passage
- Find the specific passage in the cited source that the claim references.
- If the source is long, use the page number, section, or figure referenced.
- If no specific location is given, search the source for the key terms of the claim.
- Record the exact passage for comparison.

### Step 2: Compare Claim to Source — Content Check
- Does the cited source actually make this claim?
- Is the claim a direct quote, a paraphrase, or an interpretation?
- If paraphrased, does the paraphrase preserve the original meaning?
- Rate content fidelity: **Faithful**, **Minor drift**, **Significant distortion**, **Fabricated**.

### Step 3: Compare Claim to Source — Context Check
- Read the surrounding context in the cited source.
- Does the cited source present this claim as:
  - A confirmed finding or a tentative hypothesis?
  - A general statement or one with important caveats?
  - The author's own view or a view they are reporting or critiquing?
- Has the citing source stripped away important qualifiers or conditions?
- Rate context preservation: **Full context**, **Partial context**, **Context lost**, **Context reversed**.

### Step 4: Check for Selective Quoting
- Did the citing source cherry-pick a favorable passage while ignoring contradictory passages in the same source?
- Does the cited source present a balanced view that has been reduced to a one-sided claim?
- Look for phrases in the original like "however," "on the other hand," "with the caveat that" near the cited passage.

### Step 5: Verify Source Authenticity
- Is the cited source genuine? (Especially important for URLs, preprints, or sources in unfamiliar repositories.)
- Has the cited source been retracted, corrected, or updated since the citation was made?
- Is the version cited the most current version?
- Check retraction databases for academic papers.

### Step 6: Check Attribution Accuracy
- Is the claim attributed to the correct author(s)?
- Is the publication date correct?
- Is the journal, publisher, or platform correctly identified?
- Are there co-authors or contributors who should be credited?

### Step 7: Assign Integrity Rating
Based on the checks above, assign an overall citation integrity rating:
- **Verified**: Content faithful, context preserved, source authentic, attribution correct.
- **Minor issues**: Small paraphrasing drift or minor context omission; does not change the core meaning.
- **Significant issues**: Meaningful distortion of content or context; the claim as stated is misleading.
- **Failed**: The cited source does not support the claim, the source is fabricated, or attribution is wrong.

### Step 8: Document and Flag
- Record the integrity rating and supporting evidence for each checked citation.
- Flag citations rated "Significant issues" or "Failed" for correction or removal.
- Note citations that could not be checked (source unavailable) and flag as unverified.

## Outputs
- An integrity rating for each checked citation.
- Exact quotes from cited sources for comparison.
- Flags for problematic citations requiring correction.
- A list of citations that could not be verified due to access limitations.

## Common Pitfalls
- Assuming citations are accurate because the citing source is reputable.
- Checking only the abstract of the cited source instead of the relevant passage.
- Not verifying whether the cited source has been retracted or corrected.
- Accepting paraphrases as faithful without comparing to the original text.
- Skipping context checks because the content check passed.
- Not checking attribution details (wrong author, wrong year, wrong publication).

## Related Frameworks
- **Publication Chain**: Citation integrity is checked at each link in the chain.
- **Claim Grid**: Integrity ratings feed into the strength and confidence columns.
- **Cross-Source Verification**: If one citation fails integrity, check if the claim survives on other evidence.
- **Ladder Application**: A claim's evidence strength depends on its citation being intact.
- **Falsification**: Failed citations may invalidate claims that appeared well-supported.
