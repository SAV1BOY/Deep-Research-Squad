# Layered Synthesis Framework

## Purpose

Synthesize research findings into clearly separated layers of certainty. Layer 1 contains established facts, Layer 2 contains probable conclusions drawn from those facts, and Layer 3 contains uncertain implications that extend beyond the evidence. Each layer is explicitly labeled so the reader always knows the confidence level of what they are reading.

## When to Use

- When research produces a mix of hard facts, reasonable inferences, and speculative possibilities.
- When the audience needs to distinguish between what is known and what is interpreted.
- When decision-makers need to understand which conclusions they can rely on and which carry risk.
- When presenting findings to stakeholders with different risk tolerances.

## Inputs

- Verified facts and data points from research.
- Analytical conclusions drawn from those facts.
- Speculative implications that extend beyond direct evidence.
- Source quality assessments for each piece of information.
- Confidence ratings for each conclusion.

## Process

### Step 1: Evidence Sorting
Separate all research findings into three initial buckets:
- **Hard evidence**: Directly observed, multiply confirmed, or from authoritative primary sources.
- **Derived conclusions**: Logical inferences from hard evidence, supported by reasoning but not directly observed.
- **Speculative extensions**: Possible implications that go beyond what the evidence strictly supports.

### Step 2: Layer 1 Construction - Certain Facts
Assemble Layer 1 from hard evidence only. Each fact must meet these criteria:
- Confirmed by at least two independent sources, or from a single authoritative primary source.
- Not contradicted by any credible evidence.
- Stated without interpretive language (no "suggests," "implies," or "indicates").

### Step 3: Layer 2 Construction - Probable Conclusions
Build Layer 2 from derived conclusions. Each conclusion must:
- Be logically derived from Layer 1 facts (cite the specific facts).
- Have the reasoning chain made explicit.
- Acknowledge any assumptions required.
- Be assigned a probability estimate (likely, probable, more likely than not).

### Step 4: Layer 3 Construction - Uncertain Implications
Construct Layer 3 from speculative extensions. Each implication must:
- Be clearly labeled as uncertain or speculative.
- State what additional evidence would confirm or refute it.
- Identify what assumptions it depends on.
- Note competing alternative implications.

### Step 5: Cross-Layer Validation
Verify that Layer 2 conclusions do not contradict Layer 1 facts. Verify that Layer 3 implications are at least consistent with Layers 1 and 2. Flag any tensions.

### Step 6: Dependency Mapping
For each Layer 2 and Layer 3 item, explicitly list which Layer 1 facts it depends on. If a Layer 1 fact were to be overturned, which higher-layer conclusions would fall?

### Step 7: Presentation Assembly
Structure the final output with clear visual or textual separation between layers. Use consistent labeling throughout.

## Outputs

- **Layer 1 section**: Numbered list of confirmed facts with source citations.
- **Layer 2 section**: Numbered list of probable conclusions, each referencing its Layer 1 foundations.
- **Layer 3 section**: Numbered list of uncertain implications with stated assumptions and conditions.
- **Dependency map**: Shows which higher-layer items depend on which lower-layer items.
- **Confidence summary**: Overall assessment of how much of the synthesis rests on solid ground.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Layer inflation**: Promoting uncertain conclusions to Layer 1 because they feel right. Apply criteria strictly.
- **Layer deflation**: Demoting well-supported conclusions to Layer 3 out of excessive caution, making the synthesis less useful.
- **Missing assumptions**: Failing to state the assumptions that bridge Layer 1 facts to Layer 2 conclusions.
- **Orphan conclusions**: Layer 2 or 3 items that do not trace back to any Layer 1 fact.
- **False precision**: Assigning probability percentages when the evidence only supports qualitative confidence levels.
- **Ignoring contradictions**: Placing contradictory items in different layers without acknowledging the tension.
