# Executive Compression Framework

## Purpose

Compress extensive research findings into a one-page executive format that busy decision-makers can absorb in under five minutes. The format leads with the answer, supports it with key evidence, acknowledges uncertainty honestly, and ends with a clear recommendation.

## When to Use

- When presenting research results to executives or senior decision-makers.
- When the audience has limited time and needs the essential conclusions immediately.
- When a full research report exists but a summary is needed for decision meetings.
- When multiple stakeholders need the same bottom line without wading through details.

## Inputs

- Completed research findings, ideally already synthesized.
- The original research question or decision context.
- Knowledge of the audience and what decisions they face.
- Assessment of key uncertainties and their implications.

## Process

### Step 1: Identify the Core Answer
Distill all research into a single sentence that answers the original question. This sentence will be the first thing the reader sees. If you cannot write this sentence, you have not finished your analysis.

### Step 2: Select Supporting Evidence
Choose three to five pieces of evidence that most strongly support the core answer. Selection criteria:
- Directly relevant to the answer (not tangentially interesting).
- From credible, verifiable sources.
- Quantitative where possible, qualitative where necessary.
- Diverse (not all from the same source or type).

### Step 3: Construct the Uncertainty Statement
Write a concise statement of what you do not know or are not sure about. Include:
- The most important gap in the evidence.
- The assumption most likely to be wrong.
- The condition under which the core answer would change.

### Step 4: Formulate the Recommendation
State what the reader should do based on this research. A recommendation must be:
- Actionable (something someone can actually do).
- Specific (not "consider options" but "pursue Option A").
- Time-bound (when should action be taken).
- Conditional if appropriate ("If X, then do Y; if not X, then do Z").

### Step 5: Apply the Compression Rules
- **Total length**: One page maximum, roughly 400-500 words.
- **No jargon**: Use plain language or define technical terms inline.
- **No hedging without substance**: "This might possibly perhaps be relevant" becomes "This is uncertain because [specific reason]."
- **Active voice**: "Revenue declined 15%" not "A decline of 15% in revenue was observed."
- **Numbers over adjectives**: "37% increase" not "significant increase."

### Step 6: Structure the Page
Arrange in this exact order:
1. **Bottom Line** (1-2 sentences): The core answer.
2. **Key Evidence** (3-5 bullet points): Supporting facts.
3. **Uncertainty** (1-2 sentences): What could change this conclusion.
4. **Recommendation** (1-3 sentences): What to do next.
5. **For More Detail** (optional): Pointer to full research report.

### Step 7: Peer Review Test
Read the compressed output as if you know nothing about the topic. Does the bottom line make sense without context? Does the evidence support it? Is the recommendation clear?

## Outputs

- A single-page executive summary following the Bottom Line / Evidence / Uncertainty / Recommendation structure.
- No more than 500 words.
- Self-contained: readable without reference to the full report.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Burying the lead**: Putting context and methodology before the answer. Lead with the answer.
- **Evidence dumping**: Including too many supporting points. Five is the maximum; three is often better.
- **False confidence**: Omitting uncertainty to seem more decisive. Executives respect honesty.
- **Vague recommendations**: "Further research is needed" is not a recommendation. State what action to take now, even if it is "wait for X before deciding."
- **Oversimplification**: Compression is not the same as dumbing down. Preserve nuance through precise language.
- **Format creep**: Allowing the summary to expand beyond one page. Enforce the constraint ruthlessly.
