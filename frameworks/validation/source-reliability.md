# Source Reliability

## Purpose
A structured framework for assessing the overall reliability of a source across multiple dimensions. While Evidence Rating evaluates individual claims, Source Reliability evaluates the entity producing the evidence. This assessment informs how much weight to give all outputs from a particular source.

## When to Use
- When encountering a new or unfamiliar source for the first time
- When a source is central to the research and its credibility needs formal assessment
- When two sources conflict and you need to determine which to trust more
- When building a source library for ongoing research in a domain

## Inputs
- The source identity: organization, publication, author, or platform
- Historical outputs from the source for track record assessment
- Information about the source's funding, ownership, and affiliations
- The source's stated methodology and editorial standards

## Process

### Factor 1: Track Record
Assess the source's historical accuracy and reliability:
- Has the source been consistently accurate in past reporting or analysis?
- Have past claims been verified or refuted by subsequent evidence?
- Does the source issue corrections when errors are identified?
- Score: Strong (consistent accuracy) / Moderate (mostly accurate) / Weak (frequent errors)

### Factor 2: Methodology
Evaluate the rigor of the source's methods:
- Does the source describe how it gathers and analyzes information?
- Are methods appropriate for the claims being made?
- Is data collection systematic or anecdotal?
- Does the source distinguish between facts, analysis, and opinion?
- Score: Strong (rigorous, transparent) / Moderate (adequate, partially described) / Weak (unclear or absent)

### Factor 3: Independence
Assess freedom from conflicts of interest:
- Who funds or owns the source? Could funding influence conclusions?
- Does the source have financial, political, or ideological interests in the topic?
- Does the source disclose potential conflicts?
- Is there a pattern of conclusions favoring a particular party?
- Score: Strong (independent, disclosed) / Moderate (some ties, mostly disclosed) / Weak (significant conflicts, undisclosed)

### Factor 4: Transparency
Evaluate how open the source is about its processes:
- Does the source cite its own sources and data?
- Are datasets, methodologies, and assumptions accessible?
- Does the source allow scrutiny and respond to critiques?
- Is the source's editorial or review process visible?
- Score: Strong (fully transparent) / Moderate (partially transparent) / Weak (opaque)

### Composite Assessment
Combine the four factors into an overall reliability rating:
- **Highly Reliable**: Strong across all four factors
- **Generally Reliable**: Strong in most factors, moderate in others
- **Conditionally Reliable**: Mixed scores; reliable for some topics but not others
- **Unreliable**: Weak in two or more factors
- **Disqualified**: Demonstrated fabrication, persistent undisclosed conflicts, or systematic deception

## Outputs
- A reliability profile for the source with scores across all four factors
- A composite reliability rating with justification
- Specific caveats or conditions under which reliability may vary
- Recommendations for how to use the source in the research (primary, corroborative, or excluded)

## Common Pitfalls
- **Halo effect**: Assuming a source reliable in one domain is reliable in all domains. Assess per topic.
- **Dismissing unfamiliar sources**: New or niche sources may be highly reliable. Assess on criteria, not familiarity.
- **Ignoring institutional incentives**: Even well-regarded institutions have incentives that shape output. Always check independence.
- **Outdating assessments**: Source reliability can change over time due to ownership changes, staff turnover, or policy shifts. Reassess periodically.
- **Conflating popularity with reliability**: Widely cited sources are not necessarily accurate. Popularity is not a reliability indicator.
- **Forgetting to check corrections**: A source that issues corrections is more reliable than one that never acknowledges errors.
