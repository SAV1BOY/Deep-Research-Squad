# Recommendation Protocol Framework

## Purpose

Structure a complete, defensible recommendation that follows a disciplined path from situation assessment through options evaluation to a clear recommended action with rationale, risk acknowledgment, contingency plans, and measurable success criteria. This protocol ensures that recommendations are actionable, transparent, and accountable.

## When to Use

- When research and analysis are complete and it is time to recommend a course of action.
- When the recommendation must be presented to decision-makers who will scrutinize the logic.
- When accountability requires a clear record of what was recommended, why, and what was considered.
- When the decision is significant enough that an informal suggestion is insufficient.

## Inputs

- Completed research and analysis (ideally from prior framework applications).
- Options that have been evaluated (from decision-analyst-options-matrix.md or equivalent).
- Risk and trade-off analysis (from decision-analyst-risk-return.md and trade-off-table.md).
- Understanding of the audience, their authority, and their constraints.

## Process

### Step 1: Situation Statement
Describe the current situation in 3-5 sentences:
- What is happening that requires a decision?
- Why does it matter (stakes, urgency, consequences of inaction)?
- What constraints exist (time, budget, politics, technical)?
- What has changed that makes this decision necessary now?
Do not pad this section. It is context, not the recommendation.

### Step 2: Options Evaluated
Summarize the options that were considered. For each:
- One-sentence description.
- Key advantage.
- Key disadvantage.
- Why it was or was not selected.
Include at least three options to demonstrate thorough consideration. Always include the status quo.

### Step 3: Recommended Action
State the recommendation clearly and specifically:
- What exactly should be done?
- Who should do it?
- When should it be started and completed?
- What resources are required?
The recommendation must be actionable. "Consider further analysis" is not a recommendation. "Commission a three-week analysis by Team X focusing on Y with a budget of Z" is.

### Step 4: Rationale
Explain why this option was selected over alternatives. The rationale must:
- Reference specific evidence and analysis, not just intuition.
- Address why the key advantages of rejected options were insufficient to outweigh their disadvantages.
- Connect to the stated objectives and constraints.
- Be honest about judgment calls where data was insufficient.

### Step 5: Risk Acknowledgment
State what could go wrong with the recommended action:
- What are the top 3-5 risks?
- For each risk: probability (high/medium/low), impact (high/medium/low), and proposed mitigation.
- What is the worst-case outcome if the recommendation is followed?
- Is the worst case survivable?
Hiding risks undermines credibility. Acknowledging them builds trust.

### Step 6: Contingency Plans
For each major risk, define a contingency:
- **Trigger**: What event or signal indicates the risk is materializing?
- **Response**: What action should be taken if the trigger fires?
- **Decision point**: When should the recommendation be abandoned in favor of an alternative?
- **Fallback option**: Which alternative option should be pursued if the recommendation fails?

### Step 7: Success Criteria
Define how success will be measured:
- **Leading indicators**: Early signals that the recommendation is working (measurable within weeks).
- **Lagging indicators**: Ultimate outcomes that confirm success (measurable within months or quarters).
- **Specific targets**: Quantified where possible. "Increase by 15%" not "improve significantly."
- **Timeline**: When each indicator should be checked.
- **Accountability**: Who is responsible for measuring and reporting.

### Step 8: Implementation Outline
Provide a high-level implementation roadmap:
- **Phase 1**: First actions to take (first week or month).
- **Phase 2**: Follow-on actions that build on Phase 1.
- **Phase 3**: Completion and evaluation.
- **Dependencies**: What must happen before each phase can begin.
- **Decision gates**: Points where progress is reviewed before proceeding.

### Step 9: Recommendation Review
Before delivering, verify the recommendation:
- Is it internally consistent (does the rationale support this specific action)?
- Is it proportional to the situation (not over-engineering a small problem or under-resourcing a large one)?
- Would you be comfortable defending this recommendation in a year if things go wrong?
- Have you addressed the objections that the most critical stakeholder would raise?

## Outputs

- A structured recommendation document with all eight sections.
- A clear, specific, actionable recommended course of action.
- Transparent rationale with evidence citations.
- Risk register with mitigations and contingency plans.
- Measurable success criteria with timeline and accountability.
- High-level implementation roadmap.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Burying the recommendation**: Putting the recommended action after pages of analysis. Lead with the recommendation after the brief situation statement.
- **Mealy-mouthed recommendations**: "We might consider possibly exploring..." Commit to a clear recommendation. It is acceptable to state conditions ("If X, then we recommend Y").
- **Ignoring the status quo**: Not explaining why doing nothing is inferior to the recommended action.
- **Overconfidence**: Presenting the recommendation as certain when it is a judgment call. Appropriate confidence, not false confidence.
- **Missing contingencies**: Recommending an action with no plan for what happens if it fails.
- **Unmeasurable success**: Defining success in terms that cannot be measured, making it impossible to know whether the recommendation worked.
