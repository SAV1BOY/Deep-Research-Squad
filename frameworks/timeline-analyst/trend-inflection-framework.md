# Trend Inflection Framework

## Purpose
A systematic method for identifying, validating, and interpreting inflection points where trends change direction, accelerate, decelerate, or fundamentally shift character. Distinguishes genuine inflection points from noise, temporary deviations, and confirmation-bias-driven pattern matching.

## When to Apply
- When analyzing time-series data for strategic insight
- When stakeholders ask "when did things change?" or "are we at a turning point?"
- When forecasting requires understanding whether current trends will continue or reverse
- When comparing how different domains experienced the same macro shift
- When validating claims that a market, industry, or phenomenon has "fundamentally changed"
- When building scenarios that depend on whether current trends persist or reverse

## Core Principles
1. **Inflection points are structural, not statistical**: A true inflection point reflects a change in underlying mechanism, not just a data spike.
2. **Leading indicators precede lagging confirmation**: By the time an inflection is obvious in headline metrics, the structural shift happened earlier.
3. **Context determines significance**: The same magnitude of change can be trivial in a volatile domain and transformative in a stable one.
4. **Not all bends are breaks**: Distinguish between acceleration within a trend, reversal of a trend, and emergence of a genuinely new trend.
5. **Multiple signals strengthen identification**: An inflection confirmed by several independent indicators is far more credible than one visible in a single metric.

## Step-by-Step Methodology

### Step 1: Define the Trend Under Examination
State the specific metric, behavior, or condition being tracked. Key question: What exactly are we measuring, and over what time horizon?

### Step 2: Establish the Baseline Trajectory
Characterize the prior trend: direction, rate of change, volatility. Key question: What would we expect to see if the existing trend simply continued?

### Step 3: Identify Candidate Inflection Points
Flag moments where observed values diverge meaningfully from the baseline trajectory. Key question: Where does the data first deviate from the prior pattern?

### Step 4: Assess Structural Drivers
For each candidate, investigate what changed in the underlying system. Key question: Is there a plausible causal mechanism, or is this deviation within normal variance?

### Step 5: Check for Leading Indicators
Look for earlier signals in adjacent or upstream domains. Key question: Did related metrics shift before the primary metric, suggesting a structural cause?

### Step 6: Test Persistence
Determine whether the new trajectory held over subsequent periods. Key question: Did the new direction sustain, or did the metric revert to the prior trend?

### Step 7: Classify the Inflection Type
Categorize as: acceleration, deceleration, reversal, plateau, or regime change. Key question: How does the post-inflection trend differ from the pre-inflection trend in both direction and character?

### Step 8: Assess Forward Implications
Determine what the inflection point implies for projections and decisions. Key question: If this inflection is real, what should we expect next, and what assumptions must we revise?

## Output Format
- Table of confirmed inflection points with date, metric, prior trend, post-inflection trend, and classification
- Confidence rating for each inflection (high, moderate, speculative) with supporting rationale
- Narrative explanation of the most consequential inflection points and their downstream effects
- Leading indicator analysis showing which signals preceded each confirmed inflection
- List of candidate inflection points rejected and why
- Forward-looking assessment of potential inflection points currently forming

## Integration with Other Frameworks
- **Periodization Framework**: Confirmed inflection points often serve as period boundaries.
- **Bayesian Updating**: Each candidate inflection point is a hypothesis to be updated with evidence for and against.
- **Contrarian Inversion**: Ask whether the consensus inflection point is real or a narrative artifact imposed after the fact.
- **Signal vs. Noise Framework**: Apply signal-to-noise analysis before accepting candidate inflection points as genuine.
- **Scenario Analysis**: Post-inflection trajectories feed directly into scenario construction.
- **Base Rate Context**: Historical base rates for how often apparent inflections revert help calibrate confidence.

## Anti-Patterns
- **Narrative-first inflection hunting**: Starting with a story about change and then finding data to support it, rather than letting data surface candidates.
- **Recency bias**: Overweighting the most recent data point as an inflection simply because it is new and surprising.
- **Single-metric myopia**: Declaring an inflection based on one metric when related metrics show no corresponding change.
- **Ignoring mean reversion**: Mistaking a return to baseline after an anomaly for a new inflection point.
- **Premature declaration**: Calling an inflection point before enough post-inflection data exists to confirm persistence.
- **Conflating correlation with inflection**: Declaring an inflection because two metrics moved simultaneously without establishing a structural link between them.
- **Anchoring to a preferred narrative**: Selecting the inflection date that best supports an existing thesis rather than the date best supported by the data.
