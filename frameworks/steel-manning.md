# Steel-Manning

## Purpose
Build the strongest possible version of an opposing argument before attempting to rebut it. This ensures intellectual honesty, prevents dismissal of legitimate counter-positions, and produces more robust conclusions that survive genuine challenge.

## When to Use
- Before rejecting any alternative explanation or counter-argument
- During the Contrarian Pass (RalphLoop R12) to pressure-test conclusions
- When evaluating competing hypotheses or policy options
- When stakeholders hold opposing views and the research must address them credibly
- Whenever the temptation arises to dismiss an argument as obviously wrong

## Inputs
- The position or conclusion being defended
- The opposing position, counter-argument, or alternative explanation
- Evidence available to both sides
- Context about why reasonable people might hold the opposing view

## Process (step-by-step)

### Step 1: Identify the Counter-Position
State the opposing argument clearly and completely. Do not begin with a weak or partial version. If the opposing position is not yet well-defined, research it before proceeding.

Questions to ask:
- What is the strongest version of the opposing view?
- Who are the most credible proponents of this view?
- What evidence do they cite?
- What is their reasoning chain?

### Step 2: Remove Weak Versions
Strip away all strawman elements:
- Remove any exaggerations or caricatures of the opposing view
- Discard arguments that even proponents of the view would not defend
- Eliminate rhetorical flourishes that obscure the substantive point
- Focus on the core logical and evidentiary claims

### Step 3: Construct the Strongest Possible Case
Build the opposing argument as if you were its most capable advocate:
- Use the best available evidence that supports the opposing view
- Employ the most rigorous reasoning chain available
- Grant reasonable assumptions that the opposing view requires
- Identify where the opposing view has genuine explanatory power
- Acknowledge aspects of the evidence that the opposing view handles better than yours

Rules for construction:
- You must be able to present this case to a proponent and have them say: "Yes, that is a fair and strong representation of my position."
- If you cannot do this, the steelman is not yet strong enough.

### Step 4: Evaluate Its Merits Honestly
Assess the steelmanned argument on its own terms:
- Where is it genuinely strong?
- What evidence does it explain that your position struggles with?
- What would need to be true for this argument to be correct?
- Is there a scenario where this argument wins?
- Rate its overall strength: Compelling / Substantial / Moderate / Weak (even at its best)

### Step 5: Respond to the Strongest Version
Only now attempt to rebut or integrate:
- Address the steelmanned argument point by point
- Concede any points that are genuinely strong
- Explain where your position still holds despite the steelmanned challenge
- If the steelman reveals weaknesses in your position, update your position accordingly
- Document what you conceded and why

### Step 6: Synthesize and Integrate
Determine what the steelman exercise changed:
- Did it reveal blind spots in the original position?
- Should the original conclusion be modified, caveated, or maintained?
- Are there elements of the opposing view that should be incorporated?
- Document the final position with explicit acknowledgment of the strongest counter-arguments

## Outputs
- The steelmanned version of the opposing argument
- Honest assessment of its merits
- Point-by-point response to the strongest version
- Updated position that accounts for the strongest counter-arguments
- Documented concessions and their rationale

## Common Pitfalls
- Building a steelman that is still a disguised strawman — the proponent would reject it
- Performing the exercise perfunctorily without genuine intellectual engagement
- Conceding too readily on points where your evidence is actually stronger
- Treating steelmanning as a one-time exercise rather than a habit of thought
- Failing to update your own position when the steelman reveals genuine weaknesses
- Confusing steelmanning with false equivalence — the strongest opposing argument may still be wrong
- Using steelmanning as a rhetorical device ("I considered the other side") without actually engaging
