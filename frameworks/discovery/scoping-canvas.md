# Scoping Canvas

## Purpose
A visual canvas for scoping research projects. Provides a structured, one-page view of the research landscape before deep investigation begins. Ensures alignment on what is being researched, for whom, and within what boundaries.

## When to Use
- At the start of any new research initiative
- When a research request is vague or overly broad
- When multiple stakeholders have different expectations
- Before committing significant time to investigation

## Inputs
- Initial research request or question from the requester
- Any background materials, prior research, or contextual documents
- Stakeholder list and their known priorities
- Time and resource constraints

## Process

### 1. Problem Statement
Define the core problem in one to three sentences. Be specific. A good problem statement answers: What is happening? Why does it matter? What decision depends on this research?

### 2. Stakeholders
List all parties who will use, be affected by, or contribute to the research:
- **Primary**: Direct decision-makers who will act on findings
- **Secondary**: Those affected by decisions made from the research
- **Tertiary**: Informational stakeholders who need awareness

### 3. Constraints
Document hard boundaries on the research:
- **Time**: Deadlines and milestones
- **Resources**: Budget, tools, access limitations
- **Scope**: Geographies, time periods, sectors explicitly included or excluded
- **Ethical**: Data privacy, consent, sensitivity considerations

### 4. Research Questions
Formulate three to seven key questions the research must answer. Rank them by priority. Each question should be answerable through evidence, not opinion alone.

### 5. Success Criteria
Define what "done" looks like:
- What deliverables are expected?
- What level of confidence is required?
- What format should findings take?
- Who must approve the final output?

### 6. Boundaries
Draw explicit lines around what is in scope and out of scope:
- **In scope**: Topics, sources, time frames to investigate
- **Out of scope**: Adjacent topics explicitly excluded
- **Gray zone**: Areas that may become relevant depending on findings

## Outputs
- A completed one-page canvas document
- Aligned understanding across all stakeholders
- A prioritized list of research questions ready for the Question Tree framework
- Clear go/no-go criteria for proceeding

## Common Pitfalls
- **Scope creep at the start**: Failing to set boundaries leads to unbounded research. Be firm about exclusions.
- **Vague problem statements**: "Learn more about X" is not a problem statement. Push for specificity on the decision being supported.
- **Missing stakeholders**: Overlooking a key stakeholder leads to rework when their needs surface late.
- **Ignoring constraints**: Designing research that cannot be completed within time or resource limits wastes effort.
- **Perfectionism in scoping**: The canvas is a living document. Spend one to two hours, not days. Refine as you learn.
- **Confusing questions with hypotheses**: Questions should be open. Hypotheses are tested later. Keep the canvas exploratory.
