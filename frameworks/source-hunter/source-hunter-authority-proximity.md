# Authority Proximity Framework

## Purpose
Assess how close a source is to the original event, data, or phenomenon it describes. Sources closer to the origin carry more evidentiary weight and are less likely to contain distortions introduced through the chain of transmission.

## When to Use
- When multiple sources report the same information and you need to identify the most authoritative one.
- When evaluating the strength of evidence behind a claim.
- When deciding whether a source is sufficient or whether a closer source must be found.
- When building the evidence base for high-stakes or contested claims.

## Inputs
- A source and the claim or information it provides.
- Context about the event, data, or phenomenon being described.
- Information about the source's author, publisher, and relationship to the subject.

## Process

### Step 1: Identify the Origin Point
- What is the original event, dataset, experiment, decision, or observation?
- Who was directly involved in creating or witnessing it?
- When and where did it occur?

### Step 2: Map the Source to the Proximity Scale
Rate the source on a five-level proximity scale:

**Level 1 — Direct Source (Highest Authority)**
- The creator of the data, the eyewitness, the decision-maker.
- Examples: researcher who conducted the study, company CEO in an earnings call, sensor that recorded the data.

**Level 2 — One Step Removed**
- Someone who directly interviewed or worked with Level 1 sources.
- Examples: journalist who interviewed the researcher, auditor who examined the financial records.

**Level 3 — Analyst or Synthesizer**
- Someone who analyzed multiple Level 1 or Level 2 sources.
- Examples: review paper author, industry analyst, textbook author.

**Level 4 — Commentator**
- Someone offering opinion or interpretation based on Level 2-3 sources.
- Examples: op-ed writer, blogger, social media commenter, talking head.

**Level 5 — Aggregator (Lowest Authority)**
- Someone compiling or summarizing Level 3-4 sources without independent analysis.
- Examples: Wikipedia editor, content aggregator, listicle writer.

### Step 3: Assess Distortion Risk
- Each step away from the origin introduces potential distortion.
- Types of distortion: simplification, misinterpretation, selective emphasis, context loss, ideological framing.
- Rate the distortion risk: low (Level 1-2), moderate (Level 3), high (Level 4-5).

### Step 4: Check for Authority Credentials
- Proximity alone does not guarantee quality. A direct witness may be unreliable.
- Cross-check: Does the source have relevant expertise? Known biases? Conflicts of interest?
- A Level 3 source with deep expertise may outweigh a Level 1 source with a conflict of interest.

### Step 5: Determine Sufficiency
- For the research question at hand, what proximity level is required?
- High-stakes claims: Level 1-2 sources required.
- Contextual claims: Level 3 sources are acceptable.
- Orientation or background: Level 4-5 sources can be used with caveats.

### Step 6: Document and Flag
- Record the proximity rating for each source in the evidence file.
- Flag any claim that relies solely on Level 4-5 sources.
- Recommend sourcing improvements where proximity is insufficient.

## Outputs
- A proximity rating (Level 1-5) for each source.
- Distortion risk assessment.
- Authority credentials check results.
- Flags for claims needing closer-proximity sources.

## Common Pitfalls
- Assuming proximity equals accuracy. Eyewitnesses can be wrong; direct data can be flawed.
- Ignoring expertise. A distant but expert source may be more reliable than a close but uninformed one.
- Not accounting for conflicts of interest at any proximity level.
- Treating all Level 3 sources as equivalent when their quality varies enormously.
- Failing to trace back to the origin when a closer source is available.
- Conflating the publisher's reputation with the specific author's authority.

## Related Frameworks
- **Primary-Secondary Split**: Proximity informs but differs from the primary/secondary classification.
- **Publication Chain**: Traces the full chain from origin to the source in hand.
- **Signal-Noise Scoring**: Proximity feeds into the authority component of the score.
- **Citation Integrity**: Verifies whether proximity claims are genuine.
- **Cross-Source Verification**: Multiple sources at different proximity levels strengthen evidence.
