# Literature Seminal Chain Framework

## Purpose

Identify the seminal works that founded or transformed a field and trace their influence forward through time. Understanding the intellectual lineage of a topic reveals which ideas are foundational, which are derivative, how the field evolved, and where paradigm shifts occurred. This provides essential context for evaluating current research.

## When to Use

- When entering an unfamiliar research domain and needing to understand its origins.
- When evaluating whether a "new" idea is genuinely novel or a rediscovery.
- When building credibility in a research report by demonstrating deep domain knowledge.
- When identifying the most authoritative voices and institutions in a field.
- When understanding why current debates exist and what shaped them.

## Inputs

- The research topic or domain to investigate.
- Access to academic databases with citation tracking (e.g., Google Scholar, Semantic Scholar, Web of Science).
- Initial familiarity with key terms and concepts in the field.
- Time budget for the chain-tracing exercise.

## Process

1. **Identify candidate seminal works.** Search for highly cited papers, books, or reports in the field. Look for works cited across multiple subsequent publications. Check "foundational" or "classic" reading lists.
2. **Verify seminal status.** A seminal work should meet multiple criteria: high citation count, cited across subfields, introduced a new concept or method, frequently referenced in introductions and literature reviews.
3. **Build the citation chain forward.** From each seminal work, trace forward: What did it inspire? What subsequent works cited it most? How did they extend or challenge it?
4. **Identify branching points.** Where did the field split into subfields? What works marked those divergences?
5. **Identify paradigm shifts.** Were there moments when the field's fundamental assumptions changed? What works triggered those shifts?
6. **Map intellectual lineages.** Create a visual or structured map showing how ideas flowed from seminal works through successive generations of research.
7. **Note the critics.** Who challenged the seminal works? Were those challenges successful? Did they redirect the field?
8. **Identify the current frontier.** Where does the chain end? What are the most recent significant contributions that build on the seminal tradition?
9. **Assess continued relevance.** Are the seminal works still relevant, or has the field moved beyond them? Are foundational assumptions still valid?

## Outputs

- A list of seminal works with justification for their seminal status.
- A citation chain map showing intellectual lineage and branching.
- Identified paradigm shifts and the works that triggered them.
- Key critics and their impact on the field's direction.
- The current frontier: most recent significant works in the chain.
- Assessment of which foundational assumptions remain valid.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Citation count bias.** High citations do not always indicate seminal status; some highly cited works are methodological tools, not foundational ideas.
- **Recency bias.** Overlooking older foundational works in favor of recent high-citation papers.
- **Single-discipline blinders.** Seminal works sometimes come from adjacent fields.
- **Hagiography.** Uncritically venerating seminal authors rather than evaluating their ideas.
- **Missing oral traditions.** Some foundational ideas were never published formally but shaped the field through teaching and mentorship.
- **Stopping at the first generation.** The chain should extend to the present, not stop at the founding works.
- **Ignoring failed branches.** Tracing only successful lineages misses important lessons from abandoned approaches.

## Quality Criteria

- At least three independent indicators must support seminal status (citation count, cross-subfield influence, concept origination).
- The chain must extend from founding works to the current frontier, not stop at any intermediate point.
- Paradigm shifts must be identified with the specific works that triggered them, not described vaguely.
- Critics of seminal works must be included in the chain; the map must not be purely celebratory.
