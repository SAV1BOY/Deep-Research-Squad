# Contrarian Inversion Framework

## Purpose

Systematically invert every conclusion and rigorously test the inverse. The core question is: "How is this wrong?" By deliberately arguing the opposite, you expose hidden assumptions, weak evidence, and logical gaps that confirmation bias would otherwise conceal.

## When to Use

- After a research conclusion has been drafted and appears solid.
- When the team shows high confidence in a finding without sufficient challenge.
- When a conclusion aligns suspiciously well with prior expectations.
- During final review before publishing or delivering research.
- When stakeholders are about to make high-stakes decisions based on findings.

## Inputs

- A clearly stated research conclusion or claim.
- The evidence and reasoning chain supporting that conclusion.
- Context about what decisions depend on this conclusion.

## Process

1. **State the conclusion explicitly.** Write it as a single, unambiguous declarative sentence.
2. **Formulate the exact inverse.** Negate the conclusion precisely. If the claim is "X causes Y," the inverse is "X does not cause Y" or "X prevents Y."
3. **Steelman the inverse.** Build the strongest possible case for the inverted conclusion. Do not strawman it.
4. **Audit the original evidence through the inverse lens.** For each piece of supporting evidence, ask: Could this evidence also support the inverse? Is there an alternative interpretation?
5. **Identify assumptions that must hold.** List every assumption the original conclusion depends on. Test each one independently.
6. **Search for disconfirming evidence.** Actively look for data, studies, or cases that support the inverse rather than the original.
7. **Assess asymmetry.** Compare the strength of evidence for the original versus the inverse. Is the gap as large as initially believed?
8. **Revise or reinforce.** Either modify the original conclusion to account for weaknesses found, or document why the original stands despite the inversion test.

## Outputs

- The inverted claim, fully articulated.
- A steelmanned argument for the inverse.
- A list of assumptions the original conclusion depends on.
- An honest assessment of whether the original conclusion survived inversion.
- Revised conclusion with appropriate confidence calibration.

## Quality Criteria

- The inverse must be a precise negation, not a vague objection.
- The steelmanned inverse should be convincing enough that a reasonable person could hold it.
- Every key assumption must be explicitly listed and independently tested.
- The evidence audit must apply equal scrutiny to both the original and the inverse.
- The final assessment must state whether the original was weakened, strengthened, or unchanged by the inversion exercise.
- Confidence calibration must be explicit: do not use vague terms like "likely" without defining what probability range you mean.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Weak inversion.** Inverting lazily or creating a strawman inverse that is easy to dismiss.
- **Stopping at discomfort.** Abandoning the exercise when the inverse starts looking plausible.
- **Treating inversion as binary.** The answer may be nuanced rather than original-vs-inverse.
- **Skipping the evidence audit.** Merely stating the inverse without checking if existing evidence supports it.
- **Anchoring on the original.** Performing inversion as a ritual rather than genuinely considering the inverse could be true.
- **Conflating inversion with contrarianism.** The goal is truth-seeking, not reflexive disagreement.
- **Ignoring partial inversions.** The conclusion may be partially wrong rather than entirely wrong; test components separately.
