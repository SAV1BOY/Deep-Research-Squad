# Question Pyramid Framework

## Purpose
Structure research questions into a three-level hierarchy: mother question, daughter questions, and granddaughter questions. This pyramid ensures every research activity traces back to the core question and that no investigation happens without a clear purpose.

## When to Use
- After problem framing, as an alternative or complement to the issue tree.
- When the research needs a clear line of sight from high-level question to specific investigations.
- When communicating the research plan to stakeholders who need to see the logic flow.
- When pruning research scope mid-project.

## Inputs
- Validated problem statement (becomes the mother question).
- Scope boundaries.
- Known sub-topics or dimensions of the problem.

## Process

### Step 1: Define the Mother Question
- Express the core research problem as a single, answerable question.
- The mother question must be specific enough to have a definitive answer.
- It should not be a yes/no question; prefer "how," "what," "why," or "to what extent."
- Test: If answered fully, would the stakeholder's need be met? If not, revise.

### Step 2: Generate Daughter Questions
- Break the mother question into 3-7 daughter questions.
- Each daughter question addresses one necessary component of the mother question.
- Together, answering all daughter questions must fully answer the mother question.
- Apply MECE: no overlap between daughters, no gaps in coverage.
- Each daughter question should be answerable independently of the others.

### Step 3: Generate Granddaughter Questions
- For each daughter question, identify 2-5 granddaughter questions.
- Granddaughter questions are directly investigable through search, data analysis, or expert consultation.
- They should be specific enough that a single research action can address them.
- Not every daughter question needs the same number of granddaughters.

### Step 4: Validate the Pyramid (Bottom-Up)
- Start at the granddaughter level. For each: Does answering this contribute to its parent daughter question?
- At the daughter level: Does answering all daughters fully answer the mother question?
- Remove any question that does not contribute to its parent.
- Add any question whose absence leaves a gap.

### Step 5: Validate the Pyramid (Top-Down)
- From the mother question, trace down: Is every necessary investigation represented?
- Are there aspects of the mother question that no daughter addresses?
- Are there daughter questions that no granddaughter operationalizes?

### Step 6: Prioritize and Prune
- Rank granddaughter questions by information value (impact on the mother question's answer).
- Rank by feasibility (can this be investigated within constraints?).
- Prune low-value, low-feasibility questions.
- Mark "must answer" vs "nice to answer" at each level.

### Step 7: Assign and Track
- Assign granddaughter questions to specific research actions or agents.
- Track completion status at each level.
- A daughter question is "answered" only when all its critical granddaughters are resolved.

## Outputs
- A three-level question pyramid document.
- Priority rankings at each level.
- Assignment of granddaughter questions to research actions.
- A visual or outline representation of the full pyramid.

## Common Pitfalls
- Mother question too vague, producing unfocused daughters.
- Daughter questions that overlap, causing duplicated research effort.
- Granddaughter questions too broad, still requiring further decomposition.
- Pruning too aggressively, leaving gaps in the mother question's coverage.
- Failing to validate bottom-up, resulting in orphan questions that serve no parent.
- Treating the pyramid as static; it should evolve as findings emerge.

## Related Frameworks
- **Problem Framing**: Produces the mother question.
- **Issue Tree**: Alternative hierarchical decomposition; can be used alongside the pyramid.
- **MECE for Research**: Quality standard for daughter and granddaughter relationships.
- **Scope Bounding**: Determines how many levels and branches to pursue.
- **Keyword Grid**: Granddaughter questions feed directly into keyword generation.
