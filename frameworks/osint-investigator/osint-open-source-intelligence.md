# OSINT Open Source Intelligence Framework

## Purpose

Provide a structured methodology for collecting, processing, analyzing, and disseminating intelligence from publicly available sources. OSINT follows the intelligence cycle: planning and direction, collection, processing, analysis, and dissemination. This framework establishes clear legal and ethical boundaries at every stage.

## When to Use

- When conducting research that requires intelligence-grade rigor and methodology.
- When investigating entities, events, or trends using only public information.
- When building situational awareness about a market, technology, or competitive landscape.
- When standard research methods need to be supplemented with investigative techniques.
- When findings will inform high-stakes decisions and need to be defensible.

## Inputs

- Intelligence requirements: specific questions that need answers.
- Known information and starting points.
- Time, resource, and access constraints.
- Legal framework and jurisdictional considerations.
- Classification of the intended audience and how findings will be used.

## Process

1. **Planning and direction.** Define intelligence requirements. What specific questions must be answered? Prioritize requirements. Identify potential sources. Establish legal and ethical boundaries for this specific investigation.
2. **Collection.** Gather raw information from public sources: websites, social media, public records, news media, academic publications, government documents, satellite imagery (if publicly available), financial filings, and community forums. Use structured search techniques. Document every source.
3. **Processing.** Organize raw information. Translate if needed. Digitize if needed. Tag, categorize, and index collected materials. Remove duplicates. Convert formats for analysis.
4. **Analysis.** Evaluate source reliability and information credibility. Cross-reference findings across independent sources. Identify patterns, anomalies, and connections. Distinguish facts from assessments from speculation. Apply analytical frameworks (timeline analysis, link analysis, pattern analysis).
5. **Production.** Synthesize analysis into coherent intelligence products. Structure findings to answer the original intelligence requirements. State confidence levels for each finding. Identify gaps and uncertainties. Include dissenting views.
6. **Dissemination.** Deliver findings to the intended audience in an appropriate format. Protect sources and methods as needed. Ensure findings are actionable and contextualized.
7. **Feedback and iteration.** Gather feedback on whether intelligence requirements were met. Identify new questions raised by the findings. Update collection and analysis as needed.

## Legal and Ethical Boundaries

- Only collect information from publicly accessible sources.
- Do not hack, breach, or circumvent access controls.
- Do not impersonate, deceive, or socially engineer access to information.
- Comply with all applicable laws including privacy regulations, computer fraud statutes, and data protection requirements.
- Do not access or store information that requires authorization you do not have.
- Respect terms of service of platforms and databases.
- Protect collected personal data according to applicable privacy laws.
- Document the legal basis for collection activities.

## Outputs

- An intelligence report answering the defined requirements.
- Confidence assessments for each finding (high, moderate, low).
- Source reliability assessments.
- Identified intelligence gaps and recommended follow-up.
- A collection log documenting sources consulted and methods used.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Scope creep.** Collecting interesting but irrelevant information that distracts from the intelligence requirements.
- **Source fixation.** Over-relying on a single source type or platform.
- **Mirror imaging.** Assuming the target thinks and operates like the analyst.
- **Anchoring.** Locking onto early findings and failing to update with new information.
- **Circular reporting.** Two sources citing each other, creating the illusion of independent confirmation.
- **Legal overreach.** Crossing from public information collection into unauthorized access.
- **Analysis paralysis.** Collecting endlessly without moving to analysis and production.
