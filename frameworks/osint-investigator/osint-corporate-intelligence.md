# OSINT Corporate Intelligence Framework

## Purpose

Gather and analyze corporate intelligence from publicly available sources. This includes regulatory filings, patent applications, job postings, press releases, financial reports, litigation records, and other public documents. The goal is to build a comprehensive, evidence-based picture of a company's strategy, health, capabilities, and trajectory using only legal, public sources.

## When to Use

- When conducting due diligence on a potential partner, acquisition target, or competitor.
- When analyzing competitive strategy and positioning.
- When verifying corporate claims about size, revenue, technology, or market position.
- When tracking industry trends through the lens of corporate activity.
- When assessing corporate health indicators for investment or partnership decisions.

## Inputs

- Target company name, subsidiaries, and known identifiers (ticker, registration numbers).
- Industry and geographic context.
- Specific intelligence questions to answer (strategy, health, capabilities, risks).
- Access to public registries, filing databases, and patent systems.

## Process

1. **Map the corporate structure.** Identify parent companies, subsidiaries, joint ventures, and affiliates using public registries and filings.
2. **Analyze financial filings.** Review annual reports, quarterly filings, and financial statements for public companies. For private companies, check available registrations and credit reports.
3. **Review regulatory filings.** Check industry-specific regulatory submissions: FDA filings, FCC applications, environmental permits, banking regulations, depending on the industry.
4. **Analyze patent and IP activity.** Search patent databases for filings, grants, and citations. Patent activity reveals R&D direction, technical capabilities, and strategic priorities.
5. **Monitor job postings.** Current and historical job postings reveal: technologies in use, expansion plans, organizational structure, skill gaps, and strategic priorities.
6. **Track press releases and media.** Analyze official communications for stated strategy, partnerships, product launches, and leadership changes. Compare stated strategy with observable actions.
7. **Review litigation and legal records.** Search court records and regulatory actions for lawsuits, settlements, consent decrees, and compliance issues.
8. **Analyze leadership and governance.** Review board composition, executive backgrounds, and management changes. Leadership changes often signal strategic shifts.
9. **Cross-reference and triangulate.** Compare information across sources. Flag inconsistencies between public statements and filings. Verify claims with independent sources.
10. **Synthesize into intelligence product.** Produce a structured corporate intelligence report addressing the original questions with sourced findings.

## Outputs

- A corporate structure map showing entities and relationships.
- A financial health assessment based on public filings.
- A patent and IP landscape analysis.
- A strategic direction assessment based on job postings, patents, and communications.
- A risk profile covering litigation, regulatory issues, and governance concerns.
- Source attribution for every finding with reliability assessment.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Conflating public relations with reality.** Press releases and corporate communications are marketing, not evidence.
- **Snapshot thinking.** Corporate intelligence is dynamic; a single-point analysis misses trends and trajectories.
- **Missing private company data.** Private companies disclose less; gaps should be noted, not ignored.
- **Jurisdictional blindness.** Corporate filings vary by jurisdiction; international operations may be less visible.
- **Overinterpreting job postings.** A job posting indicates intent, not achievement. Not all posted roles are filled.
- **Ignoring subsidiaries.** Significant activity may occur in subsidiaries or entities not obviously linked to the parent company.
- **Confirmation bias in competitor analysis.** Seeking weaknesses in competitors while ignoring their strengths.
