# Insight Extraction

## Purpose
A systematic method for extracting actionable insights from raw research findings. Goes beyond summarizing facts to discover patterns, anomalies, and connections that are not obvious from any single finding alone. Transforms information into intelligence.

## When to Use
- After evidence gathering is complete and findings have been validated
- When a large volume of findings needs to be distilled into key takeaways
- When stakeholders need "so what" answers, not just "what" answers
- When the research should generate novel understanding, not just confirm known information

## Inputs
- Validated research findings organized by theme or question
- Evidence ratings and confidence levels for each finding
- The Contradiction Map showing resolved and unresolved conflicts
- Background knowledge about the domain and stakeholder context

## Process

### 1. Pattern Matching
Scan across all findings to identify recurring patterns:
- **Frequency patterns**: What themes or claims appear repeatedly across independent sources?
- **Trend patterns**: What directional changes are evident over time?
- **Correlation patterns**: What findings tend to co-occur or move together?
- **Clustering patterns**: What groups of findings naturally cluster into categories?
- Document each pattern with the specific findings that support it

### 2. Anomaly Detection
Identify findings that deviate from expected patterns:
- **Outliers**: Findings that contradict the dominant pattern
- **Surprises**: Results that were not predicted by prior knowledge or hypotheses
- **Absences**: Expected findings that did not materialize despite adequate searching
- **Disproportionalities**: Effects that are much larger or smaller than expected
- Anomalies often contain the most valuable insights; do not dismiss them

### 3. Connection Finding
Discover non-obvious relationships between findings:
- **Cross-domain links**: How do findings in one area illuminate findings in another?
- **Causal chains**: Can a sequence of findings be linked into a causal narrative?
- **Feedback loops**: Do any findings suggest self-reinforcing or self-correcting dynamics?
- **Analogies**: Do patterns in this research mirror patterns seen in other domains?
- Map connections visually where possible to reveal structure

### 4. Insight Formulation
For each pattern, anomaly, or connection, formulate a clear insight statement:
- State the insight in one to two sentences
- Explain what evidence supports it
- Describe why it matters for the research question or decision
- Rate the insight's confidence (derived from the underlying evidence)
- Classify the insight as confirmatory (validates existing beliefs) or novel (challenges or extends them)

### 5. Prioritize Insights
Rank insights by their decision value:
- **High value**: Directly informs a key decision or changes the recommended course of action
- **Medium value**: Adds important nuance or context to the analysis
- **Low value**: Interesting but does not materially affect decisions or recommendations

## Outputs
- A prioritized list of insights with supporting evidence
- Classification of each insight (pattern, anomaly, or connection)
- Confidence ratings for each insight
- A narrative connecting the most important insights into a coherent story

## Common Pitfalls
- **Confirmation bias in pattern matching**: Seeing patterns that confirm what you already believe. Test patterns against disconfirming evidence.
- **Dismissing anomalies**: Treating outliers as noise rather than signal. Investigate anomalies before discarding them.
- **Forcing connections**: Creating links between findings that are coincidental, not meaningful. Require evidence for connections.
- **Insight inflation**: Labeling obvious summaries as "insights." A true insight reveals something not immediately apparent from the raw findings.
- **Neglecting absences**: What you did not find can be as important as what you found. Actively look for missing evidence.
- **Losing traceability**: Each insight must connect back to specific findings. Untethered insights are speculation.
