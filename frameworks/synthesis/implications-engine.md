# Implications Engine

## Purpose
A systematic framework for mapping the implications of research findings across time horizons and stakeholder groups. Moves beyond "what we found" to "what it means for each affected party, now and in the future." Ensures that research outputs are forward-looking and actionable.

## When to Use
- After insights have been extracted and validated
- When research findings affect multiple stakeholder groups differently
- When decision-makers need to understand both immediate and long-term consequences
- When building the case for action or inaction based on research findings

## Inputs
- Prioritized insights from the Insight Extraction framework
- Stakeholder list from the Scoping Canvas with their priorities and concerns
- Knowledge of the decision context and timeline
- Understanding of the systems and dynamics at play in the domain

## Process

### 1. Identify Stakeholders
List all parties for whom implications should be mapped:
- Decision-makers who commissioned the research
- Internal teams or departments affected by findings
- External parties (customers, partners, regulators, competitors)
- Indirect stakeholders (communities, ecosystems, future users)

### 2. Map Immediate Implications (0 to 3 months)
For each insight, identify what follows right now:
- What decisions can or should be made immediately?
- What risks require urgent attention?
- What opportunities have a narrow window?
- What stakeholder reactions are likely in the short term?
- Tag each implication with the stakeholders it affects most

### 3. Map Short-Term Implications (3 to 12 months)
For each insight, project forward:
- How will the landscape evolve based on current trajectories?
- What second-order effects will emerge from immediate actions or inactions?
- What competitive or regulatory responses are likely?
- What capabilities or resources need to be developed?
- Consider interactions between multiple insights

### 4. Map Long-Term Implications (1 to 5 years)
For each insight, consider structural and strategic effects:
- How might this finding reshape the industry, market, or domain?
- What strategic positions are strengthened or weakened?
- What paradigm shifts could this finding signal or accelerate?
- What investments or commitments made now will pay off or become liabilities?
- Apply scenario thinking for high-uncertainty long-term implications

### 5. Cross-Stakeholder Analysis
After mapping implications across time horizons:
- Identify where stakeholder implications conflict (one group's opportunity is another's risk)
- Find alignment opportunities where multiple stakeholders benefit
- Highlight implications that compound across time horizons
- Note implications that are sensitive or require careful communication

### 6. Confidence and Conditionality
For each implication:
- Assign a confidence level based on the underlying insight's confidence
- Note conditions under which the implication holds or fails
- Identify trigger events that would activate conditional implications
- Distinguish between probable implications and possible implications

## Outputs
- A stakeholder-by-time-horizon matrix of implications
- Priority-ranked implications for each stakeholder group
- Conditional implications with trigger events identified
- A narrative summary connecting implications into a forward-looking story

## Common Pitfalls
- **Linear thinking**: Assuming implications unfold in a straight line. Real systems have feedback loops, delays, and nonlinear responses.
- **Stakeholder blindness**: Mapping implications only for the commissioning party. Other stakeholders' reactions shape outcomes.
- **Overconfident long-term projections**: The further out you project, the less certain you should be. Reflect this in confidence ratings.
- **Ignoring second-order effects**: First-order implications are obvious. The real value is in second and third-order effects that others miss.
- **Treating time horizons as independent**: Immediate actions shape short-term outcomes, which shape long-term positions. Map the chain.
- **Implication without action path**: Every implication should suggest what can be done about it, even if the answer is "monitor."
