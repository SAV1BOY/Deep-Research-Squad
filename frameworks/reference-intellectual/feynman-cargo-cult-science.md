# Feynman: Cargo Cult Science

## Purpose
Apply Richard Feynman's concept of cargo cult science to research evaluation. Cargo cult science has the appearance of rigorous research (methods, data, citations) but lacks the substance (intellectual honesty, genuine testing, transparency about limitations). This framework helps identify research that looks right but is fundamentally flawed.

## When to Use
- When evaluating the rigor of sources, studies, or analyses encountered during research
- When a finding seems too clean, too convenient, or too perfectly aligned with expectations
- When assessing whether a methodology genuinely supports its conclusions
- When building the Evidence Rating or Source Reliability assessments

## Inputs
- Research papers, reports, or analyses to evaluate
- The methodology described by the source
- The conclusions drawn and how they relate to the evidence presented
- Knowledge of what genuine rigor looks like in the relevant domain

## Process

### 1. Check for Form Without Substance
Cargo cult research mimics the rituals of science without the core discipline:
- Are statistical methods applied correctly, or just applied?
- Is the literature review comprehensive, or does it cite only supporting work?
- Are data collection methods genuinely rigorous, or described with rigorous-sounding language?
- Does the methodology actually test the hypothesis, or just appear to?

### 2. Assess Intellectual Honesty
Feynman's core principle: "You must not fool yourself, and you are the easiest person to fool."
- Does the researcher report results that contradict their hypothesis, or only confirmations?
- Are limitations and weaknesses of the study discussed honestly?
- Are alternative explanations for the findings considered seriously?
- Does the researcher distinguish between what the data shows and what they wish it showed?

### 3. Evaluate Transparency
Genuine research is transparent enough for others to find flaws:
- Is the data available for independent analysis?
- Is the methodology described in enough detail to replicate?
- Are assumptions stated explicitly rather than buried?
- Can you trace the path from raw data to conclusions?

### 4. Look for Red Flags
Common indicators of cargo cult research:
- **Cherry-picking**: Selecting data points or time periods that support the conclusion while ignoring others
- **P-hacking**: Running multiple statistical tests and reporting only significant results
- **HARKing**: Hypothesizing After Results are Known, presenting post-hoc explanations as predictions
- **Unfalsifiable conclusions**: Claims framed so that no evidence could contradict them
- **Appeal to complexity**: Hiding weak reasoning behind complicated methodology or jargon
- **Circular reasoning**: Using the conclusion as a premise in the argument that supports it

### 5. Apply the Feynman Test
For each piece of research, ask:
- "What would it take to prove this wrong?" If the answer is "nothing," it is not science.
- "Has the researcher tried to prove themselves wrong?" If not, the confirmation bias is unmanaged.
- "Would a competent skeptic find this convincing?" If the answer is "only if they already agreed," the argument is circular.
- "What has been left out?" The most important information is often what is not reported.

### 6. Calibrate Skepticism
Not all research is cargo cult science:
- Genuine researchers make honest errors. Distinguish incompetence from deception.
- Some fields have inherently messier methods. Evaluate against domain-appropriate standards.
- Preliminary findings presented as preliminary are honest. Preliminary findings presented as definitive are not.
- The goal is not to dismiss all research but to identify which research deserves trust.

## Outputs
- Assessment of each source's intellectual rigor beyond surface appearances
- Red flags identified with specific examples
- Adjusted evidence ratings that account for cargo cult indicators
- Recommendations for which sources to rely on and which to treat with skepticism

## Common Pitfalls
- **Cynicism masquerading as skepticism**: Dismissing all research as cargo cult is itself intellectually lazy. Apply the framework honestly.
- **Judging by aesthetics**: Well-formatted reports with professional graphics can be cargo cult. Poorly formatted papers can be rigorous. Judge substance.
- **Expertise bias**: Assuming that research from credentialed institutions is automatically rigorous. Credentials indicate opportunity, not necessarily quality.
- **Missing your own cargo cult behavior**: The framework applies to your own research too. Are you performing the rituals of rigor without the substance?
- **Ignoring domain differences**: What constitutes rigor varies by field. Do not apply physics standards to social science or vice versa.
- **Throwing out imperfect evidence**: In many domains, some cargo cult elements are widespread. Use the framework to discount appropriately, not to discard entirely.
