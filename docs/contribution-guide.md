# Contribution Guide

## Purpose

This guide explains how to contribute improvements to the Deep Research Squad's
processes, templates, language guides, and documentation. The squad improves
through structured contribution, not ad hoc changes.

## What Can Be Contributed

### Content Contributions

- Corrections to factual errors or unclear language.
- New phrase patterns for `phrases/`.
- New tone profiles or language guides for `voice/`.
- New policy documents for `docs/`.
- Templates for recurring deliverable types.

### Process Contributions

- Proposed changes to quality gate criteria.
- New escalation triggers or rules.
- Workflow optimizations.
- Tool or framework recommendations.

## Contribution Process

### Step 1: Identify the Change

Describe what you want to change and why:
- What is the current state?
- What is the problem or gap?
- What is the proposed improvement?

### Step 2: Draft the Contribution

- Follow existing formatting and style conventions.
- Reference the naming conventions in `docs/naming-conventions.md`.
- Maintain consistency with the squad's voice and tone standards.
- Keep files within the 40-70 line range for guides and templates.

### Step 3: Submit for Review

- Submit the draft to the Research Director or designated reviewer.
- Include a brief rationale for the change.
- Flag any dependencies (does this change affect other documents?).

### Step 4: Review and Feedback

- The reviewer assesses alignment with squad standards.
- Feedback is provided within the agreed review cycle.
- Revisions are incorporated as needed.

### Step 5: Merge and Communicate

- Approved contributions are integrated into the repository.
- Changes are noted in `docs/changelog.md`.
- Affected agents are notified of the update.

## Style Standards

- Use Markdown format for all documents.
- Follow the existing heading hierarchy (H1, H2, H3).
- Use tables for structured information.
- Use lists for sequential or enumerated items.
- Write in active voice with clear, direct sentences.

## What Not to Contribute

- Opinion pieces without evidence or rationale.
- Changes that contradict the research philosophy without a proposed alternative.
- Duplicates of existing content.
- Content that does not follow naming and formatting conventions.

## Quality Check

- Does the contribution follow formatting conventions?
- Is the rationale for the change clear?
- Does it maintain consistency with existing documents?
- Have dependencies been identified?
- Is the change logged in the changelog?
