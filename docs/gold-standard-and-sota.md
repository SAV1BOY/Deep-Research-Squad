# Gold Standard and State of the Art

## Purpose

This document defines what "gold standard" and "state of the art" (SOTA) mean
within the Deep Research Squad. These terms are used frequently and must have
precise, shared definitions to avoid ambiguity.

## Gold Standard

### Definition

A gold standard is the best available method, benchmark, or reference point
against which other approaches are measured. It represents the highest
accepted level of quality or accuracy in a given domain.

### Characteristics

- **Widely accepted:** Recognized by the relevant expert community.
- **Empirically validated:** Tested and shown to be reliable.
- **Stable:** Does not change frequently (unlike SOTA).
- **Referenceable:** Citable source or methodology exists.

### Examples

| Domain               | Gold Standard Example                        |
|----------------------|----------------------------------------------|
| Medical diagnostics  | Histopathological examination (biopsy)       |
| NLP evaluation       | Human expert annotation                      |
| Financial reporting  | Audited financial statements (GAAP/IFRS)     |
| Survey methodology   | Random probability sampling                  |

## State of the Art (SOTA)

### Definition

State of the art refers to the current best performance or most advanced
approach in a given domain. Unlike gold standards, SOTA is dynamic and
evolves as the field advances.

### Characteristics

- **Current:** Represents the latest advances (typically within 1-2 years).
- **Domain-specific:** SOTA in one field does not transfer to another.
- **Benchmark-dependent:** Defined relative to specific benchmarks.
- **Temporary:** Today's SOTA is tomorrow's baseline.

## Gold Standard vs. SOTA

| Dimension          | Gold Standard                    | State of the Art              |
|--------------------|----------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Stability          | High (changes slowly)            | Low (changes rapidly)         |
| Purpose            | Reference and validation         | Frontier and aspiration       |
| Domain scope       | Often cross-domain               | Usually domain-specific       |
| Time sensitivity   | Low                              | High (must be dated)          |

## Usage Rules

1. **Always date SOTA references.** "As of [date], the state of the art is..."
2. **Do not conflate gold standard with SOTA.** They serve different purposes.
3. **Cite the benchmark** when claiming SOTA performance.
4. **Acknowledge that SOTA is temporary.**
5. **Use gold standard as validation anchor.**

## In Research Deliverables

- Use "gold standard" when establishing the reference point for quality.
- Use "state of the art" when discussing the current frontier.
- Always date SOTA claims and cite the benchmark or source.
- In literature reviews, dedicate a section to SOTA in the relevant domain.

## Quality Check

- Are gold standard and SOTA used with their precise definitions?
- Are SOTA references dated?
- Is the benchmark cited for SOTA claims?
- Is the distinction between gold standard and SOTA clear?
