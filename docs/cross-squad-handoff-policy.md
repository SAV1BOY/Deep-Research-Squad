# Cross-Squad Handoff Policy

## Purpose

This policy governs how the Deep Research Squad delivers research outputs
to other squads. Cross-squad handoffs require additional context, explicit
assumptions, and structured formatting to prevent misinterpretation.

## When Cross-Squad Handoffs Occur

- Another squad requests research support.
- Findings from an internal project are relevant to another squad.
- Escalation rules direct intelligence to a specific team.
- A joint initiative requires shared research inputs.

## Mandatory Metadata Header

Every cross-squad deliverable must begin with:

```
From: [Squad Name / Agent Role]
To: [Receiving Squad / Role]
Date: [Delivery Date]
Classification: [Research / Intelligence / Recommendation]
Confidence: [Overall confidence level]
Scope: [What is and is not covered]
```

## Handoff Package Contents

### Required Elements

1. **Context** (2-3 sentences): Why this is being shared and what triggered it.
2. **Key Findings**: Bulleted, with confidence level per finding.
3. **Relevance Mapping**: Explicit connection to the recipient's work.
4. **Caveats and Limitations**: What the recipient should not infer.
5. **Source Summary**: Source count, type breakdown, and tier range.
6. **Suggested Actions** (optional): What the recipient might do with this.

### Translation Protocol

When the receiving squad operates in a different domain:
- Replace research jargon with their domain's vocabulary.
- Include a glossary if more than three technical terms are unavoidable.
- Use analogies to bridge domains when precision is not sacrificed.

## Handoff Process

### Step 1: Internal Quality Check

Verify all quality gate criteria are met before handoff.

### Step 2: Recipient Briefing

Provide a walkthrough of findings. Answer clarifying questions and document them.

### Step 3: Formal Transfer

Deliver the handoff package through the agreed channel.

### Step 4: Follow-Up Window

Maintain availability for questions for 5 business days post-handoff.
Document any follow-up questions and responses.

## Quality Standards

- No jargon without definition.
- Findings are self-contained (reader needs no prior context).
- Confidence levels are explained for non-research audiences.
- Action items are assigned to specific roles, not vague groups.

## Common Mistakes

- Assuming the recipient has your context.
- Using internal shorthand or acronyms.
- Delivering raw findings without domain-specific interpretation.
- Omitting confidence levels (recipients default to "confirmed").
- Not specifying what is out of scope.

## Quality Check

- Is the metadata header complete?
- Has domain translation been performed?
- Are confidence levels explicit per finding?
- Can the recipient act without follow-up questions?
