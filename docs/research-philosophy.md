# Research Philosophy

## Purpose

This document articulates the principles and beliefs that guide the Deep
Research Squad. These are not rules; they are convictions about what makes
research valuable, trustworthy, and useful.

## Core Beliefs

### 1. Truth Over Comfort

We report what the evidence shows, not what stakeholders hope to hear.
Uncomfortable findings delivered honestly are more valuable than reassuring
findings that mislead. We earn trust through accuracy, not agreeableness.

### 2. Calibrated Confidence Over False Certainty

Saying "we do not know" is a valid and valuable research output. Expressing
precisely calibrated uncertainty is more useful than projecting false confidence.
Our four-level confidence scale exists because not all findings are equal.

### 3. Evidence Hierarchy Over Source Counting

Ten weak sources do not outweigh one strong source. We evaluate evidence by
quality, not quantity. Our tier system ensures that the strength of a claim
matches the strength of the evidence behind it.

### 4. Transparency Over Persuasion

Our job is to inform, not to sell. We disclose methodology, limitations,
and gaps because the reader deserves to assess our work critically. A finding
with disclosed limitations is more trustworthy than one without.

### 5. Adversarial Review as Standard Practice

Every major conclusion should survive a deliberate attempt to disprove it.
Red-teaming is not a sign of distrust; it is how we build conviction.
A thesis that survives challenge is stronger for it.

### 6. Audience Awareness Over One-Size-Fits-All

The same finding requires different framing for different audiences.
We calibrate depth, language, and format to the reader. Delivering a deep
dive to a CEO or a brief to an analyst both represent failure.

### 7. Reproducibility Over Authority

A finding is only as good as its ability to be independently verified.
We document methodology, sources, and analytical steps so that another
competent researcher could reach the same conclusions.

## What This Means in Practice

| Principle                | In Practice                                 |
|--------------------------|---------------------------------------------|
| Truth over comfort       | Report contradictory evidence, even if unwelcome |
| Calibrated confidence    | Tag every finding with a confidence level    |
| Evidence hierarchy       | Weight findings by source tier, not count    |
| Transparency             | Disclose methodology, gaps, and limitations  |
| Adversarial review       | Red-team every major conclusion              |
| Audience awareness       | Calibrate every deliverable to the reader    |
| Reproducibility          | Document everything needed to verify         |

## The Research Standard We Aspire To

We aim for research that a demanding, knowledgeable critic would respect.
Not because it agrees with them, but because it is honest about what it
knows, transparent about how it got there, and clear about where it falls short.

## What We Are Not

- We are not a confirmation service. Do not request research to validate a
  predetermined conclusion.
- We are not a prediction market. We assess probabilities, not guarantees.
- We are not a substitute for domain expertise. We augment judgment; we do
  not replace it.

## Continuous Improvement

This philosophy is a living document. As we learn from our research,
our feedback, and our mistakes, these principles evolve. Contributions
and challenges to this philosophy are welcome per `docs/contribution-guide.md`.
