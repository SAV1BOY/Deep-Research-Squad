# Quality Gates Guide

## Purpose

This guide defines the quality gates that every research deliverable must
pass before delivery. Gates exist to catch errors, strengthen conclusions,
and maintain the squad's credibility.

## Gate Overview

| Gate   | Name                | When Applied          | Owner              |
|--------|---------------------|-----------------------|--------------------|
| Gate 1 | Scope Validation    | After scoping         | Research Director  |
| Gate 2 | Source Sufficiency   | After source collection | Source Hunter     |
| Gate 3 | Finding Integrity   | After analysis        | Quality Gate Keeper|
| Gate 4 | Adversarial Review  | After synthesis       | Red Team           |
| Gate 5 | Evidence Audit      | Before delivery       | Evidence Auditor   |
| Gate 6 | Delivery Readiness  | Before distribution   | Delivery Coordinator|

## Gate 1: Scope Validation

**Question:** Is the research question clear, feasible, and decision-relevant?

**Criteria:**
- Research question is specific and bounded.
- The decision the research informs is identified.
- Audience and format are defined.
- Timeline is realistic for the requested depth.
- Key terms are defined.

**Outcome:** Pass (proceed to research) or Return (re-scope with stakeholder).

## Gate 2: Source Sufficiency

**Question:** Do we have enough quality sources to answer the question?

**Criteria:**
- Minimum source count by evidence tier is met.
- Source diversity: at least two independent source types.
- No critical source type is missing without justification.
- Access to all listed sources is confirmed.

**Outcome:** Pass (proceed to analysis) or Flag (proceed with documented gap).

## Gate 3: Finding Integrity

**Question:** Are the findings sound, properly tagged, and well-supported?

**Criteria:**
- Every finding has a confidence tag.
- Every factual claim has a citation.
- Observations are separated from interpretations.
- Limitations are disclosed per finding.
- Language matches the evidence tier (no over-claiming).

**Outcome:** Pass (proceed to synthesis) or Return (revise findings).

## Gate 4: Adversarial Review

**Question:** Do the conclusions survive deliberate challenge?

**Criteria:**
- The prevailing thesis has been steel-manned.
- At least three challenge points have been raised.
- Alternative explanations have been considered.
- Base rates have been referenced where applicable.
- Falsification criteria are stated.

**Outcome:** Pass (thesis survives) or Weaken (revise confidence or conclusion).

## Gate 5: Evidence Audit

**Question:** Are all citations accurate and evidence tiers correctly assigned?

**Criteria:**
- Every citation is traceable to its source.
- Evidence tiers match the source characteristics.
- Interested-party sources are flagged.
- Access dates are recorded for web sources.
- No orphaned claims (claims without citations).

**Outcome:** Pass (proceed to delivery) or Return (fix citation issues).

## Gate 6: Delivery Readiness

**Question:** Is the deliverable formatted, calibrated, and ready for the audience?

**Criteria:**
- Format matches the specified channel.
- Audience depth calibration is correct.
- Tone matches the selected profile.
- Metadata header is complete (for cross-squad).
- Executive summary can stand alone.

**Outcome:** Pass (deliver) or Return (reformat or recalibrate).

## Bypassing Gates

Gates may be bypassed only with Research Director approval and:
- A documented reason for the bypass.
- An explicit caveat on the deliverable noting the skipped gate.
- A commitment to complete the gate retroactively when possible.

## Quality Check

- Are all six gates applied to every major deliverable?
- Is each gate outcome documented?
- Are bypasses rare, justified, and tracked?
