# Investor Memo Sources

## Purpose

This registry catalogs sources for investor memos, shareholder letters,
and investment analysis. These documents reveal strategic thinking,
market views, and capital allocation decisions from sophisticated investors.

## Tier 1: Public Shareholder Letters

### Berkshire Hathaway Annual Letters
- **Author**: Warren Buffett
- **Access**: berkshirehathaway.com/letters
- **Value**: Long-term thinking, honest self-assessment, plain language analysis
- **Frequency**: Annual

### Amazon Shareholder Letters
- **Author**: Jeff Bezos (historical), Andy Jassy (current)
- **Access**: aboutamazon.com/investor-relations
- **Value**: Technology strategy, customer obsession framework, long-term vision

### JPMorgan Annual Letter
- **Author**: Jamie Dimon
- **Access**: jpmorganchase.com
- **Value**: Financial sector analysis, macro-economic views, regulatory perspective

## Tier 2: VC/PE Investment Memos

### Published VC Memos
- **Benchmark (eBay memo)**: Classic investment thesis example
- **Sequoia**: Occasionally publishes investment frameworks
- **a16z**: Publishes market analyses (not individual memos but equivalent)
- **Union Square Ventures**: Blog posts function as investment memos

### Where to Find Investor Memos
- **SEC filings**: 13F filings show positions, some include commentary
- **Conference presentations**: Many investors present theses at conferences
- **Podcast transcripts**: Invest Like the Best, Capital Allocators, All-In
- **Substack/blogs**: Many fund managers publish analysis
- **Annual letters**: Tiger Global, Third Point, Pershing Square, Baupost

## Tier 3: Analyst Reports

### Sell-Side Research
- Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, JP Morgan equity research
- **Access**: Generally institutional only, summaries in media
- **Limitations**: Conflicts of interest (banking relationships)

### Independent Research
- **Gartner/Forrester**: Technology market analysis (paywalled)
- **CB Insights**: Startup and VC market data
- **PitchBook**: Private market data and analysis

## Notable Investment Memo Formats

### Standard VC Memo Structure
1. Company overview and team
2. Market opportunity (TAM/SAM/SOM)
3. Product and differentiation
4. Traction and metrics
5. Business model and unit economics
6. Competition
7. Risks and mitigants
8. Deal terms and valuation
9. Investment recommendation

## Usage Guidelines

1. Investor memos are advocacy documents — they argue for a position
2. Most valuable for market sizing, competitive framing, and risk analysis
3. Cross-reference investor views with independent research
4. Historical memos are valuable for understanding what was known at the time
5. Track investor track records before weighting their analysis
6. Distinguish between pre-investment memos (optimistic) and post-mortem analysis
