# Company Document Sources

## Purpose

This registry catalogs types of company documents useful for deep research.
Company-produced documents range from legally mandated (high reliability)
to marketing material (low reliability). Knowing the hierarchy is essential.

## Document Types by Reliability

### Tier 1: Legally Mandated (Highest Reliability)
These documents are subject to legal liability for misstatements.

**S-1 (IPO Registration)**
- Most comprehensive company disclosure ever produced
- Includes: business model, market sizing, risk factors, financials, competition
- Golden source for understanding a company's true position
- Where to find: SEC EDGAR

**10-K (Annual Report)**
- Audited financial statements
- MD&A (Management Discussion and Analysis) — strategic narrative
- Risk factors section — most honest about vulnerabilities
- Where to find: SEC EDGAR, company investor relations pages

**10-Q (Quarterly Report)**
- Reviewed (not audited) quarterly financials
- Interim MD&A updates
- Material changes since last 10-K

**Proxy Statement (DEF 14A)**
- Executive compensation details
- Board composition and governance
- Shareholder proposals and company responses

### Tier 2: Earnings Materials (High Reliability)
Not legally mandated but spoken under securities law constraints.

**Earnings Call Transcripts**
- Prepared remarks + Q&A with analysts
- Q&A reveals what management is willing/unwilling to discuss
- Where to find: Seeking Alpha, company IR pages, Bloomberg

**Earnings Presentations**
- Slide decks accompanying earnings calls
- Often contain metrics not in the 10-Q
- Where to find: Company IR pages

### Tier 3: Strategic Communications (Medium Reliability)
Intentional messaging but not legally constrained.

**Investor Day Presentations**
- Long-term strategy and targets
- Most detailed strategic roadmap publicly available

**Conference Presentations**
- Industry-specific positioning
- Competitive commentary (carefully worded)

### Tier 4: Marketing Materials (Lower Reliability)
Designed to promote, not to inform objectively.

**Press Releases**: Positive spin, selective data
**Blog Posts**: Thought leadership, positioning
**Case Studies**: Cherry-picked success stories
**Whitepapers**: Pseudo-research promoting products

## Research Squad Usage Rules

1. Always note the document tier when citing company sources
2. Prefer Tier 1-2 for factual claims about a company
3. Tier 3-4 useful for understanding strategy and positioning intent
4. Cross-reference company claims with independent sources
5. Marketing claims ("industry-leading", "best-in-class") are never evidence
6. Track changes between annual filings for strategic shifts
7. Read risk factors — they are the most honest section of any filing
