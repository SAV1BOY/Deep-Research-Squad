# Investigative Journalism Sources

## Purpose

This registry catalogs high-quality journalism sources that provide
investigative depth, market intelligence, and verified reporting. Quality
journalism often surfaces information before academic or official sources.

## Tier 1: Global Business and Finance

### Financial Times
- **Focus**: Global business, finance, economics, geopolitics
- **Access**: ft.com (paywalled, limited free articles)
- **Strengths**: Deep sourcing, global correspondent network, data journalism
- **Best for**: European business, global finance, regulatory analysis

### Bloomberg
- **Focus**: Finance, markets, economics, technology
- **Access**: bloomberg.com (paywalled), Bloomberg Terminal (institutional)
- **Strengths**: Real-time data, proprietary terminal data, investigative pieces
- **Best for**: Financial analysis, market data, company intelligence

### Reuters
- **Focus**: Global news, business, finance, legal
- **Access**: reuters.com (mostly free)
- **Strengths**: Wire service speed, global reach, legal intelligence
- **Best for**: Breaking news, cross-referencing, legal developments

### Wall Street Journal
- **Focus**: US business, finance, politics, technology
- **Access**: wsj.com (paywalled)
- **Strengths**: Deep US corporate reporting, investigative journalism
- **Best for**: US market intelligence, corporate governance stories

## Tier 2: Technology and Industry

### The Information
- **Focus**: Technology industry, startups, venture capital
- **Access**: theinformation.com (paywalled, premium)
- **Strengths**: Insider sourcing, breaks major tech stories
- **Best for**: VC/startup intelligence, tech company strategy

### Ars Technica
- **Focus**: Technology, science, policy
- **Access**: arstechnica.com (free)
- **Strengths**: Technical depth, long-form analysis

### STAT News
- **Focus**: Healthcare, biotech, pharma
- **Access**: statnews.com (free + premium)
- **Strengths**: Best healthcare-specific journalism

## Tier 3: Investigative Specialists

### New York Times Investigations
- Long-form investigative pieces, data journalism (nytimes.com)

### ProPublica
- Non-profit investigative journalism, datasets published (propublica.org)

### The Economist
- Data-driven analysis, global perspective (economist.com)

### ICIJ (International Consortium of Investigative Journalists)
- Cross-border investigations, Panama/Pandora Papers (icij.org)

## Usage Guidelines

1. Journalism is primary reporting, not peer-reviewed research
2. Distinguish news reporting (factual) from opinion/analysis
3. Check if the outlet has issued corrections on the specific story
4. Use journalism to identify leads, then verify with primary sources
5. Wire services (Reuters, AP) are most neutral; others have editorial lens
6. Paywalled sources often have higher quality due to subscriber model
7. Always note the date — journalism is time-sensitive
