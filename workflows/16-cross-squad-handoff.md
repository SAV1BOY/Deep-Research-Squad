# Workflow 16: Cross-Squad Handoff

## Purpose
Package and deliver completed research to other squads with sufficient context, recommendations, and supporting materials so the receiving squad can act on the findings without needing to repeat the research or ask extensive clarifying questions.

## Trigger
- Research is complete and audit-passed, ready for delivery to another squad.
- Another squad requests research output to support their own work.
- Research Lead identifies that findings have implications for other squads.

## Agents Involved
- **Research Lead**: Approves the handoff package and manages the delivery.
- **Handoff Coordinator**: Assembles the package and manages the transfer.
- **Synthesis Writer**: Adapts research narrative for the receiving squad's context.
- **Receiving Squad Liaison**: Representative from the receiving squad who accepts the handoff.

## Inputs
- Executive brief from Workflow 14.
- Full research output (all synthesis layers, models, decision brief).
- Audit report from Workflow 13.
- Receiving squad profile (their mission, current priorities, technical context).
- Any specific questions or needs the receiving squad has communicated.

## Steps

1. **Identify receiving squad needs**: Before packaging, understand what the receiving squad actually needs:
   - Are they making a decision based on this research?
   - Are they using it as input to their own research or project?
   - Do they need the full depth or just key findings?
   - What is their domain expertise (do they need context that we take for granted)?
   - Contact the Receiving Squad Liaison to confirm requirements.

2. **Select handoff package level**: Choose the appropriate depth:
   - **Full package**: All research artifacts, for squads that will build on the research.
   - **Standard package**: Executive brief + decision brief + key models, for squads that need to act on findings.
   - **Light package**: Executive brief + key recommendations only, for squads that need awareness but not depth.
   - Document the selection rationale.

3. **Adapt context for receiving squad**: Rewrite the context sections:
   - Translate domain-specific terminology that the receiving squad may not share.
   - Highlight the implications that are most relevant to their mission.
   - Remove or de-emphasize findings that are not relevant to their needs.
   - Add any bridging context that connects research findings to their domain.

4. **Create action recommendations**: Translate findings into actions specific to the receiving squad:
   - "Based on our research, your squad should consider..."
   - "This finding affects your current project in the following way..."
   - "We recommend your squad prioritize X because..."
   - Make recommendations concrete and actionable, not abstract.

5. **Document assumptions and limitations**: Explicitly state:
   - Key assumptions underlying the research that the receiving squad should validate.
   - Limitations that affect how the findings can be applied.
   - Confidence levels for all findings (so they know what to trust fully vs. verify).
   - Areas where the research is thin and the receiving squad should conduct their own investigation.

6. **Include source access information**: Ensure the receiving squad can verify and extend the research:
   - List key sources with access instructions (URLs, database names, API endpoints).
   - Note any sources that require special access or credentials.
   - Highlight sources that are time-sensitive (data that will be updated or removed).
   - Provide the source chain documentation for critical findings.

7. **Prepare questions and discussion points**: Anticipate what the receiving squad will want to discuss:
   - List 3-5 likely follow-up questions with prepared answers.
   - Identify areas where a briefing conversation would be more effective than a document.
   - Suggest a handoff meeting agenda if the research is complex.

8. **Assemble the handoff package**: Compile all elements into a structured delivery:
   - Cover page: Research title, ID, date, quality score, package level.
   - Executive brief (tailored to receiving squad).
   - Action recommendations.
   - Assumptions and limitations statement.
   - Key models and visuals.
   - Source access information.
   - Prepared Q&A.
   - Link to full research archive (for standard and full packages).

9. **Deliver and confirm receipt**: Send the package to the Receiving Squad Liaison:
   - Notify them of the delivery with a brief summary.
   - Confirm receipt and initial review within 48 hours.
   - Offer a briefing session for complex handoffs.
   - Log the handoff in the research registry.

10. **Post-handoff support window**: Maintain availability for questions:
    - Designate a point of contact from the research squad for 2 weeks post-handoff.
    - Answer clarifying questions promptly.
    - If the receiving squad identifies issues with the research, escalate to Research Lead.
    - After the support window, close the handoff formally and log any feedback.

## Quality Gates
- Receiving squad needs must be confirmed before packaging begins.
- Package level must be appropriate for the receiving squad's stated needs.
- Context must be adapted with no unexplained domain jargon.
- Action recommendations must be specific and actionable.
- Assumptions and limitations must be explicitly documented.
- Receipt must be confirmed within 48 hours of delivery.

## Outputs
- Handoff package (tailored to receiving squad, at appropriate depth level).
- Action recommendations specific to the receiving squad.
- Prepared Q&A document.
- Handoff log entry in the research registry.
- Post-handoff support contact assignment.
