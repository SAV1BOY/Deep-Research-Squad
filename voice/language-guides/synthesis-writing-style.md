# Synthesis Writing Style Guide

## Purpose

This guide defines how to write syntheses that combine multiple research inputs
into layered, actionable intelligence. A synthesis is not a summary. It creates
new understanding by connecting findings across sources and domains.

## What Makes a Synthesis Different

| Document Type | Purpose                        | Key Trait                   |
|---------------|--------------------------------|-----------------------------|
| Summary       | Condense a single source       | Compression                 |
| Analysis      | Examine one topic deeply       | Depth                       |
| Synthesis     | Connect multiple inputs        | Integration and implication  |

## Synthesis Structure

### Layer 1: What the Sources Say (Convergence)

Present the areas where multiple inputs agree:
- "Across [N] sources, the consistent finding is..."
- "Both the quantitative analysis and the expert interviews point to..."
- "The market data and the regulatory signals converge on..."

### Layer 2: Where They Disagree (Divergence)

Surface conflicts honestly:
- "The primary tension is between [View A] and [View B]."
- "Source X emphasizes risk, while Source Y emphasizes opportunity."
- "The disagreement centers on the assumption that..."

### Layer 3: What Emerges (Integration)

This is the synthesis layer. Connect dots the individual sources do not:
- "Taken together, these findings suggest a pattern of..."
- "The combination of [Factor A] and [Factor B] creates a dynamic where..."
- "No single source captures this, but the aggregate picture shows..."

### Layer 4: What It Means (Implications)

Translate integration into consequences:
- "For decision-makers, this means..."
- "The strategic implication is..."
- "If this pattern holds, the likely outcome is..."

### Layer 5: What Remains Open (Gaps)

Identify what the synthesis cannot resolve:
- "The synthesis does not address..."
- "A key gap is whether..."
- "Further investigation is needed on..."

## Writing Techniques

### Connective Language

Use transitions that signal synthesis, not just sequence:
- "This finding gains significance when combined with..."
- "Viewed alongside [other finding], a pattern emerges..."
- "Individually, these are incremental. Together, they signal..."

### Attribution in Synthesis

Maintain source traceability even in integrated sections:
- "Drawing on [Source A] for market data and [Source B] for regulatory context..."
- "The synthesis integrates findings from Agents 3, 7, and 12."

### Avoiding Summary Traps

- Do not list findings sequentially without connecting them.
- Do not give equal weight to all inputs; prioritize by evidence strength.
- Do not introduce new primary research in the synthesis; it integrates existing work.

## Quality Check

- Does the synthesis create understanding that no single source provides alone?
- Are all five layers present?
- Is source attribution maintained even in integrated sections?
- Are implications clearly derived from the synthesis, not assumed?
- Are gaps honestly disclosed?
