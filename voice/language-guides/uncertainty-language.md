# Uncertainty Language Guide

## Purpose

This guide defines how to communicate uncertainty without undermining credibility.
Uncertainty is not weakness; it is precision. The goal is to be honest about
what is known, probable, and unknown while maintaining authority.

## The Uncertainty Spectrum

Use these phrases in order of decreasing certainty:

1. **Known / Established:** "The data confirms..." / "It is established that..."
2. **Highly Probable:** "The evidence strongly indicates..." / "With high confidence..."
3. **Probable:** "The balance of evidence suggests..." / "It is likely that..."
4. **Plausible:** "A reasonable interpretation is..." / "This is consistent with..."
5. **Possible:** "One possibility is..." / "It cannot be ruled out that..."
6. **Speculative:** "If we extrapolate beyond the data..." / "A speculative reading..."
7. **Unknown:** "We do not have evidence on this point." / "This remains an open question."

## Rules for Uncertainty Language

### Do: Be Specific About What Is Uncertain

- "The market size is estimated between $2B and $4B, depending on the definition of..."
- "We are confident in the direction but not the magnitude."
- "The timing is uncertain; estimates range from Q3 2026 to Q1 2027."

### Do: Quantify When Possible

- "Approximately 60-70% of analysts surveyed..."
- "In 3 of 5 comparable cases, the outcome was..."
- "The confidence interval spans..."

### Do: Attribute the Uncertainty

- "Uncertainty arises because the data is from 2023 and market conditions have shifted."
- "The key unknown is whether regulatory approval will be granted."
- "This estimate depends on the assumption that..."

### Don't: Use Weasel Words

Avoid phrases that create vagueness without information:
- "It may or may not be the case..." (say which is more likely)
- "Some experts believe..." (which experts? how many?)
- "There is some evidence..." (how much? what quality?)
- "It is not impossible that..." (state the positive case)

### Don't: Hedge Every Sentence

One well-placed uncertainty statement is better than hedging every clause.
State your confidence level once per finding, not once per sentence.

## Structural Approaches

### The Confidence Bracket

Open with the finding, close with the confidence level:
> "Revenue grew 15% year-over-year. This figure is based on reported earnings
> and is considered reliable (Tier 1 evidence)."

### The Scenario Approach

When uncertainty is high, present scenarios instead of point estimates:
> "Under Scenario A (regulatory approval on time), market entry is Q4 2026.
> Under Scenario B (6-month delay), market entry shifts to Q2 2027."

### The Known-Unknown Map

For complex topics, explicitly map what is known, probable, and unknown:
> "Known: the partnership was signed. Probable: launch in H2. Unknown: pricing model."

## Quality Check

- Is uncertainty stated precisely, not vaguely?
- Is the source of uncertainty identified?
- Does the writing still project competence and authority?
- Are hedges minimal and concentrated, not scattered?
