# Formality Calibration Guide

## Purpose and Scope

This guide defines a 5-level formality scale for all research outputs produced by the Deep Research Squad. It ensures consistent tone matching across different output types and audiences. Use this guide when determining how formal or informal the language, structure, and presentation of a deliverable should be.

## Formality Scale

### Level 1 -- Casual

**When to use:** Internal team chat, quick status updates, brainstorming notes.

**Characteristics:**
- Contractions and informal phrasing allowed
- Bullet points over full sentences
- First person ("I found that..." / "We think...")
- Minimal formatting structure

**Example:**
> Checked the three main sources -- two agree on the 2024 timeline, one doesn't. Leaning toward the majority view but want to dig more before calling it.

---

### Level 2 -- Conversational

**When to use:** Internal summaries, draft research briefs, stakeholder check-ins.

**Characteristics:**
- Clear and approachable but purposeful
- Light use of contractions
- Section headers but flexible structure
- Direct address acceptable ("You may notice...")

**Example:**
> Based on our review of the available literature, two of three sources support the projected 2024 timeline. We recommend additional verification before finalizing this finding.

---

### Level 3 -- Professional (Default)

**When to use:** Standard research reports, client-facing summaries, most deliverables.

**Characteristics:**
- No contractions
- Complete sentences with clear topic structure
- Third person preferred ("The analysis indicates...")
- Defined sections: summary, findings, methodology, limitations

**Example:**
> The analysis of available literature indicates that two of three primary sources support the projected 2024 timeline. Further verification is recommended before this finding is incorporated into the final assessment.

---

### Level 4 -- Formal

**When to use:** Executive briefings, regulatory submissions, published reports.

**Characteristics:**
- Precise, measured language
- Passive voice acceptable where appropriate
- Full citations inline or in footnotes
- Structured with numbered sections and cross-references

**Example:**
> An examination of the primary literature reveals that the preponderance of evidence (two of three sources reviewed) supports the projected 2024 timeline. It is recommended that additional corroboration be sought prior to the inclusion of this finding in the final deliverable (see Section 4.2, Limitations).

---

### Level 5 -- Academic/Regulatory

**When to use:** Peer-review-ready documents, legal or compliance reports, formal policy analysis.

**Characteristics:**
- Discipline-specific terminology used precisely
- Passive voice standard
- Formal citation style (APA, IEEE, or as specified)
- Explicit methodology and limitations sections required
- Hedging language for uncertain claims ("The evidence suggests...")

**Example:**
> A systematic review of the extant literature (n=3 primary sources) indicates that a majority of sources corroborate the projected 2024 timeline (Smith et al., 2025; Chen & Patel, 2025). However, one source presents a conflicting projection (Lee, 2025), warranting further investigation prior to inclusion in the final assessment.

---

## Selecting the Right Level

| Output Type               | Default Level | Adjustable Range |
|----------------------------|:---:|:---:|
| Internal notes             | 1   | 1-2 |
| Draft briefs               | 2   | 1-3 |
| Standard research reports  | 3   | 2-4 |
| Executive briefings        | 4   | 3-5 |
| Regulatory/academic docs   | 5   | 4-5 |

When in doubt, default to Level 3 (Professional) and adjust based on the audience and delivery channel.
