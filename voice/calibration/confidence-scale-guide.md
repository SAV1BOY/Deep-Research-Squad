# Confidence Scale Guide

## Purpose

This guide defines the squad's four-level confidence scale. Every finding,
conclusion, and recommendation must be tagged with a confidence level.
Consistent use prevents overstatement and understatement.

## The Four Levels

### Level 1: We Know

**Definition:** Confirmed by multiple independent, reliable sources. The finding
is robust and unlikely to change with additional evidence.

**When to use:**
- Multiple primary sources converge.
- Data is recent, verified, and from Tier 1-2 sources.
- No credible contradictory evidence exists.

**Language:**
- "We are confident that..."
- "The evidence confirms..."
- "This is established."

**Symbol:** [CONFIRMED]

---

### Level 2: Probable

**Definition:** Supported by credible evidence but with some residual uncertainty.
The finding is more likely true than not, and additional evidence would likely confirm it.

**When to use:**
- Strong secondary sources agree, or one primary source without contradiction.
- The direction is clear but magnitude or timing is uncertain.
- Minor gaps exist but do not undermine the core finding.

**Language:**
- "The evidence strongly suggests..."
- "It is probable that..."
- "With reasonable confidence..."

**Symbol:** [PROBABLE]

---

### Level 3: Hypothesis

**Definition:** A plausible interpretation of available evidence, but alternatives
exist and the finding could change with additional data.

**When to use:**
- Evidence is limited, indirect, or from lower-tier sources.
- Multiple interpretations of the same data are viable.
- The finding is logically sound but empirically thin.

**Language:**
- "Our working hypothesis is..."
- "Preliminary evidence suggests..."
- "One plausible interpretation is..."

**Symbol:** [HYPOTHESIS]

---

### Level 4: Unknown

**Definition:** Insufficient evidence to form a judgment. The squad acknowledges
the gap and may recommend further investigation.

**When to use:**
- No reliable sources address the question.
- Available evidence is contradictory with no resolution.
- The question is outside the scope of available data.

**Language:**
- "We do not have sufficient evidence to assess..."
- "This remains an open question."
- "Further investigation is required."

**Symbol:** [UNKNOWN]

## Application Rules

1. **Tag every finding.** No finding goes untagged.
2. **One level per finding.** Do not blend levels within a single finding.
3. **Justify the level.** Briefly explain why this level was assigned.
4. **Upgrade and downgrade transparently.** If confidence changes, state what changed.
5. **Default to lower.** When in doubt between two levels, choose the lower one.

## Confidence in Recommendations

Recommendations inherit the confidence of their underlying evidence:
- Level 1-2 findings support strong recommendations.
- Level 3 findings support conditional recommendations ("if confirmed, then...").
- Level 4 findings do not support recommendations; they support further research.

## Common Mistakes

- Stating Level 3 findings with Level 1 language.
- Failing to tag findings at all (readers assume Level 1).
- Using different confidence language across documents.
- Not explaining what would change the confidence level.
