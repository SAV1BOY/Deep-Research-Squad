# Executive Brief Delivery Guide

## Purpose

This guide defines how to structure and deliver executive briefs. An executive
brief distills research into a decision-ready format. The reader should be able
to act on it within 90 seconds.

## When to Use an Executive Brief

- The audience is VP-level or above.
- A decision or escalation is required.
- The underlying research is complete and a deep dive exists.
- Time-sensitive findings need to reach leadership quickly.

## Structure: The 1-3-1 Format

### Line 1: Bottom Line (1 Sentence)

State the conclusion. No preamble, no context-setting.
- "Market entry should be delayed to Q4 due to regulatory uncertainty."
- "Competitor X will likely announce a competing product within 60 days."

### Block 2: Supporting Evidence (3 Bullets)

Three strongest data points that support the bottom line.
Each bullet: one fact, one source reference, one implication.
- "Revenue in the target segment grew 23% YoY (Source: industry report, Tier 2)."
- "Regulatory review timelines have extended by 45 days on average (Source: CVM data)."
- "Two of three comparable market entries in 2025 were delayed (Source: case analysis)."

### Line 3: Recommended Action (1 Sentence)

What you recommend the reader do, with a deadline if applicable.
- "We recommend pausing the launch and revisiting in 30 days pending regulatory clarity."

## Additional Elements (Optional)

### Confidence Statement

One sentence after the 1-3-1 block:
- "Confidence: High. Based on converging primary and secondary sources."

### Risk Flag

If there is a critical risk, add one line:
- "Key risk: If competitor announces first, our window narrows significantly."

### Link to Full Analysis

Always include:
- "Full analysis: [link to deep dive]"

## Delivery Protocol

1. **Deliver via the stakeholder's preferred channel** (email, Slack, document).
2. **Subject line is the bottom line.** Do not use generic subjects.
   - Good: "Recommend delay: regulatory risk to Q3 launch"
   - Bad: "Research update - market analysis"
3. **No attachments unless requested.** The brief should be in the body of the message.
4. **Flag urgency explicitly.** If time-sensitive, state the decision deadline.
5. **Follow up in 24 hours** if no response and the decision has a deadline.

## Formatting Rules

- No paragraphs longer than 3 sentences.
- No bullet points longer than 2 lines.
- Bold the bottom line and the recommended action.
- Use numbers, not adjectives, for magnitude.

## Common Mistakes

- Including methodology in the brief (link to it instead).
- Leading with context instead of the conclusion.
- Presenting more than 3 supporting points (prioritize).
- Hedging the bottom line (state confidence separately).
- Forgetting the recommended action.

## Quality Check

- Can the reader decide from the first sentence?
- Are supporting points the strongest three available?
- Is the recommended action specific and time-bound?
- Does the brief fit on half a page?
- Is there a link to the full analysis?
