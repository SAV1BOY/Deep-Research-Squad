# Deep Dive Delivery Guide

## Purpose

This guide defines how to structure and deliver deep dive research documents.
A deep dive is the squad's most comprehensive output format, intended for
audiences that need full evidence, methodology, and nuance.

## When to Use a Deep Dive

- The question requires analysis of multiple sources across multiple dimensions.
- The stakeholder needs to understand not just "what" but "why" and "how confident."
- The findings will inform a significant decision or strategy.
- The topic has sufficient complexity to warrant 10+ pages.

## Structure

### 1. Executive Summary (Half Page)

- Bottom line finding in 1-2 sentences.
- Three key supporting points.
- Confidence level and primary caveat.
- This section must stand alone for senior readers.

### 2. Research Question and Scope (Half Page)

- The specific question(s) being answered.
- What is in scope and what is explicitly excluded.
- Why this question matters now.

### 3. Methodology (1 Page)

- Sources consulted (count and type).
- Analytical frameworks applied.
- Timeframe of data collection.
- Known limitations of the approach.

### 4. Findings (Core Section, 5-10 Pages)

Organize by theme, hypothesis, or chronology:
- Each finding gets a heading, evidence summary, and confidence tag.
- Use evidence tiers consistently (see `evidence-language.md`).
- Include supporting data: tables, charts, quotes with attribution.
- Flag where sources converge and where they diverge.

### 5. Analysis and Synthesis (2-3 Pages)

- What the findings mean when taken together.
- Patterns, implications, and second-order effects.
- Comparison with prior research or base rates.
- See `synthesis-writing-style.md` for structure.

### 6. Limitations and Gaps (Half Page)

- What the analysis cannot answer.
- Key unknowns that remain.
- Suggested follow-up research.

### 7. Recommendations (1 Page)

- Actionable recommendations tied to findings.
- Each recommendation tagged with the confidence of its underlying evidence.
- Options format when appropriate (see `operator.md` tone profile).

### 8. Appendices

- Full source list with access dates.
- Raw data tables or extended charts.
- Methodology details for reproducibility.

## Delivery Protocol

1. **Internal review first.** Run through quality gates before delivery.
2. **Attach the executive summary as the cover.** The first thing the reader sees.
3. **Provide a 2-sentence Slack summary** with a link to the full document.
4. **Offer a walkthrough.** For complex topics, offer a 15-minute briefing.
5. **Track questions.** Log follow-up questions to inform future research.

## Formatting Standards

- Use consistent heading hierarchy (H1 for title, H2 for sections, H3 for subsections).
- Number all figures and tables.
- Use the squad's confidence tags: [CONFIRMED], [PROBABLE], [HYPOTHESIS], [UNKNOWN].
- Date the document prominently.

## Quality Check

- Does the executive summary stand alone?
- Are all findings tagged with confidence levels?
- Is the methodology reproducible from the description?
- Are limitations honestly stated?
- Can the reader find the full source for any claim?
