# Presentation Delivery Guide

## Purpose

This guide defines how to present research findings in live or recorded
presentations. Presentations are high-stakes, time-constrained, and require
a different structure than written deliverables.

## When to Use a Presentation

- Stakeholders request a walkthrough of findings.
- The topic is complex enough to benefit from narration and Q&A.
- A decision meeting requires structured input.
- The audience is cross-functional and needs context bridging.

## Presentation Structure

### Slide 1: Title and Context

- Research question in plain language.
- Date, squad, and author(s).
- Classification: Deep Dive / Brief / Update.

### Slide 2: Bottom Line Up Front

- One sentence: the core finding.
- One sentence: the confidence level.
- One sentence: the recommended action.
- This slide should stand alone if the meeting is cut short.

### Slides 3-5: Key Findings

One slide per major finding:
- Headline: the finding in one sentence.
- Evidence: 2-3 supporting data points with source attribution.
- Visual: one chart, table, or diagram per slide.
- Confidence tag on each finding.

### Slide 6: Synthesis

- What the findings mean taken together.
- Implications for the audience's domain.
- Second-order effects or emerging patterns.

### Slide 7: Limitations and Gaps

- What the research does not cover.
- Key unknowns that remain.
- Suggested follow-up.

### Slide 8: Recommendation and Next Steps

- Clear recommendation with rationale.
- Decision required from the audience.
- Timeline for next steps.

### Appendix Slides

- Methodology details.
- Full source list.
- Additional data tables.
- These are not presented but available for Q&A.

## Delivery Rules

### Timing

| Presentation Type  | Duration | Slides (Excl. Appendix) |
|--------------------|----------|-------------------------|
| Executive briefing | 10 min   | 5-6                     |
| Team walkthrough   | 20 min   | 8-10                    |
| Deep dive review   | 30 min   | 10-12                   |
| Board summary      | 5 min    | 3-4                     |

### Narration Guidelines

- Do not read the slides. The slide is the visual; you are the narrative.
- State the bottom line in the first 60 seconds.
- Use transitions that signal synthesis: "This matters because..."
- Pause after key findings for questions.

### Q&A Preparation

- Anticipate the top 5 questions and prepare answers.
- Have appendix slides ready for "how did you get this number?" questions.
- If you do not know the answer, say so and commit to a follow-up.

## Visual Standards

- One idea per slide.
- Maximum 6 bullet points per slide, 8 words per bullet.
- Use the squad's confidence color coding: green (confirmed), yellow (probable),
  orange (hypothesis), gray (unknown).
- Source attribution on every data slide.

## Common Mistakes

- Starting with methodology instead of findings.
- Too many slides (the audience loses the thread).
- No clear ask or recommended action.
- Reading slides verbatim.
- Not preparing for Q&A.

## Quality Check

- Can the audience leave after slide 2 with the key message?
- Is every finding tagged with confidence?
- Is there a clear decision or action requested?
- Are appendix slides ready for tough questions?
- Does the presentation fit the allocated time?
