# Investigative Tone Profile

## Purpose

This profile defines the writing voice for investigative and discovery-oriented work.
The investigative voice follows evidence wherever it leads and flags what does not fit.

## Core Principles

1. **Question everything.** Start with what is claimed, then test it.
2. **Follow contradictions.** When sources disagree, that is the story.
3. **Surface weak signals.** Small anomalies can indicate large patterns.
4. **Separate what is known from what is assumed.** Make the boundary visible.
5. **Maintain intellectual honesty.** Report what was found, not what was expected.

## Voice Characteristics

| Attribute         | Target                                |
|-------------------|---------------------------------------|
| Sentence length   | 10-22 words average                   |
| Paragraph length  | 2-5 sentences                         |
| Jargon            | Minimal; use plain language           |
| Passive voice     | Avoid; attribute actions to actors    |
| Hedging           | Explicit and calibrated               |

## Structure Pattern

- **The Question:** What triggered the investigation and what is at stake.
- **What We Found:** Organized chronologically or by source.
- **Where Sources Agree:** Convergent evidence, listed with attribution.
- **Where Sources Conflict:** Divergent evidence, with possible explanations.
- **What Remains Unknown:** Explicit gaps, with suggested next steps.
- **Implications:** What the findings mean if confirmed.

## Signal Detection Language

- "Source A states X, but Source B reports Y. The discrepancy may be explained by..."
- "This data point is inconsistent with the prevailing narrative because..."
- "We found no evidence to support the claim that..."
- "A pattern emerges when comparing..."

## Language Dos

- "The evidence suggests, but does not confirm..."
- "We identified a discrepancy between..."
- "This warrants further investigation because..."
- "The absence of X is itself notable."

## Language Don'ts

- "It is a well-known fact that..."
- Accepting claims at face value without verification
- Ignoring contradictory evidence
- Editorializing ("shockingly," "alarmingly")

## When to Use

- Source validation and cross-referencing
- Anomaly investigation
- Competitive intelligence
- Due diligence analysis

## Quality Check

Before delivering in investigative tone, verify:
- Are all claims attributed to specific sources?
- Are contradictions surfaced, not buried?
- Is the boundary between known and unknown explicit?
- Have weak signals been documented, even if unexplained?
- Is the tone neutral, not sensational?
