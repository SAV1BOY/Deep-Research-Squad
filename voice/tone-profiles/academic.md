# Academic Tone Profile

## Purpose

This profile defines the writing voice for research outputs that require
scholarly rigor, proper citation, and engagement with the state of the art.

## Core Principles

1. **Engage the literature.** Position findings relative to existing work.
2. **Cite everything.** Every factual claim, framework, or borrowed concept needs attribution.
3. **Be rigorous in structure.** Follow established academic conventions.
4. **Distinguish contribution from prior work.** Make it clear what is new.
5. **Use precise qualifiers.** "Some studies suggest" is different from "the consensus holds."

## Voice Characteristics

| Attribute         | Target                                  |
|-------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| Sentence length   | 18-30 words average                     |
| Paragraph length  | 4-8 sentences                           |
| Jargon            | Discipline-specific, always defined     |
| Passive voice     | Acceptable per convention               |
| Hedging           | Careful and conventional                |

## Structure Pattern

- **Introduction:** Problem statement, significance, research question.
- **Literature Review:** Prior work organized thematically, identifying gaps.
- **Methodology:** Approach, data sources, analytical framework.
- **Findings:** Results presented without interpretation.
- **Discussion:** Interpretation, comparison with prior work, implications.
- **Limitations and Future Work:** Explicit boundaries and next steps.
- **References:** Complete bibliography in consistent format.

## Citation Protocol

- Use author-date format inline: (Smith, 2024).
- For direct quotes, include page numbers: (Smith, 2024, p. 42).
- For multiple sources supporting one claim, list chronologically.
- Distinguish primary sources from secondary citations.
- See `voice/language-guides/citation-style.md` for full guidelines.

## Language Dos

- "Prior research has established that... (Author, Year)."
- "This finding extends the work of..."
- "Consistent with the framework proposed by..."
- "A gap exists in the current literature regarding..."

## Language Don'ts

- Uncited factual claims
- First person singular ("I found") -- use "we" or passive
- Colloquial language or idioms
- Overgeneralizing from limited evidence

## When to Use

- Literature reviews and meta-analyses
- State-of-the-art surveys
- Methodology papers
- Research proposals and white papers

## Quality Check

Before delivering in academic tone, verify:
- Is every factual claim cited?
- Is the literature review current (within 2-3 years for fast-moving fields)?
- Are contributions clearly distinguished from prior work?
- Does the structure follow discipline conventions?
- Are limitations honestly stated?
