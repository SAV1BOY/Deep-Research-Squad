# Cross-Squad Delivery Prompts - Language Patterns

## Purpose

These language patterns help agents communicate research findings to
other squads. Cross-squad delivery requires extra context, explicit
assumptions, and domain translation.

## Context-Setting Phrases

### Introducing the Deliverable

- "This research was produced by [squad/agent] in response to [trigger/request]."
- "The following findings are relevant to your work on [their project/decision]."
- "We are sharing this because [specific relevance to their domain]."
- "This delivers on the request from [date/channel] regarding [topic]."

### Establishing Scope

- "This analysis covers [scope]. It does not cover [excluded areas]."
- "The timeframe of the data is [start] to [end]."
- "We examined [N] sources, primarily [source types]."
- "Methodology details are available in the full report: [link]."

## Translation Phrases

### For Technical to Business Translation

- "In business terms, this means [plain language equivalent]."
- "The technical finding translates to [business impact]."
- "For your team, the key takeaway is [actionable insight]."
- "The bottom line for [their domain]: [one-sentence summary]."

### For Research to Operations Translation

- "The research suggests [finding]. The operational implication is [action]."
- "If acting on this finding, the key considerations are [list]."
- "This finding affects your [process/decision/timeline] because [reason]."

### For Quantitative to Qualitative Translation

- "The data shows [metric]. In practical terms, this means [interpretation]."
- "The [X]% change translates to approximately [real-world impact]."
- "For context, this is [comparable to / larger than / smaller than] [reference point]."

## Confidence Communication Phrases

- "Confidence in this finding: [level]. Based on [evidence summary]."
- "We are [confident / moderately confident / uncertain] about [specific aspect]."
- "Key caveat: [caveat]. This should be factored into your planning."
- "The finding is directionally reliable. The magnitude is less certain."

## Handoff Phrases

### Transferring Ownership

- "This deliverable is now with your squad. We recommend [next steps]."
- "We are available for follow-up questions through [channel] until [date]."
- "If you need additional depth on [aspect], please request via [process]."
- "The analysis is complete from our side. Suggested actions for your team: [list]."

### Requesting Feedback

- "Please confirm receipt and flag any questions by [date]."
- "We welcome feedback on whether this format meets your needs."
- "If this triggers additional research needs, please route through [process]."
- "Let us know if the confidence level affects your decision timeline."

## Formatting for Cross-Squad

Always include the metadata header:
```
From: [Squad / Agent]
To: [Receiving Squad / Role]
Date: [Date]
Confidence: [Level]
Scope: [Brief scope statement]
```

## Common Mistakes

- Assuming shared context or vocabulary.
- Using internal shorthand or acronyms without definition.
- Delivering raw findings without domain-specific interpretation.
- Omitting confidence levels (recipients default to "confirmed").
- Not specifying what is out of scope (recipients assume full coverage).

## Quality Check

- Would someone outside your squad understand this without follow-up?
- Is every technical term defined or translated?
- Are confidence levels explicit for each finding?
- Is the relevance to the recipient's work stated?
- Is the handoff clean (clear next steps, contact channel, deadline)?
