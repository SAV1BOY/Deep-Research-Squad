# Confidence Language - Phrase Library

## Purpose

This phrase library provides standardized language for expressing confidence
levels in research findings. Consistent confidence language prevents readers
from over- or under-weighting findings.

## Confidence Level Phrases

### Level 1: Confirmed (We Know)

Use when multiple independent, reliable sources converge.

- "We are confident that [finding]."
- "The evidence confirms [finding]."
- "This is well-supported by [N] independent sources."
- "We can state with confidence that [finding]."
- "The data is conclusive on this point."

**Supporting phrases:**
- "Confirmed by [source types]."
- "Corroborated across [N] independent data sets."
- "No credible evidence contradicts this finding."

### Level 2: Probable (Strong Evidence)

Use when evidence is strong but not fully conclusive.

- "The evidence strongly suggests [finding]."
- "It is probable that [finding], based on [evidence summary]."
- "With reasonable confidence, we assess that [finding]."
- "The weight of evidence supports [finding]."
- "Our assessment, at high confidence, is [finding]."

**Supporting phrases:**
- "Based on [N] sources, primarily Tier [1-2]."
- "Consistent with [known pattern / prior research]."
- "Minor uncertainties remain regarding [specific aspect]."

### Level 3: Hypothesis (Working Assessment)

Use when evidence supports a direction but alternatives remain viable.

- "Our working hypothesis is [finding]."
- "Preliminary evidence points to [finding]."
- "One plausible interpretation is [finding]."
- "Early indicators suggest [finding], pending further validation."
- "We tentatively assess that [finding]."

**Supporting phrases:**
- "Based on limited evidence from [sources]."
- "Alternative interpretations include [alternatives]."
- "This assessment may change as more data becomes available."

### Level 4: Unknown (Insufficient Evidence)

Use when evidence is absent or contradictory without resolution.

- "We do not have sufficient evidence to assess [topic]."
- "This remains an open question."
- "Available evidence is inconclusive."
- "We cannot make a confident assessment at this time."
- "Further research is needed before any judgment is warranted."

**Supporting phrases:**
- "No reliable sources address this question."
- "Existing evidence is contradictory and unresolved."
- "We recommend [specific research] to close this gap."

## Confidence Transitions

When confidence changes between versions:

### Upgrading

- "Our confidence has increased from [level] to [level] because [new evidence]."
- "Previously a hypothesis, this is now probable based on [what changed]."
- "New data from [source] corroborates our earlier tentative finding."

### Downgrading

- "Our confidence has decreased from [level] to [level] because [contradictory evidence]."
- "Previously assessed as probable, we now treat this as a hypothesis due to [reason]."
- "New information calls into question our earlier finding that [finding]."

## Formatting Conventions

- Tag findings inline: "[CONFIRMED] Market size exceeds $5B."
- Use consistent tags: [CONFIRMED], [PROBABLE], [HYPOTHESIS], [UNKNOWN].
- In tables, add a Confidence column.
- In executive briefs, state confidence once for the overall assessment.

## Quality Check

- Is every finding tagged with a confidence level?
- Does the language match the tag (no Level 1 language for Level 3 findings)?
- Are confidence transitions explained when they occur?
- Is the reader clear on what would change the confidence level?
