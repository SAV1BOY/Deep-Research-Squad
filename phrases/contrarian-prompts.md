# Contrarian Analysis Prompts - Language Patterns

## Purpose

These language patterns support red-team analysis, thesis stress-testing,
and deliberate challenge of prevailing conclusions. The contrarian role
strengthens research by attempting to break it.

## Steel-Manning Phrases

### Presenting the Strongest Version of the Thesis

- "The strongest version of this thesis is: [thesis at its best]."
- "Proponents of this view would argue that [strongest argument]."
- "The best evidence supporting this conclusion is [evidence]."
- "Even a skeptic must acknowledge that [strongest point in favor]."
- "Before challenging this thesis, we note its strongest foundations: [list]."

## Challenge Phrases

### Identifying Weaknesses

- "The thesis assumes [assumption]. If this assumption is wrong, [consequence]."
- "The strongest argument against this thesis is [argument]."
- "This conclusion depends on [fragile condition] that may not hold because [reason]."
- "The evidence cited is [limitation: old / single-source / interested-party]."
- "The analysis overlooks [overlooked factor] which could change the conclusion."

### Questioning the Evidence Base

- "The supporting evidence is [N] sources deep. Is that sufficient for this claim?"
- "All cited sources share [common bias or limitation]."
- "The evidence is correlational, but the conclusion implies causation."
- "Would this conclusion change if we removed the [most favorable data point]?"

## Alternative Thesis Phrases

- "An alternative explanation for the same evidence is [alternative]."
- "The data is equally consistent with [different interpretation]."
- "If we assume [different assumption], the evidence points to [different conclusion]."
- "A competing thesis would be: [thesis]. It explains [what it explains better]."
- "Before accepting this conclusion, consider: what if [opposite] is true?"

## Base Rate Phrases

- "Historically, predictions of this type have been correct [N]% of the time."
- "The base rate for [category of prediction] is [rate]."
- "In comparable situations, the predicted outcome occurred in [N] of [M] cases."
- "The base rate suggests we should be [more/less] confident than the analysis implies."
- "Before accepting this forecast, consider the reference class: [class and its track record]."

## Pre-Mortem Phrases

- "If this thesis is wrong, the most likely reason will be [reason]."
- "A pre-mortem reveals [N] potential failure modes: [list]."
- "The earliest sign that this thesis is failing would be [indicator]."
- "The thesis is most vulnerable to [type of disruption or new information]."

## Falsification Criteria Phrases

- "This thesis would be falsified if [specific observable condition]."
- "We should abandon this conclusion if [evidence or event] materializes."
- "The thesis makes a testable prediction: [prediction]. If wrong, reconsider."
- "Key test: if [X] happens by [date], the thesis is weakened."

## Net Assessment Phrases

- "After stress-testing, the thesis [survives / is weakened / does not hold]."
- "The thesis is robust against [challenge A] but vulnerable to [challenge B]."
- "We raise the following concerns but do not overturn the thesis: [concerns]."
- "On balance, the contrarian analysis [strengthens / weakens] confidence in the original conclusion."

## Quality Check

- Was the thesis steel-manned before being challenged?
- Are challenges evidence-based, not rhetorical?
- Are alternative explanations genuinely plausible?
- Are falsification criteria specific and testable?
- Does the net assessment give a clear verdict?
