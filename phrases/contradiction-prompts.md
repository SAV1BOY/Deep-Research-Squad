# Contradiction Prompts - Language Patterns

## Purpose

These language patterns help agents surface, discuss, and resolve contradictions
found during research. Contradictions are not problems to hide; they are signals
that demand investigation.

## Surfacing Contradictions

### Identifying Conflicting Sources

- "Source A reports [X], while Source B reports [Y]. These claims are in direct conflict."
- "The quantitative data indicates [trend], but the qualitative evidence suggests [opposite]."
- "The expert consensus is [view], yet the empirical data shows [different]."
- "Company statements claim [X], but the financial filings reveal [Y]."

### Identifying Internal Contradictions

- "Within the same report, the author states [X] on page [N] but [Y] on page [M]."
- "The conclusion does not follow from the evidence presented. Specifically, [gap]."
- "The methodology would predict [result], but the reported findings show [different result]."
- "The executive summary contradicts the detailed findings on [specific point]."

## Analyzing Contradictions

### Possible Explanations

- "The contradiction may arise from [different definitions / timeframes / scopes]."
- "Source A and Source B may both be correct if [condition that reconciles them]."
- "The discrepancy is likely due to [methodological difference]: Source A measures [X] while Source B measures [Y]."
- "One possible resolution: [explanation]. However, this requires the assumption that [assumption]."

### Assessing Which Source to Favor

- "On balance, Source A is more reliable here because [reason: methodology / access / independence]."
- "Neither source is clearly superior. We present both views and flag the uncertainty."
- "The more recent data (Source B) should take precedence unless [condition]."
- "We favor the primary source over the secondary account because [reason]."

## Reporting Contradictions

### In Deep Dives

- "A key tension in the evidence is between [View A] and [View B]."
- "We found contradictory evidence on [topic]. The details are in Section [N]."
- "This finding is contested. The strongest case for each position is as follows..."

### In Executive Briefs

- "Note: evidence is mixed on [point]. Our assessment favors [position] because [reason]."
- "Sources disagree on [topic]. We have medium confidence in [our position]."

### In Synthesis Documents

- "The synthesis reveals a structural tension between [domain A findings] and [domain B findings]."
- "These contradictions may indicate [emerging shift / data quality issue / definitional inconsistency]."

## Resolution Framework Phrases

- "To resolve this contradiction, we would need [specific data or investigation]."
- "We attempted to reconcile these sources by [approach]. The result was [outcome]."
- "This contradiction cannot be resolved with available evidence. We flag it as an open question."
- "The contradiction resolves when we control for [variable], suggesting [conclusion]."

## Escalation Language

- "This contradiction is material to the conclusion and should be flagged to [stakeholder]."
- "The conflicting evidence creates sufficient uncertainty to downgrade our confidence to [level]."
- "We recommend additional investigation before acting on this finding."
- "This contradiction was present in the sources and is faithfully reported, not an analytical error."

## Quality Check

- Are all contradictions surfaced, not buried?
- Is each contradiction analyzed with possible explanations?
- Is it clear which source the analysis favors and why?
- Are unresolved contradictions explicitly flagged?
