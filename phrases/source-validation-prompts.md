# Source Validation Prompts - Language Patterns

## Purpose

These language patterns help agents evaluate and communicate the reliability
of sources. Every source must be assessed before its claims are accepted.

## Source Identification Phrases

### Classifying the Source

- "This is a [primary/secondary/tertiary] source."
- "The source is a [peer-reviewed journal / industry report / news article / blog post]."
- "The author is [affiliated with / independent of] the subject being discussed."
- "This source has [direct / indirect / no] access to the underlying data."

### Establishing Provenance

- "The original data comes from [origin]. This source is [N] steps removed."
- "We traced this claim to its primary source: [source]."
- "This claim is widely repeated but we found only one original source: [source]."
- "The source cites [reference], which we have independently verified."

## Reliability Assessment Phrases

### High Reliability

- "This source has a strong track record on this topic."
- "The data is from [government/regulatory/peer-reviewed] sources with established methodology."
- "The findings are corroborated by [N] independent sources."
- "We find no conflicts of interest that would compromise this source."

### Moderate Reliability

- "This source is generally reliable but has limited coverage of [specific area]."
- "The methodology is sound, but the sample size / timeframe is limited."
- "The source is credible but has a [commercial / advocacy] interest in the outcome."
- "We have one corroborating source but would prefer additional confirmation."

### Low Reliability

- "This source has not been independently verified."
- "The claim is made by an interested party without independent corroboration."
- "The methodology is not disclosed, preventing assessment of rigor."
- "This source has a documented history of inaccuracy on related topics."

## Bias Detection Phrases

- "The source has a potential [financial / ideological / institutional] bias because [reason]."
- "This is a vendor-produced report. Findings favorable to the vendor's product should be treated with caution."
- "The source selectively presents data that supports [conclusion], omitting [contrary evidence]."
- "We note that the source's funding comes from [entity], which has an interest in [outcome]."

## Cross-Validation Phrases

- "We cross-referenced this claim with [N] additional sources."
- "Source A and Source B agree on [finding]. Source C contradicts on [point]."
- "The quantitative data from [Source A] is consistent with the qualitative account in [Source B]."
- "No independent source confirms this claim. We classify it as unverified."

## Recency Assessment Phrases

- "This data is from [date]. Market conditions may have changed since then."
- "The source is [N] months/years old. We checked for more recent data and [found/did not find] updates."
- "This is the most recent available data on this topic as of [date]."
- "The findings may be outdated given [recent event or change]."

## Source Rejection Phrases

- "We excluded this source because [reason: bias / methodology / age / unreliability]."
- "This source does not meet our Tier [N] evidence threshold because [reason]."
- "While this source is frequently cited, its underlying methodology is [flawed/undisclosed]."
- "We flag this source as potentially unreliable and do not rely on it for conclusions."

## Quality Check

- Is every source classified by type and tier?
- Are conflicts of interest disclosed?
- Has cross-validation been attempted?
- Are excluded sources documented with reasons?
