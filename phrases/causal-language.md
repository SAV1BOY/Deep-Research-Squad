# Causal Language - Phrase Library

## Purpose

This phrase library provides standardized language for discussing causation,
correlation, and the relationship between events. Imprecise causal language
is one of the most common sources of error in research communication.

## The Causal Ladder

### Level 1: Correlation (Observed Together)

Use when two variables move together but causation is not established.

- "X and Y are correlated (r = [value])."
- "We observe that when X increases, Y tends to increase."
- "There is an association between X and Y."
- "X and Y co-occur, but the causal direction is unclear."
- "The data shows a relationship between X and Y, not necessarily a causal one."

### Level 2: Plausible Mechanism (Could Cause)

Use when there is a logical mechanism but no controlled evidence.

- "A plausible mechanism links X to Y: [mechanism]."
- "X could drive Y through [pathway], though this has not been tested."
- "The theoretical basis for X causing Y is [framework], but empirical confirmation is lacking."
- "If X causes Y, the likely mechanism is [mechanism]."

### Level 3: Evidence of Causation (Likely Causes)

Use when controlled studies or strong quasi-experimental evidence exists.

- "The evidence supports a causal relationship between X and Y."
- "Controlled studies show that changing X produces a change in Y."
- "After controlling for confounders, the effect of X on Y remains significant."
- "The causal link between X and Y is supported by [type of evidence]."

### Level 4: Established Causation (Causes)

Use only when causation is well-established in the literature.

- "X causes Y. This is well-established by [evidence type]."
- "The causal relationship between X and Y is a matter of scientific consensus."
- "Intervening on X reliably produces a change in Y."

## Confounding Language

- "The observed relationship may be confounded by [variable]."
- "A third factor, [Z], may explain both X and Y."
- "After controlling for [confounder], the relationship [persists / weakens / disappears]."
- "We cannot rule out that [alternative cause] drives this relationship."

## Direction of Causation Language

- "The data does not distinguish whether X causes Y or Y causes X."
- "Reverse causation is possible: Y may drive X rather than the other way around."
- "The temporal sequence suggests X precedes Y, consistent with (but not proof of) X causing Y."
- "Bidirectional causation is plausible: X and Y may reinforce each other."

## Intervention Language

- "If we intervene on X, the expected effect on Y is [effect]."
- "Changing X is predicted to [increase / decrease / not affect] Y."
- "An intervention on X would be expected to produce [outcome], assuming [conditions]."
- "The causal model predicts that removing X would [consequence]."

## Common Errors to Avoid

| Error                           | Correction                                    |
|---------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|
| "X leads to Y" (without basis) | "X is associated with Y"                      |
| "X proves Y"                   | "X is consistent with Y"                      |
| "X causes Y" (from correlation)| "X and Y are correlated; causation is not established" |
| "Because X, therefore Y"       | "X preceded Y, suggesting a possible link"    |
| Ignoring confounders            | "Controlling for Z, the relationship..."      |

## Quality Check

- Is every causal claim matched to the appropriate level on the ladder?
- Are confounders identified and discussed?
- Is the direction of causation addressed?
- Are correlation and causation clearly distinguished?
- Would a methodologist agree with the causal language used?
