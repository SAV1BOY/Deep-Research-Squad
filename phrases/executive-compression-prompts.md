# Executive Compression Prompts - Language Patterns

## Purpose

These language patterns help agents compress detailed research into
executive-ready formats. Every phrase is designed to convey maximum
information in minimum words.

## Bottom Line Phrases

### Leading with the Conclusion

- "The core finding is: [one sentence]."
- "In short: [conclusion]."
- "The data supports [action/position]. Here is why."
- "[Topic] is [positive/negative/neutral] for [stakeholder]. Key driver: [driver]."

### Framing the Stakes

- "This matters because [consequence if ignored]."
- "The window for [action] closes in [timeframe]."
- "The cost of inaction is [quantified impact]."
- "This changes the calculus on [decision] because [reason]."

## Evidence Compression Phrases

### Summarizing Supporting Evidence

- "Three data points support this: [1], [2], [3]."
- "The evidence is drawn from [N] sources, primarily [source types]."
- "Key metric: [metric] moved from [A] to [B] over [period]."
- "Comparable cases show [outcome] in [N] of [M] instances."

### Compressing Confidence

- "High confidence: multiple independent sources confirm."
- "Medium confidence: directionally clear, magnitude uncertain."
- "Low confidence: single source, not yet corroborated."
- "Based on [evidence tier]: [finding]."

## Action Compression Phrases

### Stating Recommendations

- "Recommended action: [specific action] by [deadline]."
- "Next step: [owner] to [action] within [timeframe]."
- "Decision required: [yes/no decision] by [date]."
- "Two options: (A) [option] or (B) [option]. We recommend (A) because [reason]."

### Flagging Risks

- "Primary risk: [risk]. Mitigation: [mitigation]."
- "If [condition], the recommendation changes to [alternative]."
- "Watch for: [trigger event] that would invalidate this analysis."
- "Downside scenario: [worst case]. Probability: [estimate]."

## Compression Transition Phrases

These phrases help compress transitions between ideas:
- "Relatedly, [connected finding]."
- "This compounds with [other factor], resulting in [combined effect]."
- "On the other hand, [counterpoint in one sentence]."
- "Net: [net assessment in one sentence]."

## The So-What Append

After every compressed finding, append the executive implication:
- "[Finding] -- so [implication for the business]."
- "[Data point] -- meaning [what this translates to operationally]."
- "[Trend] -- which suggests [strategic consequence]."

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- "After extensive research across multiple domains..." (just state the finding).
- "It is worth noting that..." (if it is worth noting, note it directly).
- "In conclusion..." (the entire brief is the conclusion).
- "As mentioned earlier..." (nothing was mentioned earlier in a brief).
- "There are several factors..." (name the factors or do not mention them).

## Quality Check

- Can the reader act on the first two sentences?
- Is every supporting point the strongest available?
- Are confidence and risk stated, not implied?
- Does every sentence earn its place?
- Is the brief under half a page?
