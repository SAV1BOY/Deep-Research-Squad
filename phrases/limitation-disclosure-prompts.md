# Limitation Disclosure Prompts - Language Patterns

## Purpose

These language patterns help agents disclose the limitations of their
research with precision and honesty. Limitation disclosure builds trust
and prevents stakeholders from over-relying on findings.

## Types of Limitations

### Scope Limitations

- "This analysis is limited to [geography / timeframe / segment]."
- "We did not examine [related topic], which may affect the conclusions."
- "The scope was constrained by [request parameters / available time / resources]."
- "Findings apply to [specific context] and may not generalize to [broader context]."

### Data Limitations

- "The analysis relies on data through [date]. More recent developments are not captured."
- "Primary data was unavailable. Conclusions are based on secondary sources."
- "The dataset has [known limitation: sample size, coverage gaps, measurement issues]."
- "Key data points are self-reported by [entity], which introduces potential bias."

### Methodological Limitations

- "The methodology used does not establish causation, only correlation."
- "Our analytical framework assumes [assumption], which may not hold in all cases."
- "An alternative framework could yield different conclusions on [specific point]."
- "Time constraints prevented [deeper analysis type] that could have strengthened findings."

### Access Limitations

- "We were unable to access [source / database / expert] due to [reason]."
- "Proprietary data that could strengthen this analysis was not available."
- "Classified or restricted information may exist that we could not consult."
- "Expert interviews were limited to [N] participants. A larger sample would improve confidence."

## Impact Assessment Phrases

### Low Impact on Conclusions

- "This limitation is unlikely to change the core finding because [reason]."
- "Despite this limitation, the conclusion is robust due to [compensating evidence]."
- "We note this limitation for completeness, but it does not materially affect the assessment."

### Moderate Impact on Conclusions

- "This limitation introduces uncertainty around [specific aspect], though the direction holds."
- "The finding is directionally reliable but the magnitude is affected by this limitation."
- "Readers should weight this finding with the caveat that [limitation may affect X]."

### High Impact on Conclusions

- "This limitation significantly affects our confidence in [specific finding]."
- "The conclusion rests on [assumption] that we cannot verify due to this limitation."
- "If this limitation were addressed, the finding could [change / be strengthened / be overturned]."

## Remediation Phrases

- "This limitation could be addressed by [specific action or follow-up research]."
- "A future analysis with access to [data / source / tool] would resolve this."
- "We recommend [specific step] to close this gap in a subsequent phase."
- "Monitoring [indicator] would help validate the finding despite this limitation."

## Framing Limitations Constructively

- "We disclose this limitation because [transparency / rigor / enabling better decisions]."
- "Acknowledging this limitation allows stakeholders to [calibrate expectations / plan accordingly]."
- "This limitation defines the boundary of what our analysis can support."
- "The value of the analysis is in [what it does establish], within the boundaries noted above."

## Disclosure Placement Guidelines

| Document Type   | Placement                                        |
|-----------------|--------------------------------------------------|
| Deep dive       | Dedicated section after findings                 |
| Executive brief | One-sentence caveat after recommendation         |
| Synthesis       | Woven into findings + summary section            |
| Presentation    | Dedicated slide before recommendations           |
| Slack update    | Brief note at end: "Caveat: [key limitation]"   |

## Quality Check

- Are all significant limitations disclosed?
- Is the impact of each limitation on the conclusions assessed?
- Are remediation suggestions provided for high-impact limitations?
- Is the disclosure honest without being self-defeating?
- Does the framing maintain confidence in what the analysis does establish?
