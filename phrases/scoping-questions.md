# Scoping Questions - Language Patterns

## Purpose

These language patterns help agents scope research problems precisely.
Good scoping prevents wasted effort, sets clear boundaries, and ensures
the research answers the actual question.

## Problem Definition Phrases

### Clarifying the Core Question

- "The central question we are answering is: [specific question]."
- "This research seeks to determine whether [X] or [Y] is the case."
- "The stakeholder's underlying need is [need], which translates to the research question: [question]."
- "Before proceeding, we need to distinguish between [question A] and [question B], as they require different approaches."

### Decomposing Complex Questions

- "This question breaks down into [N] sub-questions: [list]."
- "To answer the main question, we first need to establish: [prerequisite]."
- "The question has both a factual component ([what is]) and a predictive component ([what will be])."
- "We can decompose this into: (1) what we can observe, (2) what we can infer, and (3) what we must assume."

## Scope Boundary Phrases

### Defining What Is In Scope

- "This analysis covers [domain/timeframe/geography]."
- "We are examining [specific aspect] of [broader topic]."
- "The scope is limited to [boundary] because [reason]."
- "For this research, we define [term] as [definition]."

### Defining What Is Out of Scope

- "Explicitly out of scope: [topic/area]. This may be addressed in follow-up research."
- "We are not assessing [related topic] as it requires [different methodology/data]."
- "The question of [tangential topic] is adjacent but distinct. It is excluded here."
- "Legal/regulatory assessment is outside this scope. Consult [team] for that analysis."

## Feasibility Assessment Phrases

- "Given available sources, we can answer this with [confidence level] confidence."
- "This question is answerable with public data. Proprietary data would improve [aspect]."
- "The timeline permits [depth level] analysis. A deeper analysis would require [time]."
- "We have [strong/limited/no] prior research on this topic to build on."

## Stakeholder Alignment Phrases

- "To confirm: the decision this research informs is [decision]."
- "The output format most useful for this question is [format]."
- "Who is the primary audience, and what is their technical depth?"
- "Is there a deadline that constrains the depth of analysis?"
- "Are there any hypotheses you want us to specifically test or challenge?"

## Assumption Surfacing Phrases

- "This scoping assumes that [assumption]. If this is incorrect, the approach changes."
- "We are proceeding on the assumption that [X]. Please flag if this does not hold."
- "A key assumption underlying this scope is [assumption]. We will validate this early."
- "The scope is sensitive to [variable]. If [variable] changes, we will re-scope."

## Red Flags in Scoping

Watch for these signals that the scope needs refinement:
- The question is really multiple questions disguised as one.
- The stakeholder cannot articulate what decision the research informs.
- The scope is so broad that no meaningful conclusion is possible.
- The expected output format does not match the question's complexity.
- Key terms are undefined or used inconsistently.
