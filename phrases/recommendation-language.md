# Recommendation Language - Phrase Library

## Purpose

This phrase library provides standardized language for making recommendations
based on research findings. Recommendations must be actionable, tied to evidence,
and appropriately qualified by confidence level.

## Recommendation Strength Tiers

### Strong Recommendation (Backed by Confirmed/Probable Evidence)

- "We recommend [action] based on [evidence summary]."
- "The evidence supports [action] as the preferred course."
- "Our recommendation is [action]. The rationale: [key reasons]."
- "Based on our analysis, [action] is the strongest option."
- "We advise [action], with implementation by [deadline]."

### Conditional Recommendation (Backed by Hypothesis-Level Evidence)

- "If [condition] holds, we recommend [action]."
- "Pending confirmation of [finding], the recommended course is [action]."
- "We tentatively recommend [action], subject to [validation step]."
- "The evidence supports [action] if the assumption that [assumption] is correct."
- "We recommend [action] with the caveat that [caveat]."

### No Recommendation (Insufficient Evidence)

- "We do not have sufficient evidence to recommend a course of action."
- "Further research is needed before a recommendation is warranted."
- "We present the options without a recommendation, as the evidence is inconclusive."
- "Given the current uncertainty, we recommend gathering [specific data] before deciding."

## Options Presentation Phrases

### Framing Options

- "We identified [N] viable options: [list]."
- "The key trade-off is between [dimension A] and [dimension B]."
- "Option A optimizes for [goal]; Option B optimizes for [different goal]."
- "The options differ primarily in [cost / speed / risk / coverage]."

### Comparing Options

- "Option A is preferred if [condition]. Option B is preferred if [other condition]."
- "The risk-adjusted comparison favors [option] because [reason]."
- "On a cost-benefit basis, [option] delivers the best return."
- "Option A has higher upside but also higher downside risk than Option B."

## Action Language Phrases

### Specifying Actions

- "The recommended next step is [action], owned by [role], by [date]."
- "Implementation requires [resources] over [timeline]."
- "Phase 1: [action]. Phase 2: [action]. Decision gate after Phase 1."
- "The minimum viable action is [action]. The full recommendation is [broader action]."

### Specifying Decision Points

- "A decision is needed by [date] to [preserve optionality / meet deadline]."
- "The decision point is: [specific yes/no or A/B question]."
- "Delaying this decision past [date] increases [risk] by [amount]."

## Qualifying Recommendations

- "This recommendation is based on evidence as of [date]."
- "If [trigger event] occurs, this recommendation should be revisited."
- "The recommendation assumes [assumption]. If invalid, reconsider [aspect]."
- "Confidence in this recommendation: [level], driven by [evidence quality]."

## Anti-Patterns

- "We suggest maybe considering..." (be direct).
- Recommendations without evidence linkage.
- Options listed without trade-off analysis.
- Actions without owners or deadlines.
- Recommending without stating confidence level.

## Quality Check

- Is the recommendation tied to specific evidence?
- Is the confidence level stated?
- Are alternatives presented with trade-offs?
- Is ownership and timeline specified?
- Are conditions for revisiting the recommendation identified?
