# Evidence Strength Prompts - Language Patterns

## Purpose

These language patterns help agents communicate the strength and quality
of evidence underlying research findings. Precise evidence language prevents
readers from misjudging the reliability of conclusions.

## Evidence Tier Language

### Tier 1: Confirmed Evidence

- "Confirmed by multiple independent primary sources."
- "The evidence is robust: [N] independent sources converge without contradiction."
- "Primary data from [source] is corroborated by [additional sources]."
- "This finding meets our highest evidence standard."

### Tier 2: Strong Indication

- "One primary source is supported by corroborating secondary evidence."
- "Strong indication based on [primary source] plus [N] secondary sources."
- "The evidence is strong, though not fully independent across all sources."
- "We have high-quality evidence from [source type] with supporting data from [source type]."

### Tier 3: Moderate Indication

- "Secondary sources agree, with no primary source contradiction."
- "The evidence is moderate: multiple secondary sources align."
- "Based on [source type] reports, which we have not independently verified."
- "The direction is supported but the evidence base would benefit from primary data."

### Tier 4: Weak Signal

- "This is a weak signal based on [single source / unverified data]."
- "One source reports [finding], but no corroboration is available."
- "Preliminary and unverified: treat with appropriate caution."
- "The signal is plausible but thin. We note it without relying on it."

### Tier 5: Unverified

- "This claim exists but lacks any corroboration."
- "Unverified: the source and methodology are unknown."
- "We include this for completeness but do not base conclusions on it."
- "Treat as rumor until independently confirmed."

## Evidence Quality Descriptors

### Source Quality

- "The source has [established / limited / unknown] credibility on this topic."
- "This is [first-party / third-party / aggregated] data."
- "The source has [no / potential / clear] conflict of interest."
- "The methodology is [disclosed and sound / disclosed but limited / undisclosed]."

### Evidence Freshness

- "Current as of [date]."
- "This data is [N] months old. More recent data may exist."
- "The most recent available; no newer source was found as of [date]."
- "Dated: this evidence predates [relevant event] and may be outdated."

### Evidence Completeness

- "This covers [scope]. It does not address [excluded scope]."
- "The evidence answers the 'what' but not the 'why.'"
- "Complete for [geography / segment / timeframe]. Gaps exist in [area]."
- "The evidence is partial: it covers [covered aspects] but omits [missing aspects]."

## Comparative Evidence Language

- "The evidence for [A] is stronger than the evidence for [B] because [reason]."
- "Both claims have Tier [N] evidence, but [A] has more recent data."
- "The evidence base for this finding is [stronger / weaker / comparable] to [reference]."

## Evidence Absence Language

- "We found no evidence on this point after searching [sources consulted]."
- "The absence of evidence is notable given [why we would expect to find it]."
- "We distinguish: we did not find evidence (not the same as evidence against)."
- "This gap may reflect [genuine absence / access limitations / search limitations]."

## Quality Check

- Is every finding tagged with an evidence tier?
- Are source quality, freshness, and completeness addressed?
- Is absence of evidence distinguished from evidence of absence?
- Are comparative judgments between evidence bases explicit?
