# Synthesis Prompts - Language Patterns

## Purpose

These language patterns help agents write synthesis sections that integrate
multiple research inputs into new understanding. Synthesis goes beyond
summarizing; it connects, compares, and derives implications.

## Convergence Language

### When Sources Agree

- "Across [N] independent sources, the consistent finding is [finding]."
- "Both the quantitative analysis and the qualitative interviews converge on [conclusion]."
- "Market data, expert opinion, and historical precedent all point to [direction]."
- "The evidence from [Domain A] is reinforced by parallel findings in [Domain B]."

### Strengthening Through Convergence

- "The convergence of [N] independent sources increases our confidence to [level]."
- "This finding is robust: it holds across [different methodologies / geographies / timeframes]."
- "Multiple lines of evidence support this conclusion, reducing the likelihood of [alternative]."

## Integration Language

### Connecting Findings

- "Finding A gains significance when viewed alongside Finding B, because together they suggest [synthesis]."
- "Individually, these data points are incremental. Collectively, they signal [pattern]."
- "The combination of [Factor X] and [Factor Y] creates a dynamic that neither predicts alone."
- "When we overlay [data set A] with [data set B], a pattern emerges: [pattern]."

### Deriving Implications

- "If both [Finding A] and [Finding B] hold, the implication is [implication]."
- "This synthesis suggests that [strategic consequence]."
- "For [stakeholder group], this means [specific impact]."
- "The second-order effect of these combined findings is [effect]."

## Divergence Language

### When Sources Disagree

- "The primary tension in the evidence is between [View A] and [View B]."
- "While the market data suggests [X], the regulatory signals point to [Y]."
- "Sources converge on the direction but diverge on timing and magnitude."
- "The disagreement centers on the assumption that [underlying assumption]."

### Weighing Divergent Evidence

- "On balance, the evidence favors [position] because [reason]."
- "Both positions have merit. The determining factor is likely [factor]."
- "We present both scenarios because the evidence does not clearly favor either."

## Pattern Recognition Language

- "A pattern emerges across these findings: [pattern description]."
- "We observe a recurring theme of [theme] across [N] different analyses."
- "The data suggests a shift from [old pattern] to [new pattern]."
- "This pattern is consistent with [known framework or historical precedent]."

## Gap Identification Language

- "The synthesis reveals a gap: no source addresses [specific question]."
- "We can connect [A] to [B] and [B] to [C], but the link between [A] and [C] is unsubstantiated."
- "The evidence supports the what but not the why. Causal mechanisms remain unclear."
- "Further investigation is needed to determine whether [specific unknown]."

## Synthesis Quality Phrases

- "This synthesis integrates [N] sources across [M] domains."
- "The integrated view provides insight that no single source offers alone."
- "We note that the synthesis depends on [key assumption]. If that changes, [consequence]."

## Quality Check

- Does the synthesis create new understanding beyond individual summaries?
- Are connections between findings explicitly drawn?
- Are divergences acknowledged and weighed?
- Are implications clearly derived from the integrated evidence?
- Are gaps honestly identified?
